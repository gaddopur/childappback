## 📘 Key Learnings

This story teaches us how perspective shapes experience and how humour can be found even in frightening situations.

1.  **The Power of Narrative Voice:** The story's humour stems directly from the narrator's tone and his internal monologue, which contrasts sharply with the danger he faces. He recounts his vanity (wanting to look handsome, planning his marriage to a specific type of woman) right before and even *during* the terrifying encounter.
    *   *Example:* His "earth-shaking decisions" about shaving daily and keeping an attractive smile seem absurdly trivial compared to the cobra on his arm.

2.  **Contrast Between Dreams and Reality:** The doctor has grand ambitions despite his humble circumstances.
    *   **Dreams:** Handsome appearance, successful practice, marrying a rich, fat doctor.
    *   **Reality:** Meagre earnings, basic room shared with rats, life-threatening encounter, eventual marriage to a "thin reedy person with the gift of a sprinter," and being robbed of his few possessions.

3.  **The Role of the Mirror:** The mirror is central. Initially, it reflects the doctor's vanity. Later, it becomes the unlikely instrument of his salvation when the snake becomes captivated by its own reflection, allowing the doctor to escape. This adds a layer of irony – vanity saves him.

4.  **Human Vulnerability:** The story highlights how quickly human pride and plans can crumble in the face of primal fear and the indifference of nature. The doctor's medical knowledge is useless; his existence is reduced to hoping for divine intervention or sheer luck.

5.  **Humour as a Literary Tool:** Vaikom Muhammad Basheer masterfully uses humour not to diminish the danger but to highlight the absurdity of the human condition. The final twist of the robbery adds a final, darkly humorous touch.
