## 📘 Key Learnings

1.  **Interpreting Philosophical Texts:**
    *   **Einstein's View:** Industrial development, while advancing civilisation, can hinder individual free development by intensifying the struggle for existence. A planned division of labour is necessary for material security, allowing individuals spare time and energy for personal growth. (Text I)
    *   **Kipling's "If—":** The poem outlines a code of conduct for achieving true manhood, emphasizing balance: maintaining composure under pressure, trusting oneself while considering others' doubts, handling success ("triumph") and failure ("disaster") equally, persevering through loss, staying humble amidst crowds or royalty, and valuing time. (Text III)

2.  **Understanding Historical Correspondence:**
    *   The letters between Einstein and Gandhi reveal mutual respect and shared ideals. Einstein admired Gandhi for demonstrating the possibility of achieving success through non-violence, even against those who use violence. He hoped Gandhi's example would promote international peace. Gandhi expressed consolation that his work was appreciated by Einstein and wished they could meet at his Ashram in India. (Text II)

3.  **Mastering Participle Clauses:**
    *   Participle clauses (using present participles like 'waiting', 'walking', 'having lost') make writing more concise. They are used when the participle and the main verb share the same subject.
    *   **Formation Example:**
        *   *Sentence 1:* He felt sleepy.
        *   *Sentence 2:* He went to bed.
        *   *Combined using Present Participle:* Feeling sleepy, he went to bed.
    *   **Structure:** [Present Participle (-ing form) + (rest of clause)], [Main Clause]

4.  **Vocabulary in Context:** Understanding that words can have multiple meanings depending on the context (e.g., 'world' meaning 'everything important', 'the earth', 'a specific field', 'society/reality'). Also, differentiating commonly confused words like 'elicit'/'illicit' and 'principle'/'principal'.

5.  **Transformation through Compassion:** The story of Angulimala illustrates the transformative power of peace and kindness, as Buddha's calm demeanor and words turned a feared robber into a respected monk. (Listening Text)
