## 📘 Key Learnings 📈

**1. Reading Comprehension ("The Miraculous Escape"):**
*   **Plot Summary:** A wealthy man, bored with his life, goes on a voyage. His ship is caught in a hurricane near a dreaded island. While seeking food, he and his companions encounter a giant serpent. Two companions are swallowed.
*   **Character's Response to Danger:** The narrator initially feels terror-stricken but then uses ingenuity (building a wooden fortress) driven by the instinct for survival.
*   **Resolution:** The narrator escapes the serpent due to his protective structure and is eventually rescued by a passing ship.
*   **Analysis:** The story explores themes of boredom leading to adventure, the power of nature, primal fear, human resilience, and the instinct for self-preservation.

**2. Vocabulary Enrichment:**
*   **Words for Fear:** terrific, terrible, dreaded, startled, rustling, serpent, swallow, running, sad, hissing, alarmed, frightened, terror-stricken, clung, fear, frustration, defensive, waited, dared not, desperate.
*   **Words for Getting Tired:** exhausted, tired, hunger, slept, half dead, suffocated.
*   **Suffixes:**
    *   `-ation`: inform -> information, transform -> transformation
    *   `-ness`: ill -> illness, kind -> kindness
*   **Synonyms/Antonyms:** Practice identifying words with similar (e.g., wealthy/rich, gliding/moved, exhausted/tired, terror-stricken/scared, instinct/urge) and opposite meanings (e.g., poor/wealthy, weak/strength, relieve/enforce, securely/insecurely, defensive/offensive) from the text.
*   **Common Errors:** Understanding distinctions like `far from` vs `far away with`, `it's` vs `its`, `on and off` vs `off and on`, `accept` vs `except`, and avoiding unnecessary commas.

**3. Grammar Application:**
*   **Reported Speech (Questions):**
    *   Rule: When reporting questions, use reporting verbs like 'asked', 'inquired'. Change the question form to statement form. Use 'if' or 'whether' for Yes/No questions. Use the Wh-word itself for Wh-questions. Tenses, pronouns, and time/place expressions change as per standard rules.
    *   *Example:* Direct: He asked, "Who do you think are the most intelligent people?" -> Indirect: He asked the young boy who he thought were the most intelligent people.
*   **Preposition Correction ("The Dog and His Bone"):** Identifying and correcting errors like `searching about` (should be `searching for`), `picked it out` (should be `picked it up`), `ran out into` (should be `ran off into`), `take it over` (should be `take it away`), `trotted around` (should be `trotted onto/across`), `glance into` (correct), `scowled towards` (should be `scowled at`), `at the image on` (should be `in`), `giving that bone` (should be `giving up that bone`), `went over of sight` (should be `went out of sight`), `bite` (should be `bark`).
*   **Sentence Rearrangement:** Practice ordering jumbled words logically and applying correct punctuation (commas, full stops).
    *   *Example:* `when I gave her, the bad news, she turned pale` -> `When I gave her the bad news, she turned pale.`
