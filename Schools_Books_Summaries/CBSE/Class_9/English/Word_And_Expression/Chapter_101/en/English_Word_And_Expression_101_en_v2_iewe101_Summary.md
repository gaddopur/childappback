## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Future Technologies:**

*   **Self-driving Cars:** Vehicles capable of operating without human input. They promise increased safety (communicating with each other), reduced need for parking (estimated 20-30% urban space), less traffic congestion, and environmental benefits (likely electric). Commuting time can be used for other activities.
    *   *Diagram Idea:* Flowchart showing benefits: Self-driving car -> Reduced Human Error -> Fewer Accidents -> Smoother Traffic Flow -> Less Congestion. Another branch: Continuous Use -> Reduced Parking Need -> More Green Space. 📈
*   **Drones:** Unmanned aerial vehicles initially used by the military, now expanding into consumer/commercial uses like infrastructure inspection, disaster surveying, medical supply delivery to remote areas (like in parts of India), and even anti-poaching efforts.
*   **Flying Cars:** Personal aerial vehicles using drone technology (advanced materials, batteries, software) aiming to be more affordable and convenient than current planes/helicopters.
*   **Humanoid Robots & AI:** Robots designed to resemble and interact like humans, powered by Artificial Intelligence. Sophia (created by David Hanson) is a prime example, capable of witty conversation, expressing programmed opinions, and learning. The text explores her capabilities and the potential future of human-robot interaction.

**2. Vocabulary Enhancement:**

*   **Prefixes:** Used to change word meanings, often creating opposites (e.g., `agree` -> `disagree`, `visible` -> `invisible`).
*   **Suffixes:** Used to change a word's grammatical function (e.g., noun to adjective: `danger` -> `dangerous`; verb to noun: `teach` -> `teacher`).
*   **Contextual Understanding:** Identifying word meanings based on their use in passages (e.g., finding synonyms from 'The Fun They Had').

**3. Grammar Application:**

*   **`Unless` and `If...not`:** Both express a condition. `Unless` means 'except if'.
    *   Example: "You can't go on a holiday **unless** you save some money." = "**If** you do **not** save some money, you can't go on a holiday."
*   **Adverb Placement:** Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Their position can vary but often follows the verb or object.
    *   Example: "The man thanked me **profusely** before he drove off." (Modifies 'thanked')
    *   Example: "**Fortunately**, the police had several photographs of the suspect." (Modifies the entire sentence)

**4. Punctuation and Editing:**

*   Correct usage of capital letters (start of sentences, proper nouns), full stops (end of declarative sentences), commas (lists, clauses, introductory phrases), and inverted commas (direct speech, quotes) is crucial for clarity.
