**3. Enhancing Vocabulary:**

*   **Prefixes for Opposites:** `un-` (use -> **un**use, predictable -> **un**predictable), `dis-` (agree -> **dis**agree, connect -> **dis**connect, like -> **dis**like), `mis-` (understand -> **mis**understand, spell -> **mis**spell), `in-` (visible -> **in**visible).
*   **Suffixes for Adjectives:** `-able` (comfort -> comfort**able**, read -> read**able**, enjoy -> enjoy**able**, suit -> suit**able**), `-y` (sun -> sunn**y**), `-ful` (beauty -> beauti**ful**, help -> help**ful**, use -> use**ful**), `-ous` (hazard -> hazard**ous**), `-ive` (impress -> impress**ive**).

**4. Mastering Grammar Concepts:**

*   **'Unless' vs. 'If...not':** Both express a condition. 'Unless' means 'except if'.
    *   *Example:* "You can’t go on a holiday **unless** you save some money." is equivalent to "**If** you do **not** save some money, you can’t go on a holiday."
*   **Adverb Placement:** Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Their placement can vary but often occurs before the main verb, after the auxiliary verb, or at the beginning/end of a sentence depending on the adverb type and emphasis.
    *   *Example:* "Mr. Sharma died **suddenly**..." (modifying 'died') vs. "**Fortunately**, the police had several photographs..." (modifying the whole sentence).
*   **Punctuation:** Essential for clarity. Includes:
    *   **Capital Letters:** Start of sentences, proper nouns.
    *   **Full Stops (.)**: End of declarative sentences.
    *   **Commas (,):** Separating items in a list, clauses, introductory phrases, direct address.
    *   **Inverted Commas (" "):** Direct speech/quotations.
