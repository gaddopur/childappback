# [Class 9] Moments Supplementry - Chapter 2: The Adventures of Toto

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter revolves around the humorous and troublesome experiences of a family who adopts a baby monkey named Toto.

1.  **Characterisation:**
    *   **Toto:** A "pretty" but extremely mischievous baby monkey. His actions drive the plot.
        *   Physical Traits: Bright mischievous eyes, pearly white teeth, dried-up hands, long tail (used as a third hand).
        *   Behavioural Traits: Destructive, clever in causing trouble, imitative, sensitive (hurt feelings during bath), enjoys warmth.
    *   **Grandfather:** An animal lover who maintains a 'private zoo'. Initially amused by Toto's cleverness, but eventually pragmatic about the trouble Toto causes.
    *   **Grandmother:** Dislikes Grandfather bringing new pets; represents the practical challenges of keeping disruptive animals. Eventually accepts Toto, but faces his worst mischief.
    *   **Narrator (Ruskin Bond as a child):** An observer and participant in Toto's adventures.

2.  **Theme:**
    *   **Human-Animal Interaction:** Explores the complexities and challenges of keeping wild animals as pets.
    *   **Mischief and Trouble:** Focuses on the humorous yet problematic consequences of Toto's inherently mischievous nature.
    *   **Acceptance and Limits:** Shows the family's initial attempts to accept Toto, contrasted with the eventual realization that he is unsuitable as a pet for them.
    *   **Observation and Learning:** Toto learns behaviours (like bathing) through observation.

3.  **Plot Development:**
    *   Introduction: Toto's acquisition and initial concealment.
    *   Rising Action: Series of mischievous incidents (wallpaper, blazer, train journey, stable incident, bath ritual, near-boiling incident, pullao incident).
    *   Climax: The pullao incident, where Toto eats the family's lunch, throws things, and breaks the dish.
    *   Falling Action: Grandfather's realization that Toto is too much trouble and expense.
    *   Resolution: Toto is sold back to the tonga-driver.

4.  **Humour:** The narrative uses humour derived from Toto's antics and the family's reactions.

## 📘 Key Learnings

*   **Acquisition and Initial Trouble:** Grandfather buys Toto for five rupees, attracted by his mischievous look but feeling he was out of place with the tonga-driver. Toto's presence is kept secret from Grandmother. His first act is destroying wallpaper and shredding the narrator's blazer, which Grandfather amusingly interprets as cleverness.

*   **The Saharanpur Journey:** Toto is secretly taken on a train journey in a canvas bag. He startles the ticket collector by poking his head out. Despite Grandfather's protests, the ticket collector classifies Toto as a dog and charges a fare of three rupees. This incident highlights Toto's unpredictability and the trouble he causes even outside the home.

*   **Life with Other Pets:** When moved to the stable, Toto torments the family donkey, Nana, by biting her ears, showing he cannot coexist peacefully with other animals.

*   **The Bathing Ritual:** Toto learns to bathe by imitating the narrator. He enjoys warm water, tests the temperature, and uses soap. This demonstrates his capacity for learning through observation. However, this leads to a dangerous incident where he climbs into a large kettle of water left on the fire to boil, nearly boiling himself alive before being rescued by Grandmother.

*   **Escalating Mischief and Departure:** Toto's destructiveness increases – tearing clothes, breaking dishes (the pullao incident). The family, not being wealthy ("We were not well-to-do"), cannot sustain the constant losses. Grandfather pragmatically decides they cannot keep Toto. He finds the original tonga-driver and sells Toto back for three rupees, accepting a financial loss to regain peace.

*   **Unsuitability as a Pet:** The story concludes that while amusing, Toto's wild and mischievous nature made him unsuitable as a domestic pet for the family, highlighting that not all animals adapt well to human households.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Setting and Cultural References:** The story is firmly set in India, likely in the early to mid-20th century, given the reference to Anglo-Indian ladies and tongas.
    *   **Locations:** Dehra Dun and Saharanpur are real cities in North India.
    *   **Transport:** The 'tonga' (horse-drawn carriage) was a common mode of transport.
    *   **Social Elements:** The mention of 'Anglo-Indian ladies' provides a specific social context. Grandfather's 'private zoo' reflects a hobby sometimes pursued by affluent individuals or animal enthusiasts in that era.
    *   **Flora/Fauna:** The 'jackfruit tree' is native to India. Monkeys are common and often interact (sometimes problematically) with human settlements in India.
    *   **Food:** 'Pullao' (pilaf) is a popular rice dish in India.

*   **Socio-Economic Undertones:**
    *   **Cost of Living/Pets:** The story subtly touches upon economics. Toto is bought for five rupees and sold for three, indicating a monetary loss. The narrator explicitly states, "We were not well-to-do, and could not afford the frequent loss of dishes, clothes, curtains and wallpaper." This highlights that keeping even a small, inexpensive animal can become burdensome if it causes damage, reflecting realistic economic constraints faced by many families.
    *   **Human-Animal Relationship in India:** While the story is fictional, it reflects the complex relationship between humans and animals in India. Monkeys, in particular, are viewed with a mix of affection, amusement, tolerance (due to religious associations in some contexts), and frustration (due to their mischievous nature and potential for causing damage). Grandfather's fondness for animals represents a common sentiment, but the eventual need to give Toto away underscores the practical difficulties.

*   **Ruskin Bond's Work:** Ruskin Bond is an Indian author of British descent, known for his stories set in the Himalayan foothills. His works often feature gentle humour, keen observation of nature and human character, and a strong sense of place, all evident in "The Adventures of Toto." The story reflects a nostalgic view of a simpler time while realistically portraying the challenges of living with a wild animal.
