## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter introduces the fundamental concepts related to accessing and utilising the Internet.

1.  **The Internet:**
    *   A global network connecting computers and devices.
    *   Used for communication (email, messaging), information retrieval, sharing resources (videos, images, blogs), online learning, etc.
    *   Requires an electronic device (computer, smartphone, tablet) and an Internet connection.
2.  **World Wide Web (WWW):**
    *   An information space on the Internet.
    *   Contains documents and multimedia resources stored as webpages.
3.  **Web Browser:**
    *   Software used to access information on the WWW.
    *   Examples: Mozilla Firefox, Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Safari.
    *   Key feature: Address Bar for typing URLs.
4.  **Websites and Webpages:**
    *   **Webpage:** A single document on the WWW, containing multimedia information.
    *   **Website:** A collection of related webpages.
    *   **Home Page:** The main or introductory page of a website.
    *   **URL (Uniform Resource Locator):** The unique address of a resource (like a webpage) on the web.
    *   **Hyperlink:** Text or image that links to another webpage or resource.
5.  **Search Engines:**
    *   Tools for finding information on the WWW.
    *   Examples: Google, Bing, Yahoo, Ask, DuckDuckGo.
    *   Use **Keywords** entered in a **Search Box** to find relevant webpages.
6.  **Effective Searching:**
    *   **Keyword Selection:** Choosing precise words relevant to the search query. Avoiding common words.
    *   **Filtering Results:** Categorising search results (All, Images, Videos, etc.).
    *   **Search Operators:** Refining searches using operators like `AND`, `OR`, `""` (exact phrase), `...` (number range).
7.  **Web Resources & Tools:**
    *   **Plug-in:** Software adding functionality to a browser (e.g., to display specific content).
    *   **Bookmarking:** Saving a webpage's URL for quick future access.
    *   **Citing Sources:** Giving credit to the original creators of web resources.
8.  **Evaluating Online Information:**
    *   Critical assessment of web resources for accuracy, reliability, ownership, and purpose.
    *   Importance of cross-checking information from multiple authentic sources.
9.  **Safe Internet Practices:**
    *   Recognising potential risks in the **Cyber World**.
    *   Checking for **HTTPS** in URLs for secure connections.
    *   Using **Safe Search** settings in browsers.
    *   Being cautious before clicking on links.
    *   Choosing a secure Web browser.
10. **Electronic Mail (Email):**
    *   A method for sending and receiving messages electronically over the Internet.
    *   Requires an **Email Account** with a service provider (e.g., Gmail, Yahoo Mail, Outlook).
    *   **Email Address Format:** `username@email-service-provider` (e.g., `shiksha@gmail.com`).
    *   **Creating an Account:** Involves choosing a unique username, setting a strong password, providing personal details, and agreeing to Terms & Conditions.
    *   **Strong Password:** Combination of uppercase/lowercase letters, numbers, symbols; minimum length; unique per account; changed frequently.
11. **Using Email:**
    *   **Composing:** Writing and sending emails (Fields: To, Subject, Cc, Bcc; Body; Attachments).
    *   **Inbox:** Folder where received emails are stored.
    *   **Attachments:** Files (documents, images, etc.) sent along with an email (**Uploading**).
    *   **Downloading:** Transferring files from the Internet (e.g., email attachments) to your computer.
12. **Email Security:**
    *   **Virus:** Harmful program affecting computer performance and data.
    *   **Anti-virus Software:** Programs to detect and remove viruses (e.g., Norton, Kaspersky, McAfee).
    *   **Safe Downloading:** Checking sender trust, file format, and scanning attachments with anti-virus before downloading.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Accessing the Internet and WWW:**
The Internet has revolutionized how we access information, moving beyond traditional libraries. To connect, you need a device and an internet connection. The World Wide Web (WWW) is the primary space where information resides. A **Web Browser** (like Firefox, Chrome) acts as your gateway to the WWW. You navigate by typing a web address (**URL**) into the **Address Bar**.

*Diagrammatic Concept:* Imagine the Internet as a vast city, the WWW as its library district, Websites as individual libraries, Webpages as books/sections within libraries, and the Web Browser as your vehicle to travel between them using URLs as addresses.

**2. Navigating Websites and Using Search Engines:**
A **Website** is made up of **Webpages**, with the **Home Page** being the starting point. **Hyperlinks** allow you to jump between pages or resources. When you don't know the specific URL, a **Search Engine** (like Google or DuckDuckGo) helps you find information using **Keywords**.

*Diagrammatic Concept:* A typical browser window (like Fig 6.2) shows the address bar at the top. A search engine page (like Fig 6.3) features a prominent search box for keywords. Search results pages often have filters (like Fig 6.4 - All, Images, Videos).

**3. Refining Your Search:**
Effective searching involves planning (what information type/format you need) and using precise **Keywords**. **Search Operators** (`AND`, `OR`, `""`, `...`) help narrow down results significantly (Table 6.2). For example, searching `"Role of youth in nation building"` yields more specific results than `youth nation building`.

**4. Managing and Using Online Resources:**
Sometimes, viewing specific content requires installing a **Plug-in**. To easily revisit useful pages, you can **Bookmark** them (Fig 6.6). Crucially, always **cite** the source when using information from the web to give credit to the creator.

**5. Evaluating Information Critically:**
The internet allows anyone to publish content, so not all information is accurate or reliable. You must evaluate resources based on:
*   **Authority:** Who created the website? Can they be contacted?
*   **Purpose:** Is it commercial, educational, personal? Who is the intended audience?
*   **Reliability:** Is the information verifiable? Cross-check with other sources (books, experts).
*   **Cost:** Is the information free or paid?

**6. Practicing Safe Browsing:**
Safety online is paramount. Key practices include:
*   Look for **HTTPS** in the URL for secure sites.
*   Enable **Safe Search** in your browser settings.
*   Be cautious about clicking unknown links.
*   Use a reputable and updated web browser.

**7. Understanding and Using Email:**
**Email** is a vital communication tool. You need an account from a provider (Gmail, Yahoo, etc.). Creating one involves choosing a username and a **strong password**.
*   **Composing Email (Fig 6.8):** Requires recipient(s) address (To, Cc, Bcc), a Subject line, the message body, and optional attachments.
*   **Receiving Email:** Emails arrive in your **Inbox** (Fig 6.10).
*   **Attachments (Fig 6.9):** Be extremely cautious when **downloading** attachments (Fig 6.11). Always verify the sender and scan for **viruses** using **Anti-virus** software before opening.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

The skills learned in this chapter are directly applicable to accessing valuable resources relevant to India:

1.  **Government & Citizen Services:** Websites like `mygov.in` (citizen engagement platform) and `ors.gov.in` (Online Registration System for hospitals) provide essential services and information. Evaluating the authenticity of such government portals is crucial.
2.  **Educational Resources:** Platforms like `swayam.gov.in` (online courses) and `ndl.iitkgp.ac.in` (National Digital Library of India) offer vast learning materials. Students can use search skills to find specific syllabi (e.g., from `ncert.nic.in` - Exercise 2 & 5) or research papers.
3.  **Economic & Social Data:** Information related to specific sectors like agriculture (`farmer.gov.in`) or national initiatives like the Green Revolution (involving figures like M.S. Swaminathan) can be researched online. Effective keyword searching helps locate specific data.
4.  **Travel & Tourism:** Planning travel within India often involves using websites like the Indian Railways portal or state tourism websites (e.g., Delhi Tourism - Exercise 2). Search skills help find schedules, booking options, and information.

Understanding how to effectively search, evaluate information, communicate via email, and practice online safety is essential for leveraging these digital resources for personal, educational, and civic engagement in the Indian context.
