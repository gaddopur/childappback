# [Class 9] Maths - Chapter 9: Circles

## 🌟 Core Concepts

**1. Circle Basics:**
    *   **Definition:** A circle is the collection of all points in a plane equidistant from a fixed point (the **centre**). The fixed distance is the **radius**.
    *   **Parts:** Chord, Diameter (longest chord passing through the centre), Arc (minor/major), Segment (minor/major), Sector (minor/major).
    *   **Congruent Circles:** Circles with the same radii.

**2. Chords and Angles Subtended:**
    *   **Angle Subtended:** An angle formed by a line segment/chord/arc at a point (e.g., at the centre or on the circumference).
    *   **Theorem 9.1:** Equal chords of a circle (or congruent circles) subtend equal angles at the centre(s).
    *   **Theorem 9.2 (Converse of 9.1):** If the angles subtended by chords of a circle (or congruent circles) at the centre(s) are equal, then the chords are equal.

**3. Perpendicular from Centre to Chord:**
    *   **Theorem 9.3:** The perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
    *   **Theorem 9.4 (Converse of 9.3):** The line drawn through the centre of a circle to bisect a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

**4. Equal Chords and Distance from Centre:**
    *   **Distance of a point from a line:** The length of the perpendicular from the point to the line.
    *   **Theorem 9.5:** Equal chords of a circle (or congruent circles) are equidistant from the centre(s).
    *   **Theorem 9.6 (Converse of 9.5):** Chords equidistant from the centre of a circle are equal in length.

**5. Arcs and Angles Subtended:**
    *   **Congruent Arcs:** Arcs that superimpose completely. Equal chords correspond to congruent arcs and vice-versa.
    *   **Angle Subtended by Arc:** The angle subtended by the corresponding chord at the centre (minor arc subtends the angle, major arc subtends the reflex angle). Congruent arcs subtend equal angles at the centre.
    *   **Theorem 9.7:** The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.
    *   **Theorem 9.8:** Angles in the same segment of a circle are equal.
    *   **Property:** Angle in a semicircle is a right angle (90°).
    *   **Theorem 9.9 (Converse of 9.8):** If a line segment joining two points subtends equal angles at two other points lying on the same side of the line containing the segment, the four points lie on a circle (are **concyclic**).

**6. Cyclic Quadrilaterals:**
    *   **Definition:** A quadrilateral whose all four vertices lie on a circle.
    *   **Theorem 9.10:** The sum of either pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is 180°.
    *   **Theorem 9.11 (Converse of 9.10):** If the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is 180°, the quadrilateral is cyclic.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**Understanding Chord Properties:**

*   **Equality and Angles:** Equal chords always create equal angles at the centre. Conversely, if chords create equal angles at the centre, they must be equal. This is proven using SSS congruence (Theorem 9.1) and SAS congruence (Theorem 9.2).
    *   *Diagrammatic Representation:* Imagine two equal chords AB and CD in a circle with centre O. Triangles AOB and COD are congruent (OA=OC, OB=OD radii; AB=CD given). Hence ∠AOB = ∠COD (CPCT).
*   **Perpendicular Bisector:** The line segment from the centre perpendicular to a chord always cuts the chord into two equal halves (bisects it). Conversely, a line from the centre that bisects a chord is always perpendicular to it. This is proven using RHS or SSS congruence (Theorems 9.3 & 9.4).
    *   *Diagrammatic Representation:* Chord AB, centre O, OM ⊥ AB. Triangles OAM and OBM are congruent (OA=OB radii, OM common, ∠OMA = ∠OMB = 90°). Hence AM = BM (CPCT).
*   **Equality and Distance:** Equal chords are always the same distance (perpendicular distance) from the centre. Conversely, chords that are equidistant from the centre are equal in length (Theorems 9.5 & 9.6).
    *   *Diagrammatic Representation:* Equal chords AB and CD, perpendiculars OM and ON from centre O. Proving ∆OLE ≅ ∆OME in Example 1 uses angle properties and AAS congruence to show OL=OM, implying AB=CD.

**Understanding Arc and Angle Relationships:**

*   **Centre vs. Circumference:** The angle an arc makes at the centre is *always* twice the angle it makes at any point on the remaining circumference (Theorem 9.7). This holds true for minor arcs, major arcs (reflex angle at centre), and semicircles.
    *   *Diagrammatic Representation (Minor Arc PQ):* Centre O, point A on major arc. Join AO and extend. Use exterior angle property of triangles OAQ and OAP (where OA=OQ and OA=OP are radii) to show ∠POQ = 2∠PAQ.
*   **Angles in the Same Segment:** All angles subtended by the same arc (or chord) in the same segment are equal (Theorem 9.8). This follows directly from Theorem 9.7.
    *   *Diagrammatic Representation:* Arc PQ subtends ∠PAQ and ∠PCQ at points A and C on the remaining part. Since both ∠PAQ and ∠PCQ are half of ∠POQ, they must be equal.
*   **Angle in a Semicircle:** A direct consequence of Theorem 9.7. The angle subtended by the diameter (a semicircle arc) at the centre is 180°. Therefore, the angle subtended at any point on the circumference is half of 180°, which is 90°.

**Understanding Cyclic Quadrilaterals:**

*   **Opposite Angles:** In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles always add up to 180° (supplementary). (Theorem 9.10).
*   **Converse Property:** If the opposite angles of a quadrilateral are supplementary, then it must be a cyclic quadrilateral (Theorem 9.11). This is useful for proving that four points lie on a circle.
    *   *Example:* If in quadrilateral ABCD, ∠A + ∠C = 180°, then A, B, C, D are concyclic.
