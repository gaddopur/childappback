# [Class 9] General - Chapter 9: Circles

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter delves into the properties of circles, chords, arcs, and the angles they subtend. Key concepts are organized hierarchically:

1.  **Fundamentals of Circles**
    *   Definition: Collection of points equidistant from a fixed point (centre).
    *   Parts: Radius, Diameter, Chord, Arc (Minor, Major, Semicircle), Segment (Minor, Major), Centre.
    *   Congruent Circles: Circles with equal radii.

2.  **Chords and Angles at the Centre**
    *   Angle Subtended by a Chord/Arc: The angle formed by joining the endpoints of the chord/arc to a point (centre or on the circle).
    *   **Theorem 9.1:** Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.
    *   **Theorem 9.2 (Converse of 9.1):** If angles subtended by chords at the centre are equal, the chords are equal.

3.  **Perpendicular from Centre to Chord**
    *   **Theorem 9.3:** The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
    *   **Theorem 9.4 (Converse of 9.3):** The line drawn through the centre of a circle to bisect a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

4.  **Chord Properties Related to Distance from Centre**
    *   Distance of a point from a line: The length of the perpendicular from the point to the line.
    *   **Theorem 9.5:** Equal chords of a circle (or congruent circles) are equidistant from the centre (or centres).
    *   **Theorem 9.6 (Converse of 9.5):** Chords equidistant from the centre of a circle are equal in length.

5.  **Arcs and Angles Subtended**
    *   Congruent Arcs: Arcs that superimpose completely. Equal chords correspond to congruent arcs and vice-versa.
    *   Angle Subtended by an Arc at Centre: Defined by the corresponding chord; minor arc subtends the central angle, major arc subtends the reflex angle. Congruent arcs subtend equal angles at the centre.
    *   **Theorem 9.7:** The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle. 📈
    *   **Theorem 9.8:** Angles in the same segment of a circle are equal.
    *   Property: Angle in a semicircle is a right angle (90°).

6.  **Concyclic Points and Cyclic Quadrilaterals**
    *   Concyclic Points: Points lying on the same circle.
    *   **Theorem 9.9:** If a line segment joining two points subtends equal angles at two other points lying on the same side of the line, the four points are concyclic.
    *   Cyclic Quadrilateral: A quadrilateral whose vertices all lie on a circle. 📊
    *   **Theorem 9.10:** The sum of either pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is 180°.
    *   **Theorem 9.11 (Converse of 9.10):** If the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is 180°, the quadrilateral is cyclic.

## 📘 Key Learnings

This section elaborates on the fundamental theorems and their proofs as presented in the NCERT text.

1.  **Equality of Chords and Angles at Centre (Theorems 9.1 & 9.2):**
    *   **Theorem 9.1:** If two chords (say AB and CD) in a circle with centre O are equal, then the angles they subtend at the centre (∠AOB and ∠COD) are equal. This is proven using the SSS (Side-Side-Side) congruence rule on ∆AOB and ∆COD (OA=OC, OB=OD being radii, and AB=CD given). By CPCT (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles), ∠AOB = ∠COD.
    *   **Theorem 9.2:** Conversely, if ∠AOB = ∠COD, then chords AB = CD. This is proven using SAS (Side-Angle-Side) congruence (OA=OC, OB=OD, ∠AOB = ∠COD given), leading to AB=CD by CPCT.

2.  **Perpendicular Bisector Property (Theorems 9.3 & 9.4):**
    *   **Theorem 9.3:** A perpendicular drawn from the centre O to a chord AB (say at point M) bisects the chord (AM = MB). This is proven by considering right triangles ∆OAM and ∆OBM. Using RHS (Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side) congruence (∠OMA = ∠OMB = 90°, OA=OB radii, OM=OM common), we get AM=MB by CPCT. Alternatively, SSS can be used if M is initially defined as the midpoint.
    *   **Theorem 9.4:** A line joining the centre O to the midpoint M of a chord AB is perpendicular to the chord (OM ⊥ AB). Proven using SSS congruence on ∆OAM and ∆OBM (OA=OB radii, AM=BM given, OM=OM common), which gives ∠OMA = ∠OMB by CPCT. Since these angles form a linear pair (∠OMA + ∠OMB = 180°), each must be 90°.

3.  **Equidistant Chords (Theorems 9.5 & 9.6):**
    *   **Theorem 9.5:** Equal chords (AB=CD) are equidistant from the centre O. This means the perpendicular distances (say OM ⊥ AB, ON ⊥ CD) are equal (OM=ON). The proof involves showing congruence between triangles formed using radii and half-chords (e.g., ∆OAM ≅ ∆OCN using RHS, since AM = CN as halves of equal chords).
    *   **Theorem 9.6:** Chords equidistant from the centre (OM=ON) are equal in length (AB=CD). Proven similarly, using congruence (e.g., RHS on ∆OAM and ∆OCN) to show AM=CN, and thus AB=2AM = 2CN = CD.
    *   *Evaluation:* Note how the distance is defined as the *least* distance, which is the perpendicular distance. Longer chords are closer to the centre, with the diameter being the longest chord at zero distance.

4.  **Angle Subtended by Arcs (Theorems 9.7 & 9.8):**
    *   **Theorem 9.7:** Angle at Centre = 2 × Angle at Circumference. For an arc PQ, ∠POQ = 2∠PAQ, where A is any point on the remaining part of the circle. The proof involves joining AO and extending it, then using the exterior angle theorem on isosceles triangles (like ∆OAQ where OA=OQ). This holds for minor arcs, major arcs (reflex angle at centre), and semicircles. 📈
    *   **Theorem 9.8:** Angles subtended by the same arc (or in the same segment) at different points on the circumference are equal (e.g., ∠PAQ = ∠PCQ). This is a direct consequence of Theorem 9.7, as both angles are half the angle subtended by the same arc at the centre.
    *   **Angle in a Semicircle:** A special case of Theorem 9.7. The angle subtended by a diameter (semicircular arc) at the centre is 180°. Therefore, the angle subtended by it at any point on the circumference is 1/2 × 180° = 90°.

5.  **Cyclic Properties (Theorems 9.9, 9.10 & 9.11):**
    *   **Theorem 9.9:** Provides a condition for four points to be concyclic. If A, B, C, D are points such that C and D are on the same side of line AB, and ∠ACB = ∠ADB, then A, B, C, D lie on a circle. The proof uses contradiction, assuming D is not on the circle passing through A, C, B.
    *   **Theorem 9.10:** In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, opposite angles sum to 180° (∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180°). This can be proven using Theorem 9.7 by considering the angles subtended by arcs at the centre and circumference.
    *   **Theorem 9.11:** If a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral sum to 180°, it is cyclic. This converse theorem is crucial for proving quadrilaterals are cyclic.
