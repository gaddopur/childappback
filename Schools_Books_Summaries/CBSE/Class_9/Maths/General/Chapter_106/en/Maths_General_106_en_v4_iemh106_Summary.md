## 📘 Key Learnings

This section details the fundamental axioms and theorems governing lines and angles.

1.  **Basic Definitions Review:**
    *   **Line Segment:** Part of a line with two endpoints.
    *   **Ray:** Part of a line with one endpoint, extending indefinitely in one direction.
    *   **Angle:** Formed by two rays originating from the same endpoint (vertex).
    *   **Types of Angles:** Acute, Right, Obtuse, Straight, Reflex (as defined above).
    *   **Complementary/Supplementary:** Pairs of angles adding up to 90°/180° respectively.
    *   **Adjacent Angles:** Share a common vertex and common arm, non-common arms lie on opposite sides.
        *   `[Diagram showing two adjacent angles ABD and DBC with common vertex B and common arm BD]`
    *   **Linear Pair:** Adjacent angles whose non-common arms form a straight line. Their sum is always 180°.
        *   `[Diagram showing angles ABD and DBC forming a linear pair along line AC]`
    *   **Vertically Opposite Angles:** Formed when two lines intersect. They are equal.
        *   `[Diagram showing lines AB and CD intersecting at O, forming vertically opposite pairs (AOC & BOD) and (AOD & BOC)]`

2.  **Linear Pair Axiom:**
    *   **Axiom 6.1:** If a ray stands on a line, then the sum of the two adjacent angles so formed is 180°.
        *   `[Diagram showing ray OC standing on line AB at point O. Angle AOC + Angle BOC = 180°]`
    *   **Axiom 6.2 (Converse of Axiom 6.1):** If the sum of two adjacent angles is 180°, then the non-common arms of the angles form a line.
    *   These two axioms together establish the property of a linear pair.

3.  **Vertically Opposite Angles Theorem:**
    *   **Theorem 6.1:** If two lines intersect each other, then the vertically opposite angles are equal.
    *   **Proof Idea:** Uses the Linear Pair Axiom. If lines AB and CD intersect at O, then ∠AOC + ∠AOD = 180° (Linear Pair) and ∠AOD + ∠BOD = 180° (Linear Pair). Equating these gives ∠AOC + ∠AOD = ∠AOD + ∠BOD, which implies ∠AOC = ∠BOD. Similarly, ∠AOD = ∠BOC.
        *   `[Diagram showing intersecting lines AB and CD at O, highlighting the pairs of equal vertically opposite angles]`

4.  **Parallel Lines and Transversals:**
    *   When a transversal intersects two parallel lines:
        *   Pairs of **Corresponding Angles** are equal. (Axiom)
            *   `[Diagram showing parallel lines l and m intersected by transversal t, highlighting a pair of corresponding angles]`
        *   Pairs of **Alternate Interior Angles** are equal. (Theorem)
            *   `[Diagram showing parallel lines l and m intersected by transversal t, highlighting a pair of alternate interior angles]`
        *   Pairs of **Interior Angles on the Same Side of the Transversal** are supplementary (sum is 180°). (Theorem)
            *   `[Diagram showing parallel lines l and m intersected by transversal t, highlighting a pair of interior angles on the same side]`
    *   **Converses:** If any of the above conditions (equal corresponding angles, equal alternate interior angles, or supplementary consecutive interior angles) are met, then the two lines are parallel. (Converse Axiom/Theorems)

5.  **Lines Parallel to the Same Line:**
    *   **Theorem 6.6:** Lines which are parallel to the same line are parallel to each other.
    *   **Proof Idea:** If line *m* || line *l* and line *n* || line *l*, draw a transversal *t*. Using the Corresponding Angles Axiom, angles formed by *t* with *m* and *n* are equal to the angle formed with *l*, implying they are equal to each other. By the Converse of the Corresponding Angles Axiom, *m* || *n*.
        *   `[Diagram showing three lines l, m, n where m || l and n || l, intersected by a transversal t]`
