# [Class 9] Maths - Chapter 7: Triangles

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter delves into the fundamental geometric concept of **Congruence** as applied to **Triangles**.

1.  **Triangle Basics:**
    *   Definition: A closed figure formed by three intersecting lines.
    *   Components: 3 Sides, 3 Angles, 3 Vertices.
    *   Notation: ∆ ABC denotes a triangle with vertices A, B, C.

2.  **Congruence:**
    *   Definition: Figures having the same shape and the same size. ('Congruent' means equal in all respects).
    *   Examples: Two identical photographs, two bangles of the same size, two ATM cards from the same bank, two squares of the same side length, two circles of the same radius.
    *   Application: Essential in mass production (e.g., ice cube moulds, pen refills) where identical objects are required.

3.  **Congruence of Triangles:**
    *   Definition: Two triangles are congruent if their corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal.
    *   Notation: ∆ PQR ≅ ∆ ABC implies a specific one-to-one correspondence between vertices (P ↔ A, Q ↔ B, R ↔ C).
    *   **CPCT:** Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are equal. This is a crucial consequence used after proving congruence.

4.  **Criteria for Congruence (Rules):**
    *   Conditions under which two triangles can be proven congruent without checking all 6 corresponding parts (3 sides, 3 angles).
    *   **SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Congruence Rule (Axiom 7.1):** Two triangles are congruent if two sides and the *included* angle of one triangle are equal to two sides and the *included* angle of the other triangle. (Note: ASS or SSA is *not* a valid congruence rule).
    *   **ASA (Angle-Side-Angle) Congruence Rule (Theorem 7.1):** Two triangles are congruent if two angles and the *included* side of one triangle are equal to two angles and the *included* side of the other triangle.
    *   **AAS (Angle-Angle-Side) Congruence Rule:** Two triangles are congruent if any two pairs of angles and one pair of corresponding sides are equal. (Derived from ASA, as the third angle is also equal). (Note: AAA is *not* sufficient for congruence).
    *   **SSS (Side-Side-Side) Congruence Rule (Theorem 7.4):** Two triangles are congruent if three sides of one triangle are equal to the three sides of the other triangle.
    *   **RHS (Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side) Congruence Rule (Theorem 7.5):** Two *right-angled* triangles are congruent if the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one corresponding side of the other triangle.

5.  **Properties of Isosceles Triangles:**
    *   Definition: A triangle with two equal sides.
    *   **Theorem 7.2:** Angles opposite to equal sides of an isosceles triangle are equal.
    *   **Theorem 7.3 (Converse of 7.2):** Sides opposite to equal angles of a triangle are equal.

6.  **Properties of Equilateral Triangles:**
    *   All sides are equal.
    *   All angles are equal to 60°. (Derived from isosceles triangle properties).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Congruence:**
Congruence means identical shape and size. Think of two coins minted in the same year – one perfectly covers the other. For triangles, ∆ABC ≅ ∆PQR means if you could pick up ∆ABC and place it on ∆PQR, they would match exactly, with A landing on P, B on Q, and C on R.

**Diagrammatic Representation (Conceptual):**
Imagine ∆ABC and ∆PQR:
*   AB = PQ, BC = QR, CA = RP
*   ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R
*   Vertex Correspondence: A ↔ P, B ↔ Q, C ↔ R

**2. Congruence Criteria Explained:**

*   **SAS (Axiom):** Requires two sides and the angle *between* them to be equal.
    *   *Example:* If in ∆ABC and ∆PQR, AB=PQ, BC=QR, and ∠B = ∠Q, then ∆ABC ≅ ∆PQR.
    *   *Diagram:* [Side]---(Angle)---[Side]
    *   *Caution:* If the angle is not included (e.g., SSA or ASS), congruence is not guaranteed (See Fig 7.10 in NCERT).

*   **ASA (Theorem):** Requires two angles and the side *between* them to be equal.
    *   *Example:* If in ∆ABC and ∆DEF, ∠B = ∠E, BC=EF, and ∠C = ∠F, then ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF.
    *   *Diagram:* (Angle)---[Side]---(Angle)

*   **AAS (Rule):** Requires any two angles and a *corresponding* side to be equal.
    *   *Example:* If in ∆AOB and ∆DOC, ∠ABO = ∠DCO (Alternate angles), ∠AOB = ∠DOC (Vertically Opposite), and OA = OD (Given), then ∆AOB ≅ ∆DOC (as shown in Example 3).
    *   *Reasoning:* If two angles are equal, the third angle must also be equal (sum is 180°). This reduces AAS to ASA.
    *   *Diagram:* (Angle)---(Angle)---[Side] (Side corresponds to one of the angles).

*   **SSS (Theorem):** Requires all three corresponding sides to be equal.
    *   *Example:* If in ∆PAQ and ∆PBQ, AP=BP, AQ=BQ, and PQ=PQ (Common), then ∆PAQ ≅ ∆PBQ (as shown in Example 7).
    *   *Diagram:* [Side]---[Side]---[Side]

*   **RHS (Theorem):** Specific to right-angled triangles. Requires the Hypotenuse and one corresponding side to be equal.
    *   *Example:* If ∆PAB and ∆PAC are right-angled at B and C respectively, with hypotenuse PA common and PB=PC, then ∆PAB ≅ ∆PAC (as shown in Example 8).
    *   *Diagram:* [Right Angle]---[Hypotenuse]---[Side]

**3. Importance of CPCT:**
Once two triangles are proven congruent using any of the above criteria (SAS, ASA, AAS, SSS, RHS), we can conclude that their *Corresponding Parts* (remaining sides and angles) are also equal. This is abbreviated as **CPCT**. It's the primary tool for deducing further equalities after establishing congruence.
*   *Example:* If ∆AOD ≅ ∆BOC by SAS (Example 1), then by CPCT, we can state ∠OAD = ∠OBC and AD = BC.

**4. Properties of Isosceles Triangles:**
*   If AB = AC in ∆ABC, then ∠C = ∠B (angles opposite equal sides are equal).
*   Conversely, if ∠B = ∠C in ∆ABC, then AC = AB (sides opposite equal angles are equal).
*   These properties are often used in proofs, sometimes in conjunction with congruence criteria. (See Examples 4, 5, 6 and Exercises 7.2).
