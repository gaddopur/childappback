📊 **Hierarchy:**
1.  **Solid Shapes:** Focus on non-prismatic solids.
    *   **Right Circular Cone:** Generated by rotating a right-angled triangle.
        *   **Components:** Vertex, Height (h), Radius (r), Slant Height (l).
        *   **Key Relation:** Slant height, height, and radius form a right-angled triangle (l² = r² + h²).
        *   **Surface Area:**
            *   Curved Surface Area (CSA): The area of the slanted surface (πrl).
            *   Total Surface Area (TSA): CSA + Area of the circular base (πrl + πr²).
        *   **Volume:** One-third the volume of a cylinder with the same base radius and height ((1/3)πr²h).
    *   **Sphere:** A three-dimensional figure where all points on the surface are equidistant from the center.
        *   **Components:** Centre, Radius (r).
        *   **Surface Area:** Total area of the curved surface (4πr²).
        *   **Volume:** Space occupied by the sphere ((4/3)πr³).
    *   **Hemisphere:** Exactly half of a sphere, formed by cutting a sphere through its center.
        *   **Components:** Radius (r), Curved Surface, Flat Circular Base.
        *   **Surface Area:**
            *   Curved Surface Area (CSA): Half the surface area of the sphere (2πr²).
            *   Total Surface Area (TSA): CSA + Area of the flat base (2πr² + πr² = 3πr²).
        *   **Volume:** Half the volume of the sphere ((2/3)πr³).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Right Circular Cone:**

*   **Definition:** A solid generated by rotating a right-angled triangle about one of its perpendicular sides.
*   **Components:**
    *   `r`: Radius of the circular base.
    *   `h`: Perpendicular height from the vertex to the center of the base.
    *   `l`: Slant height, the distance from the vertex to any point on the circumference of the base.
    *   **Pythagorean Relationship:** `l² = r² + h²` or `l = √(r² + h²)`.
    ```mermaid
    graph TD
        subgraph Cone Components
            V(Vertex) ---|Height h| B(Base Center)
            V ---|Slant Height l| P(Point on Circumference)
            B ---|Radius r| P
        end
    ```
*   **Curved Surface Area (CSA):** The area of the conical surface excluding the base.
    *   **Derivation Idea:** Unrolling the cone gives a sector of a circle. Cutting this sector into many small triangles, the sum of their areas approximates (1/2) * l * (circumference of base) = (1/2) * l * (2πr) = πrl.
    *   **Formula:** `CSA = πrl`
    *   *Example 1:* Cone with r=7cm, l=10cm. CSA = (22/7) * 7 * 10 = 220 cm².
*   **Total Surface Area (TSA):** The sum of the curved surface area and the area of the circular base.
    *   **Formula:** `TSA = CSA + Area of Base = πrl + πr² = πr(l + r)`
    *   *Example 2:* Cone with h=16cm, r=12cm. First find l = √(16² + 12²) = √400 = 20cm.
        *   CSA = 3.14 * 12 * 20 = 753.6 cm².
        *   TSA = 753.6 + 3.14 * 12² = 753.6 + 452.16 = 1205.76 cm².
*   **Volume:** The space enclosed by the cone.
    *   **Derivation Idea:** Experimentally shown that 3 cones fill a cylinder of the same base radius and height.
    *   **Formula:** `Volume = (1/3)πr²h`
    *   *Example 8:* Cone with h=21cm, l=28cm. First find r = √(l² - h²) = √(28² - 21²) = √(784 - 441) = √343 = 7√7 cm.
        *   Volume = (1/3) * (22/7) * (7√7)² * 21 = (1/3) * (22/7) * 49 * 7 * 21 = 7546 cm³.

**2. Sphere:**

*   **Definition:** A perfectly round geometrical object in three-dimensional space, like the surface of a ball. All points on the surface are equidistant from the center.
*   **Components:**
    *   `r`: Radius, the distance from the center to any point on the surface.
    ```mermaid
    graph TD
        subgraph Sphere
            C(Center) ---|Radius r| P(Point on Surface)
        end
    ```
*   **Surface Area (SA):** The total area of the outer surface.
    *   **Derivation Idea:** Experimentally shown that the string covering a sphere fills four circles of the same radius.
    *   **Formula:** `SA = 4πr²`
    *   *Example 4:* Sphere with r=7cm. SA = 4 * (22/7) * 7² = 616 cm².
*   **Volume:** The space enclosed by the sphere.
    *   **Derivation Idea:** Experimentally shown by water displacement that the volume is (4/3)πr³.
    *   **Formula:** `Volume = (4/3)πr³`
    *   *Example 10:* Sphere with r=11.2cm. Volume = (4/3) * (22/7) * (11.2)³ ≈ 5887.32 cm³.

**3. Hemisphere:**

*   **Definition:** Half of a sphere, obtained by cutting a sphere through its center.
*   **Components:**
    *   `r`: Radius (same as the sphere it's part of).
    *   Has one curved surface and one flat circular base.
    ```mermaid
    graph TD
        subgraph Hemisphere
            C(Center of Base) ---|Radius r| P(Point on Rim)
            C ---|Radius r| T(Top of Curved Surface)
            style T fill:#f9f,stroke:#333,stroke-width:2px
        end
    ```
*   **Curved Surface Area (CSA):** The area of the rounded surface.
    *   **Formula:** `CSA = (1/2) * SA of sphere = 2πr²`
    *   *Example 5(i):* Hemisphere with r=21cm. CSA = 2 * (22/7) * 21² = 2772 cm².
*   **Total Surface Area (TSA):** The sum of the curved surface area and the area of the flat circular base.
    *   **Formula:** `TSA = CSA + Area of Base = 2πr² + πr² = 3πr²`
    *   *Example 5(ii):* Hemisphere with r=21cm. TSA = 3 * (22/7) * 21² = 4158 cm².
*   **Volume:** The space enclosed by the hemisphere.
    *   **Formula:** `Volume = (1/2) * Volume of sphere = (2/3)πr³`
    *   *Example 12:* Hemisphere with r=3.5cm. Volume = (2/3) * (22/7) * (3.5)³ ≈ 89.8 cm³.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Agriculture & Storage:** The example of a **corn cob** (Ex 3) relates directly to Indian agriculture. Calculating surface area helps estimate potential yield (grains per unit area). Similarly, wheat stored in **conical heaps** (Ex 11.3, Q9) is common in rural India and mandis. Calculating volume determines storage capacity, and CSA determines the amount of protective covering (like tarpaulin) needed, impacting costs for farmers or agencies like FCI.
*   **Architecture & Construction:** **Hemispherical domes** (Ex 7, Ex 11.4 Q8) are features in various Indian historical and modern buildings (e.g., Rashtrapati Bhavan central dome, various mosques and temples). Calculating their surface area is crucial for estimating costs of painting, plastering, or specialized roofing, which involves significant expenditure. Conical structures are also used, for instance, in temple 'shikharas' (though often more complex than simple cones).
*   **Everyday Objects & Small Scale Industry:** **Joker's caps** (Ex 11.1 Q7) made for festivals or parties, or **hemispherical bowls** (katoris) (Ex 11.2 Q5, Ex 11.4 Q5) made of brass or steel are common household items. Calculating the material required (sheet area for caps, volume of metal for bowls) and processing costs (like tin-plating) is relevant to small-scale manufacturing units across India.
*   **Infrastructure & Safety:** Using hollow cones made of **recycled cardboard** for barricading roads (Ex 11.1 Q8) reflects practical, low-cost solutions often seen in Indian cities. Calculating the surface area determines the amount of paint needed (affecting maintenance costs for civic bodies) and highlights the use of sustainable materials.
*   **Sports:** The **shot-putt** (Ex 11) is a standard sphere used in athletics. Calculating its volume and mass (using density) relates to material science and sports equipment standards followed in India.
