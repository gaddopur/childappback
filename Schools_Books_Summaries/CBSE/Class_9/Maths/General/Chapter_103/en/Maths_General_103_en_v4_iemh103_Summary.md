# [Class 9] Maths - Chapter 3: Coordinate Geometry

## 🌟 Core Concepts

📊 **Coordinate Geometry Hierarchy:**

1.  **Need for a System:**
    *   Limitation of single reference point (e.g., number line, street number).
    *   Requirement of two independent pieces of information to locate a point in a plane (e.g., street number AND house number, distance from two edges).
2.  **Cartesian Plane (Coordinate Plane / xy-plane):**
    *   Developed by René Descartes.
    *   Formed by two perpendicular number lines intersecting at their origins.
    *   **Coordinate Axes:**
        *   Horizontal line: **x-axis** (X'OX)
        *   Vertical line: **y-axis** (Y'OY)
    *   **Origin (O):** The point of intersection of the axes (0, 0).
    *   **Directions:**
        *   Positive Directions: OX (positive x-axis), OY (positive y-axis).
        *   Negative Directions: OX' (negative x-axis), OY' (negative y-axis).
3.  **Coordinates of a Point (x, y):**
    *   Ordered pair describing the unique position of a point.
    *   **x-coordinate (Abscissa):** Perpendicular distance from the y-axis, measured along the x-axis. Positive to the right of the y-axis, negative to the left.
    *   **y-coordinate (Ordinate):** Perpendicular distance from the x-axis, measured along the y-axis. Positive above the x-axis, negative below.
    *   Convention: Abscissa (x) written first, followed by Ordinate (y), enclosed in brackets: (x, y). Note: (x, y) ≠ (y, x) unless x = y.
4.  **Quadrants:**
    *   The two axes divide the plane into four regions.
    *   Numbered I, II, III, IV in anti-clockwise direction starting from OX.
    *   **Sign Conventions:**
        *   Quadrant I: (+, +)
        *   Quadrant II: (–, +)
        *   Quadrant III: (–, –)
        *   Quadrant IV: (+, –)
5.  **Points on Axes:**
    *   Any point on the x-axis: (x, 0) (y-coordinate is zero).
    *   Any point on the y-axis: (0, y) (x-coordinate is zero).
    *   Origin: (0, 0).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Why Coordinate Geometry?**
To describe the exact position of an object (like a dot on paper, a house on a street, or a student in a classroom) in a two-dimensional plane, we need a reference system. A single reference (like just the street name) is insufficient. Coordinate geometry provides this system using two perpendicular lines.

**Example:** To find a house, knowing it's on 'Street 2' isn't enough. Knowing it's 'House number 5 on Street 2' fixes its location. This corresponds to needing two values (like column and row number in a seating plan).

**2. The Cartesian System**
Imagine two number lines: one horizontal (X'X) and one vertical (Y'Y). Place them so they cross at their zero points (the Origin 'O').

*   **x-axis:** The horizontal line X'X.
*   **y-axis:** The vertical line Y'Y.
*   **Origin:** The point O where the axes intersect. Its coordinates are (0, 0).

📈 **Diagrammatic Representation:**
(Imagine Fig. 3.8 from the text: Two perpendicular lines intersecting at O. X' is left, X is right. Y' is down, Y is up. Numbers marked on both axes.)

**3. Locating Points using Coordinates**
The position of any point P in the plane is given by an ordered pair (x, y).

*   **x-coordinate (Abscissa):** The perpendicular distance of P from the y-axis. It's positive if P is to the right of the y-axis, negative if to the left.
*   **y-coordinate (Ordinate):** The perpendicular distance of P from the x-axis. It's positive if P is above the x-axis, negative if below.

**Example (from Fig. 3.10):**
*   Point P: Distance from y-axis = 4 units (along positive x-axis). Distance from x-axis = 3 units (along positive y-axis). Coordinates of P are (4, 3).
*   Point Q: Distance from y-axis = 6 units (along negative x-axis). Distance from x-axis = 2 units (along negative y-axis). Coordinates of Q are (–6, –2).

📈 **Diagrammatic Representation:**
(Imagine Fig. 3.10: Point P in the top-right region, perpendiculars drawn to axes showing distances 4 and 3. Point Q in the bottom-left region, perpendiculars showing distances -6 and -2.)

**4. Quadrants and Sign Conventions**
The axes divide the plane into four quadrants:

*   **Quadrant I:** x > 0, y > 0 (+, +)
*   **Quadrant II:** x < 0, y > 0 (–, +)
*   **Quadrant III:** x < 0, y < 0 (–, –)
*   **Quadrant IV:** x > 0, y < 0 (+, –)

📈 **Diagrammatic Representation:**
(Imagine Fig. 3.13: The four quadrants labelled I, II, III, IV anti-clockwise, with sign conventions (+,+), (-,+), (-,-), (+,-) indicated in each.)

**5. Points on the Axes**
*   A point lies on the **x-axis** if its perpendicular distance from the x-axis is zero. Its coordinates are **(x, 0)**. Example: A(4, 0), C(–5, 0) in Fig. 3.12.
*   A point lies on the **y-axis** if its perpendicular distance from the y-axis is zero. Its coordinates are **(0, y)**. Example: B(0, 3), D(0, –4) in Fig. 3.12.
*   The **Origin (0, 0)** lies on both axes.
