## 🌟 Core Concepts

📊 **Concept Hierarchy:**

1.  **Motion & Rest**
    *   Definition of Motion (Change in position)
    *   Concept of Rest
    *   Relativity of Motion (Observer-dependent)
    *   Reference Point (Origin) - Essential for describing position.
2.  **Describing Motion**
    *   **Path Length (Distance)**
        *   Definition: Total length of the path covered.
        *   Scalar quantity (Magnitude only).
        *   Unit: metre (m).
    *   **Displacement**
        *   Definition: Shortest distance between initial and final positions.
        *   Vector quantity (Magnitude and Direction).
        *   Unit: metre (m).
        *   Can be zero even if distance is non-zero.
        *   Magnitude can be equal to or less than distance.
3.  **Types of Motion**
    *   **Uniform Motion**
        *   Definition: Equal distances covered in equal time intervals.
        *   Constant velocity.
    *   **Non-Uniform Motion**
        *   Definition: Unequal distances covered in equal time intervals.
        *   Variable velocity.
4.  **Rate of Motion**
    *   **Speed**
        *   Definition: Distance covered per unit time.
        *   Formula: `v = s / t`
        *   Scalar quantity.
        *   SI Unit: m/s (metre per second). Other units: km/h, cm/s.
    *   **Average Speed**
        *   Definition: Total distance divided by total time taken.
        *   Formula: `Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time`
        *   Used for non-uniform motion.
    *   **Velocity**
        *   Definition: Displacement per unit time (Speed with direction).
        *   Vector quantity.
        *   SI Unit: m/s.
        *   Can be changed by changing speed, direction, or both.
    *   **Average Velocity**
        *   Definition: Total displacement divided by total time taken.
        *   Formula: `Average Velocity = Total Displacement / Total Time`
        *   For uniformly changing velocity: `v_av = (u + v) / 2` (where u=initial velocity, v=final velocity).
5.  **Rate of Change of Velocity**
    *   **Acceleration**
        *   Definition: Rate of change of velocity per unit time.
        *   Formula: `a = (v - u) / t`
        *   Vector quantity.
        *   SI Unit: m/s² (metre per second squared).
        *   Positive Acceleration: Velocity increases in the direction of motion.
        *   Negative Acceleration (Deceleration/Retardation): Velocity decreases, or acceleration is opposite to the direction of velocity.
    *   **Uniform Acceleration**
        *   Definition: Velocity changes by equal amounts in equal time intervals. (e.g., freely falling body).
    *   **Non-Uniform Acceleration**
        *   Definition: Velocity changes by unequal amounts in equal time intervals. (e.g., car on a crowded street).
6.  **Graphical Representation of Motion**
    *   **Distance-Time Graphs**
        *   Time (x-axis), Distance (y-axis).
        *   Uniform Speed: Straight line graph. Slope gives speed (`v = (s2 - s1) / (t2 - t1)`).
        *   Non-Uniform Speed: Curved line graph.
        *   Object at Rest: Straight line parallel to the time axis.
    *   **Velocity-Time Graphs**
        *   Time (x-axis), Velocity (y-axis).
        *   Uniform Velocity: Straight line parallel to the time axis. Area under graph gives displacement (`s = velocity × time`).
        *   Uniform Acceleration: Straight inclined line. Slope gives acceleration (`a = (v2 - v1) / (t2 - t1)`). Area under graph gives displacement (`s = Area`).
        *   Non-Uniform Acceleration: Curved line graph.
7.  **Equations of Motion (for Uniform Acceleration)**
    *   Relate initial velocity (u), final velocity (v), acceleration (a), time (t), and distance/displacement (s).
    *   First Equation (Velocity-Time Relation): `v = u + at`
    *   Second Equation (Position-Time Relation): `s = ut + ½ at²`
    *   Third Equation (Position-Velocity Relation): `2as = v² - u²`
8.  **Uniform Circular Motion**
    *   Definition: Motion of an object along a circular path with uniform speed.
    *   Accelerated Motion: Velocity changes continuously due to change in direction.
    *   Speed Calculation: `v = 2πr / t` (where r=radius, t=time for one revolution).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Motion and Reference Point:**
*   An object is in **motion** if its position changes with respect to a **reference point** (origin) over time. Otherwise, it is at **rest**.
*   Motion is relative. An object can be in motion for one observer and at rest for another (e.g., passengers inside a moving bus are at rest relative to each other but in motion relative to someone outside).

**2. Distance vs. Displacement:**
*   **Distance** is the total path length covered. It's a scalar quantity and is always positive.
*   **Displacement** is the shortest straight-line distance between the initial and final points, including direction. It's a vector quantity and can be positive, negative, or zero.
*   📈 **Example (Fig 7.1):** If an object moves from O to A (60 km) and then back to C (35 km from O), the distance is OA + AC = 60 + (60-35) = 60 + 25 = 85 km. The displacement is the final position C minus the initial position O, which is +35 km. If it returns to O, distance = 120 km, displacement = 0 km.

**3. Uniform and Non-Uniform Motion:**
*   **Uniform Motion:** Constant speed/velocity. Equal distances in equal time intervals. Distance-time graph is a straight line.
*   **Non-Uniform Motion:** Speed/velocity changes. Unequal distances in equal time intervals. Distance-time graph is a curve. Most real-life motions (like a car in traffic) are non-uniform.

**4. Speed and Velocity:**
*   **Speed** tells how fast an object moves (distance/time).
*   **Velocity** tells how fast and in which direction an object moves (displacement/time).
*   **Average Speed** is used when speed changes (`Total Distance / Total Time`).
*   **Average Velocity** considers the net change in position (`Total Displacement / Total Time`).
*   📈 **Example (Usha Swimming, Ex 7.3):** Usha swims 90m one way and 90m back (Total distance = 180m) in 1 min (60s). Her final position is the same as the initial position (Displacement = 0m).
    *   Average Speed = 180m / 60s = 3 m/s.
    *   Average Velocity = 0m / 60s = 0 m/s.
*   *Condition for |Average Velocity| = Average Speed:* Motion along a straight line without any change in direction.

**5. Acceleration:**
*   **Acceleration** measures how quickly velocity changes (`a = (v-u)/t`).
*   It occurs if speed changes, direction changes, or both.
*   **Uniform acceleration** means velocity changes at a constant rate (e.g., a freely falling object near Earth's surface, ignoring air resistance). Velocity-time graph is a straight inclined line.
*   **Non-uniform acceleration** means the rate of change of velocity is not constant. Velocity-time graph is a curve.
*   Negative acceleration (retardation) means velocity is decreasing (e.g., applying brakes).

**6. Graphical Analysis of Motion:**
*   **Distance-Time Graph:**
    *   Slope = Speed (or magnitude of velocity if motion is in a straight line).
    *   Horizontal line = Object at rest.
    *   Straight inclined line = Uniform speed.
    *   Curved line = Non-uniform speed.
    📈 **(Fig 7.3 vs Fig 7.4)**
*   **Velocity-Time Graph:**
    *   Slope = Acceleration.
    *   Area under the graph = Displacement (magnitude).
    *   Horizontal line = Uniform velocity (zero acceleration).
    *   Straight inclined line = Uniform acceleration.
    *   Curved line = Non-uniform acceleration.
    📈 **(Fig 7.5, 7.6, 7.7)**

**7. Equations of Motion (Uniform Acceleration):**
*   These equations simplify problem-solving for objects moving with constant acceleration.
    *   `v = u + at` (relates final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration, time)
    *   `s = ut + ½ at²` (relates displacement, initial velocity, acceleration, time)
    *   `2as = v² - u²` (relates displacement, final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration)
*   Remember to use consistent units (preferably SI units: m, s, m/s, m/s²).

**8. Uniform Circular Motion:**
*   An object moving in a circle at a constant **speed** is still **accelerating** because its **direction** (and hence velocity) is continuously changing.
*   The acceleration is directed towards the center of the circle (centripetal acceleration - though this term isn't explicitly used in this chapter).
*   Examples: Moon orbiting Earth, artificial satellites, athlete running on a circular track, tip of a clock hand.
*   📈 **(Fig 7.8, 7.9)** Speed is calculated using the circumference: `v = 2πr / t`.
