## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Scientific Concept of Work**

*   **Definition:** In science, work is done only when a force acting on an object causes it to be displaced in the direction of the force. This differs from the everyday use of the term 'work', which can include mental effort or activities where no displacement occurs (e.g., pushing an immovable wall, holding a heavy load still).
*   **Conditions:** Two conditions must be met for work to be done:
    1.  A force must act on the object.
    2.  The object must be displaced.
*   **Formula:** When a constant force `F` acts on an object, and the object is displaced by a distance `s` *in the direction of the force*, the work done `W` is:
    `W = F × s`
    *   *Diagram Description (Ref: Fig 10.1):* Imagine a block on a surface. A force `F` is applied horizontally, causing the block to move a distance `s` horizontally. The work done is `F` multiplied by `s`.
*   **Unit:** The SI unit of work is the **Joule (J)**.
    *   **1 Joule** is defined as the work done when a force of 1 Newton (N) displaces an object by 1 metre (m) along the line of action of the force. (1 J = 1 N m)
*   **Positive, Negative, and Zero Work:**
    *   **Positive Work:** Done when the force is in the same direction as the displacement (e.g., pulling a toy car horizontally - Ref: Fig 10.4).
    *   **Negative Work:** Done when the force acts opposite to the direction of displacement (e.g., work done by friction on a moving object, work done by gravity on an object lifted upwards).
    *   **Zero Work:** Done when:
        *   There is no displacement (`s = 0`).
        *   The force is perpendicular (90°) to the displacement (e.g., work done by gravity on a satellite orbiting Earth, work done by a porter carrying a load horizontally on his head - assuming no vertical movement).
        *   No force is acting (`F = 0`).
    *   *Diagram Description (Ref: Exercise 18):* These diagrams show force and displacement vectors. If the angle between them is acute (<90°), work is positive. If obtuse (>90°), work is negative. If right (90°), work is zero.

**2. Energy**

*   **Definition:** Energy is the capacity or ability to do work. An object possessing energy can exert a force on another object to do work.
*   **Unit:** The SI unit of energy is the same as work: **Joule (J)**. A larger unit, kilojoule (kJ), is also used (1 kJ = 1000 J).
*   **Forms of Energy:** Energy exists in various forms, including mechanical (kinetic and potential), heat, chemical, electrical, and light energy.
*   **Kinetic Energy (KE):**
    *   The energy possessed by an object by virtue of its motion. Faster moving objects and more massive objects possess more kinetic energy.
    *   Formula: `KE = ½ mv²`
        where `m` is the mass and `v` is the velocity of the object.
    *   *Derivation Insight:* The kinetic energy acquired by an object is equal to the work done on it to accelerate it from rest to velocity `v`. Using `W = Fs` and the equation of motion `v² - u² = 2as` (with `u=0`) and `F=ma`, we arrive at `W = ½ mv²`.
*   **Potential Energy (PE):**
    *   The energy stored in an object due to its position or configuration (shape).
    *   **Gravitational Potential Energy:** Energy stored due to an object's position relative to a reference level (usually the ground). It is equal to the work done in lifting the object to that height against gravity.
        *   Formula: `PE = mgh`
            where `m` is mass, `g` is acceleration due to gravity (approx. 9.8 m/s² or 10 m/s² near Earth's surface), and `h` is the height above the reference level.
        *   *Diagram Description (Ref: Fig 10.7, 10.8):* An object of mass `m` lifted vertically by height `h`. The work done against gravity (`mg`) is `mgh`, which is stored as PE. The work done by gravity (and hence the PE) depends only on the vertical height difference, not the path taken.
    *   **Elastic Potential Energy:** Energy stored when an object's shape is changed (stretched or compressed), like in a stretched rubber band, a wound spring, or a bent bow (Ref: Fig 10.6).
*   **Law of Conservation of Energy:**
    *   **Statement:** Energy can only be converted from one form to another; it can neither be created nor destroyed. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.
    *   **Example (Free Fall):** Consider an object falling from height `h`.
        *   At the top: PE is maximum (`mgh`), KE is zero. Total Energy = `mgh`.
        *   During fall: PE decreases, KE increases (`½ mv²`). Total Energy = `PE + KE`.
        *   Just before hitting ground: PE is minimum (zero if ground is reference), KE is maximum. Total Energy = `KE`.
        *   At all points during the free fall (neglecting air resistance), the sum `PE + KE` (Total Mechanical Energy) remains constant and equal to the initial `mgh`. `mgh + ½ mv² = constant`.

**3. Power**

*   **Definition:** Power is the rate at which work is done or the rate at which energy is transferred or consumed. It measures how fast work is done.
*   **Formula:** `P = W / t`
    where `W` is the work done (or energy transferred) and `t` is the time taken.
*   **Unit:** The SI unit of power is the **Watt (W)**, named after James Watt.
    *   **1 Watt** is the power when 1 Joule of work is done in 1 second (1 W = 1 J/s).
*   **Larger Unit:** Kilowatt (kW) is commonly used (1 kW = 1000 W = 1000 J/s).
*   **Average Power:** If the rate of doing work varies, average power is calculated as Total Work Done divided by Total Time Taken.

**4. Commercial Unit of Energy**

*   The unit used for measuring electrical energy consumption in households and industries is the **kilowatt-hour (kWh)**, often called a 'unit'.
*   **1 kWh** is the energy consumed when a device with a power of 1 kW operates for 1 hour.
*   **Conversion:** `1 kWh = 1 kW × 1 h = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J`.

*   **Activity: Household Energy Audit (Research-based Case Study)** 🔍
    1.  **Monitor:** Record the reading of your home electricity meter (in kWh) at the same time each day (e.g., 8:00 AM) for one week. Note the daily consumption.

    3.  **Calculate:** Estimate the daily energy consumption for each appliance (Power in kW × Hours used = kWh). Sum these up to get an estimated total daily consumption.
    4.  **Compare & Analyse:** Compare your estimated consumption with the actual meter readings. Identify the appliances consuming the most energy. Research the average cost per 'unit' (kWh) of electricity in your area from a recent electricity bill. Calculate the approximate daily and weekly cost.
    5.  **Reflect:** Suggest ways to reduce energy consumption in your household based on your findings.
