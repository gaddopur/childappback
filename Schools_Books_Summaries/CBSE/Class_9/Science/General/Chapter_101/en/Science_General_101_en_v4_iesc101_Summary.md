# [Class 9] Science - Chapter 1: Matter in Our Surroundings

## 🌟 Core Concepts

**1. Matter: Definition and Nature**
    *   Anything with mass and volume.
    *   Made up of tiny particles.
    *   Historical Context: Indian philosophy (Panch Tatva - Air, Earth, Fire, Sky, Water).
    *   Modern Classification: Based on Physical Properties & Chemical Nature.

**2. Characteristics of Particles of Matter**
    *   **Particulate Nature:** Matter is composed of discrete particles (Activity 1.1: Salt/Sugar in water).
    *   **Very Small Size:** Particles are incredibly small (Activity 1.2: Potassium Permanganate/Dettol dilution).
    *   **Spaces Between Particles:** Particles have intermolecular spaces (Activity 1.1, 1.2 - Dissolution).
    *   **Continuous Motion:** Particles are constantly moving and possess kinetic energy.
        *   Movement increases with temperature.
        *   **Diffusion:** Intermixing of particles of different types of matter on their own (Activities 1.3, 1.4, 1.5 - Incense stick, ink/honey in water, crystals in hot/cold water). Diffusion is faster at higher temperatures and in gases > liquids > solids.
    *   **Attraction Between Particles:** Particles attract each other with a force (inter-particle force).
        *   Strength varies between different types of matter (Activities 1.6, 1.7, 1.8 - Human chain, breaking substances, cutting water surface). Strongest in solids, weakest in gases.

**3. States of Matter**
    *   Exist due to variations in particle characteristics (space, motion, attraction).
    *   **Solid State:**
        *   Properties: Definite shape, distinct boundaries, fixed volume, negligible compressibility, rigidity.
        *   Particle Arrangement: Closely packed, strong inter-particle forces, low kinetic energy (vibrate in fixed positions).
        *   Examples: Book, Iron nail, Ice, Wood, Sugar crystals (retain individual shape), Sponge (compressible due to trapped air), Rubber band (changes shape under force but regains).
    *   **Liquid State:**
        *   Properties: No fixed shape (takes container's shape), fixed volume, low compressibility, fluidity (can flow).
        *   Particle Arrangement: Less closely packed than solids, weaker inter-particle forces, higher kinetic energy (particles can slide past each other).
        *   Examples: Water, Milk, Oil, Juice.
        *   Diffusion: Solids, liquids, and gases can diffuse into liquids (e.g., dissolved O2/CO2 in water for aquatic life).
    *   **Gaseous State:**
        *   Properties: No definite shape, no fixed volume (fills container completely), high compressibility, fluidity.
        *   Particle Arrangement: Far apart, negligible inter-particle forces, very high kinetic energy (random, high-speed motion).
        *   Examples: Air, Oxygen, LPG, CNG.
        *   Pressure: Exerted due to collisions of fast-moving particles with container walls.
        *   Diffusion: Very fast due to high speed and large spaces. (e.g., smell of food/perfume).

**4. Change of State**
    *   Interconversion of states by changing temperature or pressure.
    *   **Effect of Temperature:**
        *   Heating increases kinetic energy, overcoming attraction forces.
        *   **Melting (Fusion):** Solid → Liquid at Melting Point (constant temperature).
        *   **Melting Point:** Temperature at which solid melts at atmospheric pressure (Indicates strength of attraction forces). Ice melts at 0°C (273.15 K ≈ 273 K).
        *   **Latent Heat of Fusion:** Heat energy required to change 1 kg solid to liquid at its melting point at atmospheric pressure (Energy is 'hidden', used to change state, not raise temperature).
        *   **Boiling (Vaporisation):** Liquid → Gas at Boiling Point (Bulk phenomenon).
        *   **Boiling Point:** Temperature at which liquid boils at atmospheric pressure. Water boils at 100°C (373 K).
        *   **Latent Heat of Vaporisation:** Heat energy required to change 1 kg liquid to gas at its boiling point at atmospheric pressure. Steam (100°C) has more energy than water (100°C).
        *   **Condensation:** Gas → Liquid (Cooling).
        *   **Freezing (Solidification):** Liquid → Solid (Cooling).
        *   **Sublimation:** Solid ⇌ Gas (Direct change without liquid phase). Example: Camphor, Ammonium chloride, Naphthalene balls (Activity 1.13).
        *   **Deposition:** Gas → Solid (Direct change).
    *   **Effect of Pressure:**
        *   Increasing pressure brings particles closer, increasing attraction forces.
        *   Decreasing temperature reduces kinetic energy.
        *   **Liquefaction of Gases:** Applying high pressure and low temperature.
        *   **Dry Ice:** Solid Carbon Dioxide (CO2). Sublimes directly to gas when pressure decreases to 1 atm.

**5. Evaporation**
    *   Liquid → Vapour change below the boiling point.
    *   Surface phenomenon: Particles at the surface with higher kinetic energy break free.
    *   **Factors Affecting Evaporation:**
        *   **Surface Area:** Increases evaporation rate (spreading clothes).
        *   **Temperature:** Increases evaporation rate (more particles gain kinetic energy).
        *   **Humidity:** Decreases evaporation rate (air already saturated with vapour).
        *   **Wind Speed:** Increases evaporation rate (removes vapour from surroundings).
    *   **Evaporation Causes Cooling:**
        *   Particles absorb energy (latent heat of vaporisation) from surroundings/liquid body to evaporate.
        *   Examples: Acetone/Petrol/Perfume on palm, Sweating (cotton clothes absorb sweat, facilitating evaporation), Water in earthen pot (*matka*), Sprinkling water on roof/ground, Desert coolers (effective on hot, dry days).

**6. Units**
    *   Temperature: Kelvin (K) [SI], Celsius (°C). K = °C + 273.
    *   Mass: Kilogram (kg) [SI].
    *   Volume: Cubic metre (m³) [SI]. Common unit: Litre (L) (1 L = 1 dm³ = 1000 mL = 1000 cm³).
    *   Density: kg/m³ [SI]. (Density = Mass/Volume).
    *   Pressure: Pascal (Pa) [SI]. Atmosphere (atm) (1 atm = 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Matter is Particulate:**
Everything around us (chair, air, water, food, *nimbu paani*) is matter, defined by having mass and occupying space (volume). Early Indian philosophers proposed the *Panch Tatva* theory. Modern science confirms matter is made of extremely small particles, far smaller than we can imagine.
*   **Evidence:** When salt dissolves in water (Activity 1.1), the water level doesn't noticeably rise because salt particles occupy the spaces *between* water particles (Fig 1.1). Repeated dilution of potassium permanganate solution still shows colour, indicating millions of particles in a few crystals (Activity 1.2, Fig 1.2).

**2. Particle Characteristics Dictate States:**
The properties of solids, liquids, and gases arise from differences in particle arrangement, motion (kinetic energy), and attraction forces.

*   **Spaces:** Minimum in solids, intermediate in liquids, maximum in gases.
*   **Motion (Kinetic Energy):** Minimum (vibration) in solids, intermediate (sliding) in liquids, maximum (random, fast) in gases. Kinetic energy increases with temperature.
*   **Attraction:** Maximum in solids, intermediate in liquids, minimum (negligible) in gases.

    ![Fig 1.5: Particle arrangement in states of matter.](placeholder_fig_1.5.png)
    *Fig. 1.5: a, b and c show the magnified schematic pictures of the three states of matter. The motion of the particles can be seen and compared in the three states of matter.*

*   **Diffusion:** The spontaneous intermixing of particles. Fastest in gases (smell of hot food/perfume travels far), slower in liquids (ink spreading in water), extremely slow in solids. Heating increases diffusion rate (Activity 1.5).

**3. Properties of States:**

| Property          | Solid                                     | Liquid                                         | Gas                                            |
| :---------------- | :---------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| Shape             | Definite                                  | Takes shape of container                       | Takes shape of container                       |
| Volume            | Definite                                  | Definite                                       | Fills container completely (No definite vol.) |
| Compressibility   | Negligible                                | Very Low                                       | High                                           |
| Rigidity/Fluidity | Rigid                                     | Fluid                                          | Fluid                                          |
| Particle Packing  | Very Close                                | Close                                          | Far Apart                                      |
| Inter-particle Force | Very Strong                               | Moderate                                       | Negligible                                     |
| Kinetic Energy    | Low (Vibrational)                         | Moderate (Translational)                       | High (Rapid, Random)                           |
| Density           | Generally High (Exception: Ice < Water) | Moderate                                       | Low                                            |
| Diffusion         | Extremely Slow                            | Slow                                           | Very Fast                                      |

**4. Interconversion of States:**
Matter changes state with changes in temperature and pressure.

*   **Temperature Changes:**
    *   Heating: Solid → Liquid (Melting/Fusion) → Gas (Boiling/Vaporisation).
    *   Cooling: Gas → Liquid (Condensation) → Solid (Freezing/Solidification).
    *   During state change (melting/boiling), temperature remains constant as the supplied heat energy (Latent Heat) is used to overcome inter-particle forces.

**5. Evaporation and Cooling:**
Evaporation is a surface phenomenon occurring at any temperature below the boiling point. Faster evaporation occurs with larger surface area, higher temperature, lower humidity, and higher wind speed. It causes cooling because the escaping high-energy particles take heat (latent heat of vaporisation) from the remaining liquid or surroundings.
*   **Examples:** Cooling effect of *matka* (earthen pot) through pore evaporation, drying sweat cools the body (why cotton clothes are preferred in summer), acetone on palm feels cold, desert coolers work best on hot, dry days.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Ancient Philosophy:** The concept of matter being composed of five basic elements – *Panch Tatva* (Air—*Vayu*, Earth—*Prithvi*, Fire—*Agni*, Sky—*Akash*, Water—*Jal*) – is a significant contribution from early Indian philosophers, showing an early attempt to classify the natural world.
*   **Traditional Practices:** The use of earthen pots (*matkas*) for cooling water during summer is a widespread practice in India. This utilizes the principle of evaporation causing cooling, as water seeps through the pot's pores and evaporates from the outer surface, drawing heat from the water inside. This is an example of indigenous scientific knowledge.
*   **Climate and Lifestyle:** India's predominantly hot climate makes understanding evaporation and cooling crucial. The advice to wear cotton clothes in summer is directly related to cotton's ability to absorb sweat (perspiration) and allow it to evaporate easily, thus cooling the body. Desert coolers are popular in the hot, dry regions of North and Central India, working efficiently due to low humidity.
*   **Cultural Examples:**
    *   The smell of incense sticks (*agarbatti*) spreading during prayers demonstrates diffusion in gases.
    *   Camphor (*kapur*), commonly used in Hindu rituals (*aarti*), exhibits sublimation when burnt.
    *   Making *nimbu paani* (lemonade) involves dissolving sugar/salt in water, illustrating the concept of particles fitting into intermolecular spaces.
    *   The reference to Idu-Mishmi dancers (from Arunachal Pradesh) in Activity 1.6 provides a cultural analogy for strong inter-particle forces.
*   **Modern Applications:** The adoption of Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) as a vehicular fuel in major Indian cities like Delhi aims to reduce air pollution. This highlights the practical application of gas compressibility for storage and transport, impacting urban environment and economy (fuel costs). The challenges related to CNG infrastructure and safety are also relevant socio-economic aspects.
