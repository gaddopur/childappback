# [Class 9] Matter in Our Surroundings - Chapter 1

## 🌟 Core Concepts

📊 **Matter: Definition and Classification**
*   **Definition:** Anything that occupies space and has mass.
    *   Examples: Air, water, food, stones, plants, animals, sand particles.
*   **Early Classifications:**
    *   Indian Philosophers: "Panch Tatva" (Air, Earth, Fire, Sky, Water).
    *   Ancient Greek Philosophers: Similar classification.
*   **Modern Classification:**
    *   Based on Physical Properties (Focus of this chapter: Solid, Liquid, Gas).
    *   Based on Chemical Nature (Discussed in later chapters).

📊 **Particle Nature of Matter**
*   Matter is composed of extremely small particles.
*   **Characteristics of Particles:**
    1.  **Have Space Between Them:** Particles are not tightly packed; there are intermolecular spaces.
    2.  **Are Continuously Moving:** Possess kinetic energy; movement increases with temperature. This leads to **Diffusion**: Intermixing of particles of different types of matter on their own.
    3.  **Attract Each Other:** Held together by forces of attraction (intermolecular forces); strength varies between different types of matter.

📊 **States of Matter**
*   Three primary states: Solid, Liquid, Gas.
*   States arise due to variations in particle characteristics (spacing, kinetic energy, forces of attraction).
*   **Properties:** Shape, Volume, Compressibility, Rigidity/Fluidity, Density, Particle Arrangement, Kinetic Energy.

📊 **Change of State**
*   Matter can change from one state to another.
*   **Factors Influencing Change:** Temperature and Pressure.
*   **Processes:**
    *   **Melting (Fusion):** Solid → Liquid (at Melting Point)
    *   **Boiling (Vaporization):** Liquid → Gas (at Boiling Point)
    *   **Condensation:** Gas → Liquid
    *   **Freezing (Solidification):** Liquid → Solid
    *   **Sublimation:** Solid → Gas (directly)
    *   **Deposition:** Gas → Solid (directly)
*   **Latent Heat:** Hidden heat energy absorbed or released during a change of state without a change in temperature.
    *   **Latent Heat of Fusion:** Heat required to convert 1 kg solid to liquid at its melting point.
    *   **Latent Heat of Vaporization:** Heat required to convert 1 kg liquid to gas at its boiling point.

📊 **Evaporation**
*   Conversion of liquid to vapour at any temperature below its boiling point.
*   A surface phenomenon.
*   **Factors Affecting Evaporation:** Surface Area, Temperature, Humidity, Wind Speed.
*   **Effect:** Causes Cooling (due to absorption of energy from surroundings).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Matter is Particulate**
*   Early debate: Continuous vs. Particulate.
*   **Evidence (Activity 1.1):** When salt or sugar dissolves in water, the volume doesn't significantly change. The particles of salt/sugar occupy the spaces *between* the water particles. (See Fig. 1.1).
    *   *Fig. 1.1 Concept:* Particles of solute fit into intermolecular spaces of the solvent.
*   **Evidence (Activity 1.2):** A few crystals of potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) can colour a large volume of water through repeated dilution. This shows one crystal contains millions of tiny particles. The same applies to Dettol's smell spreading upon dilution. (See Fig. 1.2).
    *   *Conclusion:* Particles of matter are incredibly small.

**2. Characteristics of Particles**
*   **Spaces (Activity 1.1, 1.2):** Dissolving substances (salt, sugar, KMnO₄, Dettol) and making beverages like tea, coffee, or *nimbu paani* demonstrate that particles of one substance fit into the spaces between particles of another.
*   **Movement & Diffusion (Activities 1.3, 1.4, 1.5):**
    *   *Incense Stick (1.3):* Smell spreads due to particles moving and mixing with air (diffusion). Smell of *hot* food travels faster because particles have higher kinetic energy at higher temperatures.
    *   *Ink/Honey in Water (1.4):* Ink spreads faster than honey, showing diffusion rate depends on the substance. Both eventually spread evenly.
    *   *KMnO₄ in Hot/Cold Water (1.5):* Dissolves and spreads faster in hot water because particles have higher kinetic energy and move faster, increasing the rate of diffusion.
    *   *Kinetic Energy:* Particles possess kinetic energy due to motion. It increases with temperature.
    *   *Diffusion:* The spontaneous intermixing of particles of different types of matter. Faster in gases > liquids > solids. Faster at higher temperatures.
*   **Attraction (Activities 1.6, 1.7, 1.8):**
    *   *Human Chains (1.6):* Easier to break chains formed by fingertip touch (weak attraction) than locked arms (strong attraction, like Idu-Mishmi dancers). Simulates varying forces between particles.
    *   *Breaking Substances (1.7):* Easier to break chalk than an iron nail, indicating stronger forces of attraction between iron particles.
    *   *Cutting Water (1.8):* Difficult to 'cut' water permanently because particles attract each other and maintain cohesion.
    *   *Conclusion:* Particles of matter attract each other with a force whose strength varies depending on the substance.

**3. States of Matter: Properties**

| Property          | Solid                                   | Liquid                                       | Gas                                             |
| :---------------- | :-------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| **Shape**         | Definite                                | Indefinite (takes container shape)           | Indefinite (takes container shape)              |
| **Volume**        | Definite                                | Definite                                     | Indefinite (fills container completely)         |
| **Compressibility** | Negligible                              | Very Low                                     | High                                            |
| **Rigidity/Fluidity** | Rigid (maintain shape)                  | Fluid (can flow)                             | Fluid (can flow)                                |
| **Particle Spacing**| Very close                              | Moderately close                             | Far apart                                       |
| **Interparticle Force**| Strongest                               | Intermediate                                 | Weakest                                         |
| **Kinetic Energy**| Lowest                                  | Intermediate                                 | Highest                                         |
| **Diffusion Rate**| Very Slow                               | Moderate                                     | Very Fast                                       |
| **Density**       | Generally High                          | Generally Lower than solids                  | Very Low                                        |
| **Particle Motion** | Vibrate about fixed positions           | Move randomly within the bulk              | Move randomly at high speeds                    |
*   **Special Cases (Solids):**
    *   *Rubber band:* Changes shape under force but regains it; breaks if excessive force is applied. Considered solid.
    *   *Sugar/Salt:* Individual crystals have fixed shapes. Take the shape of the container *collectively*. Considered solid.
    *   *Sponge:* Compressible due to trapped air in pores, which is expelled. The solid matrix itself is not compressible. Considered solid.
*   **Liquids:** Solids (sugar), liquids (ink), and gases (O₂, CO₂) can diffuse into liquids. Dissolved O₂ is crucial for aquatic life.
*   **Gases:** High compressibility allows storage (LPG, Oxygen cylinders, CNG). Rapid diffusion causes smells (food, perfume) to spread quickly. Gas pressure is due to particles colliding with container walls. (See Fig 1.5 for particle arrangement).

📈 *Fig 1.5 Concept: Visual representation of particle arrangement and motion in solids (ordered, vibrating), liquids (close but mobile), and gases (far apart, random high-speed motion).*

**4. Change of State**

*   **Effect of Temperature (Activity 1.12):**
    *   Heating a solid increases particle kinetic energy → vibration increases → particles overcome attraction → solid melts into liquid (**Melting/Fusion**).
    *   **Melting Point:** The constant temperature at which a solid turns into liquid at atmospheric pressure (Ice: 0°C or 273.15 K). Indicates the strength of interparticle forces.
    *   Heating a liquid increases particle kinetic energy → particles move faster → particles gain enough energy to escape into gas phase (**Boiling/Vaporization**).
    *   **Boiling Point:** The constant temperature at which a liquid turns into gas at atmospheric pressure (Water: 100°C or 373.15 K). It's a bulk phenomenon.
    *   **Latent Heat:** During melting or boiling, supplied heat energy is used to overcome interparticle forces and change the state, *not* to raise the temperature.
        *   *Latent Heat of Fusion (Ice):* Energy absorbed to change ice at 0°C to water at 0°C. Water particles at 0°C have *more* energy than ice particles at 0°C.
        *   *Latent Heat of Vaporization (Water):* Energy absorbed to change water at 100°C to steam at 100°C. Steam particles at 100°C have *more* energy than water particles at 100°C (hence steam causes more severe burns).
*   **Sublimation & Deposition (Activity 1.13):**
    *   Some substances change directly from solid to gas (**Sublimation**) or gas to solid (**Deposition**) without passing through the liquid state.
    *   Example: Camphor, Ammonium chloride, Naphthalene balls. (See Fig 1.7).
*   **Effect of Pressure:**
    *   Increasing pressure brings gas particles closer, increasing interparticle forces. (See Fig 1.8).
    *   Applying high pressure and reducing temperature can **liquefy gases**.
    *   **Dry Ice:** Solid Carbon Dioxide (CO₂). Stored under high pressure. Sublimes directly to gaseous CO₂ when pressure is reduced to 1 atm.
*   **Interconversion Summary:** Temperature and pressure determine the state of a substance. (See Fig 1.9).

📈 *Fig 1.9 Concept: Diagram showing the transitions between solid, liquid, and gas states (Melting, Freezing, Boiling, Condensation, Sublimation, Deposition) driven by changes in temperature and pressure.*

**5. Evaporation**

*   Liquid changing to vapour below its boiling point. Occurs at the surface.
*   Particles at the surface with higher kinetic energy break free from forces of attraction.
*   **Factors Affecting Rate (Activity 1.14):**
    *   *Increase Surface Area:* Spreading clothes helps them dry faster.
    *   *Increase Temperature:* More particles gain sufficient kinetic energy.
    *   *Decrease Humidity:* Air can hold more vapour if it's less saturated.
    *   *Increase Wind Speed:* Vapour particles are carried away, allowing more evaporation.
*   **Evaporation Causes Cooling:**
    *   Particles absorb energy (latent heat of vaporization) from the surroundings (liquid bulk, container, body surface) to evaporate. This energy loss cools the surroundings.
    *   Examples: Acetone/petrol/perfume on palm feels cold. Sprinkling water on roofs/ground cools the surface. Sweating cools the body (especially effective with cotton clothes which absorb sweat and expose it to evaporation). Water in an earthen pot (*matka*) cools as water seeps through pores and evaporates from the outer surface. Condensation on a cold glass (water vapour in air loses energy and liquefies).

**Units:**
*   Temperature: Kelvin (K) [SI], Celsius (°C). 0°C = 273.15 K (approx. 273 K). K = °C + 273; °C = K - 273.
*   Mass: Kilogram (kg) [SI].
*   Volume: Cubic metre (m³) [SI]. Common: Litre (L), 1 L = 1 dm³ = 1000 mL = 1000 cm³.
*   Density: kg/m³ [SI]. Density = Mass/Volume.
*   Pressure: Pascal (Pa) [SI]. Common: atmosphere (atm). 1 atm ≈ 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa.
