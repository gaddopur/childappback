## 🌟 Core Concepts

📊 **Concept Hierarchy:**

1.  **Discovery of the Cell:**
    *   Robert Hooke (1665): Observed cork cells (dead), coined the term "cell".
    *   Leeuwenhoek (1674): Observed free-living cells (pond water).
    *   Robert Brown (1831): Discovered the nucleus.
    *   Purkinje (1839): Coined the term "protoplasm".
2.  **Cell Theory:**
    *   Schleiden (1838) & Schwann (1839): All plants and animals are composed of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life.
    *   Virchow (1855): All cells arise from pre-existing cells.
3.  **Types of Organisms based on Cell Number:**
    *   Unicellular: Single-celled organisms (e.g., Amoeba, Paramoecium, bacteria).
    *   Multicellular: Organisms made of many cells (e.g., fungi, plants, animals).
4.  **Cell Shape and Size:**
    *   Variable (e.g., Amoeba) or fixed (e.g., nerve cells).
    *   Related to specific function.
5.  **Structural Organisation of a Cell:**
    *   **Common Features:** Plasma Membrane, Nucleus, Cytoplasm.
    *   **Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane):**
        *   Outer boundary (animal cells); selectively permeable.
        *   Made of lipids and proteins.
        *   Transport Mechanisms: Diffusion (gases like O2, CO2), Osmosis (water).
        *   Endocytosis (e.g., Amoeba feeding).
    *   **Cell Wall:**
        *   Rigid outer layer in plants, fungi, bacteria (outside plasma membrane).
        *   Plant cell wall mainly cellulose; provides structural strength.
        *   Prevents bursting in hypotonic solutions; involved in plasmolysis.
    *   **Nucleus:**
        *   "Control center"; contains genetic material.
        *   Nuclear membrane (double-layered, porous) - present in eukaryotes.
        *   Contains Chromosomes (DNA + protein) -> Genes.
        *   Chromatin material (in non-dividing cells).
        *   Role in cellular reproduction and directing cell activities.
        *   Nucleoid: Undefined nuclear region in prokaryotes (lacks nuclear membrane).
    *   **Cytoplasm:**
        *   Fluid content inside plasma membrane, outside nucleus.
        *   Contains cell organelles.
    *   **Cell Organelles (Membrane-bound structures in Eukaryotes):**
        *   Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER): Network of membranes (RER with ribosomes for protein synthesis, SER for lipid synthesis, detoxification). Membrane biogenesis. Transport channel.
        *   Golgi Apparatus: Stacks of flattened sacs (cisterns); modifies, packages, dispatches substances; lysosome formation.
        *   Lysosomes: "Suicide bags"; contain digestive enzymes; waste disposal, digest foreign material/worn-out organelles.
        *   Mitochondria: "Powerhouses"; ATP production (energy currency); double membrane; own DNA and ribosomes.
        *   Plastids (Plant cells): Chromoplasts (coloured, e.g., Chloroplasts for photosynthesis) and Leucoplasts (storage); own DNA and ribosomes.
        *   Vacuoles: Storage sacs; large central vacuole in plants (turgidity, storage); smaller in animals; food vacuole (Amoeba), contractile vacuole (unicellular organisms).
6.  **Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells:**
    *   Prokaryotes: Lack nuclear membrane (nucleoid), lack membrane-bound organelles, generally smaller (1-10 µm), single chromosome (e.g., bacteria).
    *   Eukaryotes: True nucleus with nuclear membrane, membrane-bound organelles present, generally larger (5-100 µm), multiple chromosomes (e.g., plants, animals, fungi).
7.  **Cell Division:**
    *   Process of forming new cells.
    *   Mitosis: For growth and repair; produces two identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the mother cell.
    *   Meiosis: For gamete formation (reproduction); involves two divisions; produces four daughter cells with half the chromosome number of the mother cell.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. The Cell as the Basic Unit:**
All living organisms are composed of fundamental units called cells. This concept originated with Robert Hooke's observation of cork in 1665 using a self-designed microscope (similar concept to Fig 5.1). He saw honeycomb-like compartments he termed "cells". Later discoveries by Leeuwenhoek, Brown, Schleiden, Schwann, and Virchow established the Cell Theory: cells are the basic structural and functional units of life, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

**2. Cellular Diversity:**
Organisms can be unicellular (like *Amoeba*, *Paramoecium*) or multicellular (like plants and animals). Within multicellular organisms, cells differentiate to perform specific functions, leading to variations in shape and size (e.g., nerve cells, muscle cells, blood cells - depicted conceptually in Fig 5.3). Despite differences, fundamental structures are shared. Activity 5.1 (Observing onion peel cells, Fig 5.2) demonstrates the basic structure of plant cells, showing distinct cell walls and nuclei. Activity 5.7 (Observing human cheek cells) shows typical animal cell structures.

**3. The Plasma Membrane: The Gatekeeper:**
This is the outermost boundary in animal cells, located just inside the cell wall in plant cells. It is selectively permeable, controlling the entry and exit of substances.
*   **Diffusion:** Movement of substances like O2 and CO2 from high to low concentration across the membrane. Essential for gaseous exchange.
*   **Osmosis:** Movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of high water concentration (dilute solution) to low water concentration (concentrated solution).
    *   **Hypotonic Solution:** External solution is dilute; cell gains water and swells (or bursts in animal cells).
    *   **Isotonic Solution:** Equal concentration; no net water movement; cell size remains stable.
    *   **Hypertonic Solution:** External solution is concentrated; cell loses water and shrinks. (Activities 5.3 with eggs and 5.4 with raisins demonstrate osmosis).
*   **Endocytosis:** Flexibility allows engulfing food/material (e.g., *Amoeba*).

**4. The Cell Wall: Structural Support (Plants, Fungi, Bacteria):**
A rigid layer outside the plasma membrane, primarily cellulose in plants. It provides structural support and protection. It allows cells to withstand hypotonic environments without bursting by resisting the osmotic pressure (turgor pressure). Plasmolysis (shrinkage of cell contents away from the cell wall) occurs when a plant cell loses water in a hypertonic solution (Activity 5.6 with *Rhoeo* leaf).

**5. The Nucleus: The Control Centre:**
Enclosed by a double nuclear membrane (in eukaryotes), it contains chromosomes made of DNA (carrying genetic information as genes) and protein. It controls cell growth, metabolism, and reproduction (cell division). In prokaryotes (like bacteria, Fig 5.4), the genetic material (nucleoid) lies in the cytoplasm without a nuclear membrane.

**6. Cytoplasm and Organelles: The Functional Machinery:**
The cytoplasm is the jelly-like substance filling the cell, enclosing various organelles in eukaryotes. Organelles are membrane-bound structures performing specific functions (division of labour):
*   **ER (Endoplasmic Reticulum):** Transport network; RER synthesizes proteins (due to ribosomes), SER synthesizes lipids and detoxifies. Involved in membrane biogenesis.
*   **Golgi Apparatus:** Processes, packages, and transports materials synthesized in the ER. Forms lysosomes. (Named after Camillo Golgi).
*   **Lysosomes:** Contain digestive enzymes to break down waste, foreign particles, and worn-out organelles ("waste disposal"). Can cause cell self-destruction ("suicide bags") if they burst.
*   **Mitochondria:** Generate energy (ATP) through cellular respiration ("powerhouses"). Have their own DNA and ribosomes. (Fig 5.5 Animal Cell / Fig 5.6 Plant Cell show organelles).
*   **Plastids (Plants):** Chloroplasts (contain chlorophyll for photosynthesis), Chromoplasts (contain other pigments), Leucoplasts (storage). Have their own DNA and ribosomes.
*   **Vacuoles:** Storage (water, nutrients, waste). Large central vacuole in plant cells maintains turgidity.

**7. Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells:**
A fundamental distinction based on nuclear organization and organelles. Prokaryotes are simpler, lacking a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles, while eukaryotes are more complex with these features.

**8. Cell Division: Continuity of Life:**
*   **Mitosis:** Produces identical cells for growth and repair (Fig 5.7). Maintains chromosome number.
*   **Meiosis:** Produces gametes (sex cells) for reproduction (Fig 5.8). Involves two divisions, halving the chromosome number.
