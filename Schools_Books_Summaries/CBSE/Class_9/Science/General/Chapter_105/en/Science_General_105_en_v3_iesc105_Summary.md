## 🌟 Core Concepts

This hierarchy outlines the fundamental concepts related to the cell as the basic unit of life:

📊 **Concept Hierarchy:**

1.  **Discovery of the Cell**
    *   Robert Hooke (1665): Observation of cork cells (dead cells), term "cell".
    *   Leeuwenhoek (1674): Observation of free-living cells (pond water).
    *   Robert Brown (1831): Discovery of the nucleus.
    *   Purkinje (1839): Coining the term "protoplasm".
2.  **Cell Theory**
    *   Schleiden (1838) & Schwann (1839): All plants and animals are composed of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life.
    *   Virchow (1855): All cells arise from pre-existing cells.
3.  **Types of Organisms based on Cell Number**
    *   Unicellular: Single-celled organisms (e.g., Amoeba, Paramoecium, bacteria, Chlamydomonas).
    *   Multicellular: Organisms made of many cells (e.g., fungi, plants, animals).
4.  **Cell Structure & Organisation**
    *   **Basic Components (Common Features):**
        *   Plasma Membrane (Cell Membrane)
        *   Nucleus
        *   Cytoplasm
    *   **Cell Types based on Nuclear Organisation:**
        *   Prokaryotic Cells (Lack nuclear membrane, e.g., bacteria).
        *   Eukaryotic Cells (Have true nucleus with membrane, e.g., plant/animal cells).
    *   **Specific Structures:**
        *   **Plasma Membrane:** Structure (lipids, proteins), Function (selectively permeable, diffusion, osmosis, endocytosis).
        *   **Cell Wall:** Present in plants, fungi, bacteria (outside plasma membrane); provides structural strength and protection (e.g., against hypotonic environments); composed mainly of cellulose in plants.
        *   **Nucleus:** Control center; contains genetic material (DNA, chromosomes, chromatin); nuclear membrane (in eukaryotes); nucleoid (in prokaryotes).
        *   **Cytoplasm:** Fluid matrix containing organelles.
        *   **Cell Organelles (Membrane-bound, mainly in Eukaryotes):**
            *   Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER): Rough ER (RER - protein synthesis/transport), Smooth ER (SER - lipid synthesis, detoxification).
            *   Golgi Apparatus: Modification, sorting, packaging of materials.
            *   Lysosomes: Waste disposal, digestion ("suicide bags").
            *   Mitochondria: Cellular respiration, ATP production ("powerhouses").
            *   Plastids (Plant cells): Chloroplasts (photosynthesis), Leucoplasts (storage).
            *   Vacuoles: Storage (large central vacuole in plants for turgidity/storage; smaller in animals; food/contractile vacuoles in unicellular organisms).
5.  **Cellular Processes**
    *   **Transport Across Membrane:**
        *   Diffusion: Movement from high to low concentration (e.g., CO2, O2 exchange).
        *   Osmosis: Diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane (influenced by solute concentration - hypotonic, isotonic, hypertonic solutions).
        *   Endocytosis: Engulfing material from outside (e.g., Amoeba feeding).
        *   Active Transport (Mentioned briefly): Requires energy.
    *   **Plasmolysis:** Shrinkage of cell contents away from the cell wall in hypertonic solutions (plant cells).
    *   **Cell Division:**
        *   Mitosis: For growth and repair; produces two identical daughter cells.
        *   Meiosis: For gamete formation; produces four daughter cells with half the number of chromosomes.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. The Cell: Basic Unit of Life**
*   All living organisms are composed of fundamental units called **cells**.
*   Cells were first observed by **Robert Hooke** in cork (1665). Living cells were first observed by **Leeuwenhoek** (1674).
*   The **Cell Theory** states that all organisms are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

**2. Cell Structure and Function**
*   **Plasma Membrane:** The outer boundary of the cell, made of lipids and proteins. It is **selectively permeable**, controlling the entry and exit of substances via processes like **diffusion** (movement of substances like O2, CO2 down concentration gradient) and **osmosis** (movement of water across the membrane).
    *   📈 **Osmosis Effects:**
        *   **Hypotonic Solution:** Lower solute concentration outside; cell gains water and swells.
        *   **Isotonic Solution:** Equal solute concentration; no net water movement, cell size remains stable.
        *   **Hypertonic Solution:** Higher solute concentration outside; cell loses water and shrinks.
*   **Cell Wall (Plants, Fungi, Bacteria):** A rigid outer layer outside the plasma membrane (mainly cellulose in plants) providing structural support and preventing bursting in hypotonic solutions. **Plasmolysis** occurs when a plant cell loses water in a hypertonic solution.
*   **Nucleus:** Contains the cell's genetic material (DNA) organized as **chromosomes** (visible during cell division) or **chromatin** (non-dividing state). DNA carries hereditary information in segments called **genes**. The nucleus controls cell activities and reproduction. It is enclosed by a **nuclear membrane** in eukaryotes, but absent in prokaryotes (region called **nucleoid**).
    *   *Refer Figure 5.4: Prokaryotic cell showing nucleoid.*
*   **Cytoplasm:** The jelly-like substance filling the cell, enclosing various **cell organelles**.
*   **Cell Organelles:** Specialized structures within eukaryotic cells performing specific functions:
    *   **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):** Network of membranes. **Rough ER (RER)** has ribosomes, synthesizes proteins. **Smooth ER (SER)** synthesizes lipids, detoxifies poisons.
    *   **Golgi Apparatus:** Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids into vesicles for transport. Involved in lysosome formation.
    *   **Lysosomes:** Contain digestive enzymes to break down waste materials and cellular debris ("suicide bags").
    *   **Mitochondria:** Generate energy (ATP) through cellular respiration ("powerhouses"). Have their own DNA and ribosomes.
    *   **Plastids (Plant cells):** **Chloroplasts** contain chlorophyll for photosynthesis. **Leucoplasts** store starch, oils, or proteins. Have their own DNA and ribosomes.
    *   **Vacuoles:** Storage sacs. Large central vacuole in plant cells maintains turgidity and stores substances. Smaller in animal cells.
    *   *Refer Figure 5.5: Animal cell diagram showing various organelles.*
    *   *Refer Figure 5.6: Plant cell diagram highlighting cell wall, chloroplasts, large central vacuole.*

**3. Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells**
*   **Prokaryotic Cells:** Simple, small (1-10 µm), lack a nuclear membrane (have a nucleoid), lack membrane-bound organelles, single chromosome (e.g., Bacteria).
*   **Eukaryotic Cells:** Complex, larger (5-100 µm), have a true nucleus with a nuclear membrane, possess membrane-bound organelles, multiple chromosomes (e.g., Plant and Animal cells).

**4. Cell Diversity**
*   Cells vary in shape and size depending on their function (e.g., nerve cells are long and branched, muscle cells are spindle-shaped).
    *   *Refer Figure 5.3: Various cells from the human body (muscle, nerve, blood, bone, fat, ovum, sperm).*

**5. Cell Division**
*   Cells divide to grow, replace old/damaged cells, and reproduce.
*   **Mitosis:** Produces two genetically identical daughter cells for growth and repair.
    *   *Refer Figure 5.7: Mitosis diagram.*
*   **Meiosis:** Produces four genetically different gametes (sex cells) with half the number of chromosomes for sexual reproduction.
    *   *Refer Figure 5.8: Meiosis diagram.*
