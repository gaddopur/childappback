# [Class 9] Science - Chapter 3: Atoms and Molecules

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This hierarchy outlines the fundamental ideas discussed in the chapter:

1.  **Historical Perspective on Matter**
    *   Ancient Ideas (Philosophical)
        *   Indian Philosophers (c. 500 BC)
            *   Maharishi Kanad: Concept of *Padarth* (matter) and *Parmanu* (indivisible particle).
            *   Pakudha Katyayama: Particles exist in combined forms.
        *   Greek Philosophers
            *   Democritus & Leucippus: Concept of *atoms* (indivisible).
    *   Shift to Experimental Science (Late 18th Century)
        *   Distinction between elements and compounds.
        *   Foundation of Chemical Sciences (Antoine L. Lavoisier).

2.  **Laws of Chemical Combination** (Experimental Basis)
    *   **Law of Conservation of Mass** (Lavoisier)
        *   Mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
        *   Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products.
    *   **Law of Constant Proportions / Definite Proportions** (Proust)
        *   In a chemical substance, elements are always present in definite proportions by mass.
        *   Example: Water (H₂O) always has H:O in a 1:8 mass ratio.
        *   Example: Ammonia (NH₃) always has N:H in a 14:3 mass ratio.

3.  **Dalton's Atomic Theory** (Theoretical Explanation for Laws)
    *   Key Postulates:
        *   Matter is made of tiny particles called atoms.
        *   Atoms are indivisible, cannot be created or destroyed (explains Law of Conservation of Mass).
        *   Atoms of a given element are identical (mass, properties).
        *   Atoms of different elements differ (mass, properties).
        *   Atoms combine in small whole-number ratios to form compounds (explains Law of Constant Proportions).
        *   Relative number and kinds of atoms are constant in a given compound.
    *   Significance: Provided the first scientific basis for the nature of matter.

4.  **Atoms**
    *   Definition: Smallest particle of an element, the building block of matter.
    *   Size: Extremely small (measured in nanometres, nm). 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m.
    *   Modern View: Atoms can be visualized using modern techniques (e.g., image of silicon surface).
    *   Symbols:
        *   Dalton's Symbols: Early pictorial representations.
        *   Berzelius's Suggestion: Use one or two letters from the element's name.
        *   IUPAC (International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry): Approves modern symbols.
            *   Rules: First letter uppercase, second (if any) lowercase (e.g., H, Al, Co).
            *   Origin: English names (Hydrogen - H), Latin/Greek/German names (Iron - Fe from *Ferrum*, Sodium - Na from *Natrium*, Potassium - K from *Kalium*).
    *   **Atomic Mass**
        *   Concept: Each element has a characteristic atomic mass.
        *   Relative Atomic Mass: Mass of an atom compared to a standard.
        *   Standard: Carbon-12 isotope (since 1961).
        *   **Atomic Mass Unit (u)**: Defined as exactly 1/12th the mass of one Carbon-12 atom. (Formerly amu).
        *   Analogy: Fruit seller using watermelon pieces as relative units.

5.  **Molecules**
    *   Definition: Smallest particle of an element or compound capable of independent existence; a group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together.
    *   **Molecules of Elements**: Formed by the same type of atoms.
        *   Monoatomic: Exist as single atoms (e.g., Ar, He).
        *   Diatomic: Consist of two atoms (e.g., O₂, H₂, N₂, Cl₂).
        *   Polyatomic: Consist of more than two atoms (e.g., Ozone - O₃, Phosphorus - P₄, Sulphur - S₈).
        *   **Atomicity**: The number of atoms in a molecule.
    *   **Molecules of Compounds**: Formed by atoms of different elements joined in definite proportions.
        *   Examples: H₂O, NH₃, CO₂.
        *   Ratio by Number of Atoms: Derived from mass ratio and atomic masses (e.g., H:O in water is 2:1).

6.  **Ions**
    *   Definition: Charged species formed from atoms or groups of atoms by loss or gain of electrons.
    *   Types:
        *   **Cation**: Positively charged ion (formed by loss of electrons, e.g., Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Al³⁺).
        *   **Anion**: Negatively charged ion (formed by gain of electrons, e.g., Cl⁻, O²⁻, N³⁻).
    *   **Polyatomic Ions**: Groups of atoms carrying a net charge (e.g., Ammonium - NH₄⁺, Hydroxide - OH⁻, Sulphate - SO₄²⁻, Carbonate - CO₃²⁻, Phosphate - PO₄³⁻).
    *   Ionic Compounds: Formed by the electrostatic attraction between cations and anions (e.g., NaCl, CaO, MgS).

7.  **Chemical Formulae**
    *   Definition: Symbolic representation of the composition of a compound.
    *   **Valency**: The combining power or capacity of an element. Represents the 'arms' an atom uses for bonding.
    *   Rules for Writing Formulae:
        *   Valencies/charges must balance (overall neutral).
        *   Metal symbol written first, then non-metal (e.g., NaCl, CaO).
        *   For polyatomic ions, use brackets if more than one ion is present (e.g., Ca(OH)₂, (NH₄)₂SO₄). No brackets if only one (e.g., NaOH, NaNO₃).
    *   **Criss-Cross Method**: Used to determine the formula of binary compounds and ionic compounds.
        *   Write symbols of elements/ions with their valencies/charges.
        *   Cross over the numerical values of valencies/charges to become subscripts (ignore signs).
        *   Simplify the formula to the simplest whole-number ratio if possible (e.g., Ca²⁺O²⁻ becomes CaO, not Ca₂O₂).
        *   Examples: HCl, H₂S, CCl₄, MgCl₂, Al₂O₃, Na₂CO₃.

8.  **Molecular Mass and Formula Unit Mass**
    *   **Molecular Mass**: Sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in a *molecule*. Expressed in 'u'.
        *   Calculation: Example H₂O = (2 × Atomic mass of H) + (1 × Atomic mass of O) = (2 × 1u) + (1 × 16u) = 18u. Example HNO₃ = 1u + 14u + (3 × 16u) = 63u.
    *   **Formula Unit Mass**: Sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in a *formula unit* of an ionic compound. Calculated similarly to molecular mass but used for substances whose constituent particles are ions. Expressed in 'u'.
        *   Calculation: Example NaCl = (1 × Atomic mass of Na) + (1 × Atomic mass of Cl) = 23u + 35.5u = 58.5u. Example CaCl₂ = (1 × Atomic mass of Ca) + (2 × Atomic mass of Cl) = 40u + (2 × 35.5u) = 111u.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Ancient Ideas vs. Scientific Laws:**
*   Early thinkers like Maharishi Kanad (India, *Parmanu*) and Democritus (Greece, *atoms*) proposed that matter is composed of indivisible particles based on philosophy.
*   Scientific understanding evolved with experiments. Lavoisier established the **Law of Conservation of Mass**: In any chemical reaction, the total mass before the reaction equals the total mass after. (Illustrated by Activity 3.1 where reactants in a sealed flask are weighed before and after mixing).
    *   *Diagram Reference*: Fig 3.1 shows the setup for verifying this law, emphasizing the need for a closed system (corked flask).
*   Proust established the **Law of Constant Proportions**: A given chemical compound always contains its component elements in a fixed ratio by mass, regardless of its source or method of preparation. (e.g., Water (H₂O) always has Hydrogen and Oxygen in a 1:8 mass ratio).

**2. Dalton's Atomic Theory:**
*   John Dalton provided a theoretical framework explaining the laws of chemical combination.
*   **Key Postulates & Explanations:**
    *   Matter is composed of atoms.
    *   Atoms are indivisible and indestructible (explains mass conservation).
    *   Atoms of one element are identical; atoms of different elements differ (basis for unique element properties).
    *   Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds (explains constant proportions).
    *   The relative number and types of atoms are constant in a compound.

**3. Understanding Atoms:**
*   Atoms are the fundamental building blocks of all matter, incredibly small (radii ~10⁻¹⁰ m).
*   **Symbols:** A shorthand notation for elements (e.g., O for Oxygen, Fe for Iron). IUPAC standardizes these symbols, often derived from English or Latin names.
*   **Atomic Mass Unit (u):** The standard unit for atomic masses, defined as 1/12th the mass of a Carbon-12 atom. Relative atomic masses are determined using this standard. (e.g., H ≈ 1u, O ≈ 16u, Na ≈ 23u).
    *   *Analogy*: Fig 3.4 illustrates relative mass using a watermelon divided into 12 parts, where fruits are weighed relative to one part (1 fmu).

**4. Molecules and Atomicity:**
*   Atoms often combine to form **molecules**.
*   **Molecules of Elements**: Contain only one type of atom. Their **atomicity** (number of atoms per molecule) varies: Ar (1), O₂ (2), O₃ (3), P₄ (4), S₈ (polyatomic).
*   **Molecules of Compounds**: Contain different types of atoms chemically bonded in fixed ratios (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, NH₃). The ratio by number of atoms can be determined from the mass ratio and atomic masses (Activity 3.2).

**5. Ions and Ionic Compounds:**
*   **Ions** are formed when atoms gain or lose electrons, resulting in a net charge.
    *   **Cations**: Positive ions (Na⁺, Mg²⁺).
    *   **Anions**: Negative ions (Cl⁻, S²⁻).
    *   **Polyatomic Ions**: Charged groups of atoms (NH₄⁺, SO₄²⁻).
*   Ionic compounds consist of cations and anions held together by electrostatic forces (e.g., Sodium Chloride - NaCl, Calcium Oxide - CaO).

**6. Writing Chemical Formulae:**
*   A **chemical formula** represents the composition of a substance using symbols and subscripts.
*   **Valency** is key – it's the combining capacity of an element.
*   **Steps (Criss-Cross Method):**
    1.  Write symbols of the combining elements/ions side-by-side (metal/cation usually first).
    2.  Write their valencies/charges above them.
    3.  Criss-cross the numerical values of the valencies/charges and write them as subscripts. Ignore the signs (+/-).
    4.  Simplify the subscripts to the smallest whole-number ratio.
    5.  Use brackets for polyatomic ions if the subscript is greater than 1.
    *   *Examples*:
        *   Magnesium (Mg²⁺) and Chlorine (Cl⁻) → Mg₁Cl₂ → **MgCl₂**
        *   Aluminium (Al³⁺) and Oxide (O²⁻) → Al₂O₃ → **Al₂O₃**
        *   Calcium (Ca²⁺) and Hydroxide (OH⁻) → Ca₁(OH)₂ → **Ca(OH)₂**
        *   Sodium (Na⁺) and Carbonate (CO₃²⁻) → Na₂(CO₃)₁ → **Na₂CO₃**

**7. Calculating Masses:**
*   **Molecular Mass**: Sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule (for covalent substances). Example: CH₄ = 12u + (4 × 1u) = 16u.
*   **Formula Unit Mass**: Sum of atomic masses in the formula unit (for ionic compounds). Example: K₂CO₃ = (2 × 39u) + 12u + (3 × 16u) = 78u + 12u + 48u = 138u.
