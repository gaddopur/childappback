# [Class 9] Science - Chapter 4: Structure of the Atom

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter delves into the fundamental structure of the atom, moving beyond Dalton's idea of indivisibility.

1.  **Sub-atomic Particles:** Atoms are composed of smaller particles.
    *   **Electrons (e⁻):** Negatively charged, negligible mass (discovered by J.J. Thomson).
    *   **Protons (p⁺):** Positively charged, mass taken as 1 unit (related to Goldstein's canal rays).
    *   **Neutrons (n):** No charge, mass taken as 1 unit (discovered by J. Chadwick). Located in the nucleus (except Hydrogen).
2.  **Atomic Nucleus:** The central, dense, positively charged core of the atom containing protons and neutrons (discovered by Rutherford).
3.  **Atomic Models:** Theories explaining the arrangement of sub-atomic particles.
    *   **Thomson's Model:** "Plum pudding" or "watermelon" model; electrons embedded in a sphere of positive charge. Explained neutrality but failed otherwise.
    *   **Rutherford's Model:** Nuclear model; dense positive nucleus at the center with electrons revolving around it. Based on the alpha-particle scattering experiment. Failed to explain atomic stability.
    *   **Bohr's Model:** Electrons revolve in discrete orbits (energy levels or shells) without radiating energy. Explained stability and electron distribution.
4.  **Electron Distribution (Electronic Configuration):** Arrangement of electrons in different energy shells (K, L, M, N...).
    *   **Bohr-Bury Rules:**
        *   Max electrons per shell = 2n² (n=shell number).
        *   Max electrons in the outermost shell = 8 (Octet Rule).
        *   Shells fill stepwise from inner to outer.
5.  **Valency:** The combining capacity of an atom, determined by the number of valence electrons (electrons in the outermost shell) involved in achieving a stable octet (or duplet for K shell).
6.  **Atomic Number (Z):** Number of protons in the nucleus. Defines the element.
7.  **Mass Number (A):** Total number of protons and neutrons (nucleons) in the nucleus.
8.  **Isotopes:** Atoms of the *same element* (same Z) with *different mass numbers* (different number of neutrons). Example: ¹H, ²H (D), ³H (T); ¹²C, ¹⁴C; ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Cl.
9.  **Isobars:** Atoms of *different elements* (different Z) with the *same mass number* (A). Example: ⁴⁰Ar (Z=18), ⁴⁰Ca (Z=20).

## 📘 Key Learnings

1.  **Discovery of Sub-atomic Particles:**
    *   Experiments with static electricity and gas discharge tubes indicated atoms were divisible.
    *   **Electron (J.J. Thomson):** Identified as a negatively charged particle with negligible mass (approx. 1/2000 mass of H atom). Represented as e⁻, charge -1.
    *   **Proton (E. Goldstein's Canal Rays):** Positively charged radiations (canal rays) led to the discovery of the proton. Mass approx. 2000 times electron, taken as 1 unit. Charge +1. Represented as p⁺.
    *   **Neutron (J. Chadwick, 1932):** Neutral particle in the nucleus with mass nearly equal to a proton (1 unit). Represented as n. Essential for atomic mass (except Protium, ¹H).

2.  **Atomic Models - Evolution of Understanding:**
    *   **Thomson's Model (Plum Pudding):**
        *   Proposed a positively charged sphere with electrons embedded within it.
        *   Explained overall electrical neutrality (equal positive and negative charge).
        *   *Limitation:* Could not explain results of later experiments like Rutherford's.
        *   📈 *Diagram:* A sphere representing positive charge, with small negative circles (electrons) scattered inside.
            ![Thomson's Model](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Plum_pudding_model.svg/300px-Plum_pudding_model.svg.png) *(Illustrative representation)*

    *   **Rutherford's Nuclear Model (Alpha-Scattering Experiment):**
        *   *Experiment:* Fast-moving alpha (α) particles (doubly charged He ions, ⁴He²⁺) bombarded a thin gold foil (~1000 atoms thick).
        *   *Observations:*
            1.  Most α-particles passed straight through.
            2.  Some were deflected by small angles.
            3.  Very few (1 in 12000) rebounded (deflected by 180°).
        *   *Conclusions:*
            1.  Most space inside an atom is empty.
            2.  Positive charge is concentrated in a very small volume (nucleus).
            3.  Nearly all mass resides in the nucleus. (Radius of nucleus ~10⁵ times smaller than atom radius).
        *   *Model Features:* Positively charged nucleus at the center; electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular paths.
        *   *Limitation:* Could not explain atomic stability. According to classical physics, an accelerating charged particle (electron in orbit) should radiate energy, lose energy, spiral into the nucleus, making the atom unstable. But atoms are stable.
        *   📈 *Diagram:* Central positive nucleus, with electrons orbiting like planets. Alpha particles shown passing through, deflecting, or rebounding.
            ![Rutherford Scattering](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Rutherford_gold_foil_experiment_results.svg/400px-Rutherford_gold_foil_experiment_results.svg.png) *(Illustrative representation)*

    *   **Bohr's Model:**
        *   Addressed Rutherford's model limitations.
        *   *Postulates:*
            1.  Electrons revolve only in certain special orbits called **discrete orbits** or **energy levels/shells** (K, L, M, N... or n=1, 2, 3, 4...).
            2.  While revolving in these discrete orbits, electrons **do not radiate energy**.
        *   Successfully explained the stability of atoms.
        *   📈 *Diagram:* Central nucleus, with electrons in specific concentric circular orbits labeled K, L, M...
            ![Bohr's Model](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Bohr_atom_model.svg/300px-Bohr_atom_model.svg.png) *(Illustrative representation)*

3.  **Distribution of Electrons (Bohr-Bury Scheme):**
    *   Rules govern how electrons fill shells:
        1.  Maximum electrons in a shell = **2n²** (K shell (n=1): 2; L shell (n=2): 8; M shell (n=3): 18; N shell (n=4): 32).
        2.  Outermost shell accommodates a maximum of **8 electrons** (Octet rule). Achieving this stable configuration drives chemical reactions.
        3.  Shells fill in a **stepwise manner** (inner shells fill before outer shells).
    *   **Electronic Configuration:** Notation showing electron distribution. E.g., Carbon (Z=6): 2, 4 (2 in K, 4 in L); Sodium (Z=11): 2, 8, 1 (2 in K, 8 in L, 1 in M).
    *   📈 *Diagram:* Refer to NCERT Fig. 4.4 for schematic structures of the first 18 elements.

4.  **Valency:**
    *   **Valence Electrons:** Electrons in the outermost shell.
    *   **Combining Capacity:** An atom's tendency to react, driven by achieving a stable octet (or duplet for K shell) by losing, gaining, or sharing electrons.
    *   **Calculation:**
        *   If valence electrons are 1, 2, 3, or 4: Valency = number of valence electrons (e.g., Na (2,8,1) -> Valency 1; Mg (2,8,2) -> Valency 2; Al (2,8,3) -> Valency 3).
        *   If valence electrons are 5, 6, 7: Valency = 8 - number of valence electrons (e.g., N (2,5) -> Valency 3; O (2,6) -> Valency 2; F (2,7) -> Valency 1).
        *   If outermost shell is full (8 electrons, or 2 for He): Valency = 0 (Inert/Noble gases like Ne (2,8), Ar (2,8,8)).
        *   Some elements exhibit variable valency (e.g., Phosphorus, Sulphur).

5.  **Atomic Number (Z) and Mass Number (A):**
    *   **Atomic Number (Z):** Number of protons. Unique identifier for an element. In a neutral atom, Number of Protons = Number of Electrons.
    *   **Mass Number (A):** Sum of protons and neutrons (A = Z + Number of Neutrons). Represents the mass of the nucleus (since electron mass is negligible).
    *   **Notation:** `Mass Number` A
        `Atomic Number` Z Symbol e.g., ¹⁴₆C (Carbon: Z=6, A=14, Neutrons = A-Z = 14-6 = 8)

6.  **Isotopes and Isobars:**
    *   **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element (same Z) but different mass numbers (A) due to different numbers of neutrons.
        *   Example: Hydrogen isotopes - ¹₁H (Protium, 1p, 0n), ²₁H (Deuterium, D, 1p, 1n), ³₁H (Tritium, T, 1p, 2n).
        *   Properties: Similar chemical properties (depend on Z/electrons), different physical properties (depend on mass).
        *   **Average Atomic Mass:** Calculated based on the natural abundance of isotopes. E.g., Chlorine exists as ³⁵Cl (75%) and ³⁷Cl (25%), so average mass = (35 × 75/100) + (37 × 25/100) = 35.5 u.
    *   **Isobars:** Atoms of different elements (different Z) but the same mass number (A).
        *   Example: ⁴⁰₁₈Ar and ⁴⁰₂₀Ca. Both have A=40, but different Z (18 and 20).

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Isotope Applications in India:**
    *   **Nuclear Energy:** India's Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) operates several nuclear power plants using Uranium isotopes (e.g., ²³⁵U) as fuel, contributing to the nation's electricity grid. Research reactors like APSARA at Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC), Mumbai, also play a crucial role in research and isotope production.
    *   **Medical Field:**
        *   **Cancer Therapy:** BARC developed the **Bhabhatron**, a telecobalt machine using Cobalt-60 (⁶⁰Co), providing affordable cancer treatment across India.
        *   **Diagnosis & Treatment:** Isotopes like Iodine-131 (¹³¹I) are widely used for diagnosing and treating thyroid disorders, a significant health concern in certain regions of India historically known for iodine deficiency. Technetium-99m (⁹⁹ᵐTc) is used in various diagnostic imaging procedures.
    *   **Agriculture:** Radiation-induced mutation breeding using isotopes helps develop improved crop varieties with higher yields or disease resistance, contributing to India's food security (e.g., work done at BARC and agricultural universities).
*   **Research Institutions:** Premier institutions like BARC (Mumbai), Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR, Mumbai), Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC, Kolkata), and numerous universities contribute significantly to nuclear physics and chemistry research, building upon the foundational concepts of atomic structure.
*   **Historical Note:** While the core discoveries discussed (Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr, Chadwick) were made in Europe, Indian scientists like Satyendra Nath Bose made fundamental contributions to quantum statistics (Bose-Einstein statistics), which is crucial for understanding particle behavior at the atomic level, though typically studied at higher levels.
