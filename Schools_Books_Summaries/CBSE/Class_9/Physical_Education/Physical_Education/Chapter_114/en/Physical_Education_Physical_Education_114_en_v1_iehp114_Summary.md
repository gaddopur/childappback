## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter focuses on understanding the unique health requirements of adolescents in India and the specialized services designed to meet these needs.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Adolescent Health Needs:**
Adolescence (10-19 years) is a critical period demanding specific health attention. Key needs include:

*   **Nutritional Needs:** High prevalence of undernutrition and anaemia.
    *   *India Data (NFHS 3):* 56% females & 25% males (15-24 yrs) anaemic. Anaemia impacts growth, cognition, and future health (maternal/child).
*   **Reproductive and Sexual Health (RSH):** Adolescents face challenges due to societal taboos, leading to misinformation, risky behaviours, STIs/HIV.
    *   *India Data (NACO 2005):* ~50% new HIV infections in 15-24 yrs age group.
    *   *India Data (NFHS 3):* 1 in 6 women aged 15-19 have begun childbearing (early marriage/pregnancy). Sexual harassment is also a concern.
*   **Mental Health:** Significant prevalence (~20%) of issues like depression, substance abuse, suicidal thoughts. Suicide is a leading cause of death in 15-19 year olds.
*   **Substance Abuse:** Increasing use of tobacco and alcohol.
    *   *India Data (NFHS 3, 15-19 yrs):* Tobacco use: 3.5% women, 28.6% men. Alcohol use: 1% women, 11% men.
*   **Accidental Injuries:** Leading cause of death/disability (road accidents, drowning, burns).

**2. The Need for Adolescent Friendly Health Services (AFHS):**
Standard health services often fail adolescents due to:
*   Hesitation and embarrassment, especially regarding RSH.
*   Lack of understanding of their own needs.
*   Preference for peer advice (often unscientific).
*   Need for a non-threatening, confidential environment.
AFHS provides specialized care addressing these unique requirements.

**3. Barriers Preventing Access to Healthcare:**
Adolescents face multiple obstacles:
*   Myths/Misconceptions about RSH.
*   Lack of awareness about available services.
*   Inconvenient clinic timings clashing with school/college.
*   Fear of lack of privacy and confidentiality.
*   Long waiting times in public clinics.
*   Cost of private clinics or transport.
*   Judgmental attitudes of healthcare staff.
*   Requirement for parental consent, especially for RSH issues.

**4. Structure and Characteristics of AFHS:**
*   **Location Models:**
    *   *Fixed Site:* Hospitals (public/private), Clinics, Primary Health Centres (PHCs), esp. under NRHM.
    *   *Outreach:* School-based/linked clinics, Workplace clinics, Satellite/Mobile clinics, Helplines, Websites, Partnerships (e.g., with NYK, NSS).
*   **Key Characteristics & Staff Role:**
    *   **Respectful & Non-Judgmental:** Staff trained to handle sensitive issues without criticism.
    *   **Confidentiality & Privacy:** Information is kept private; shared with parents only if legally required or for safety, after informing the adolescent.
    *   **Patience & Time:** Staff allocate adequate time for comfortable discussion.
    *   **Peer Educator Linkages:** Connecting adolescents with trained peers for support.

**5. Services Provided:**
AFHS offers a comprehensive package including growth monitoring, nutrition advice, immunization, counselling on injury prevention, substance abuse, RSH, life skills, and prevention of common/lifestyle diseases.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Demographic Significance:** India has one of the largest adolescent populations globally (~243 million, 2011 Census), making AFHS a national priority.
*   **National Health Data:** Findings from the **National Family Health Survey (NFHS 3)** highlight critical issues like high rates of anaemia (56% females, 25% males, 15-24 yrs), early marriage and pregnancy (1 in 6 females 15-19 yrs), and substance use (Tobacco: 28.6% males, Alcohol: 11% males, 15-19 yrs).
*   **HIV/AIDS Data:** The **National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO)** data (2005) indicated that nearly half of new HIV infections occurred in the 15–24 age group, emphasizing the need for RSH services.
*   **Government Initiatives:** AFHS are often located within the public health system, including Primary Health Centres established under the **National Rural Health Mission (NRHM)** by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), GOI.
*   **Partner Organizations:** Outreach models may involve collaboration with government bodies like **NACO** and youth organizations such as **Nehru Yuva Kendras (NYK)** and the **National Service Scheme (NSS)**.
*   **Social Context:** Societal taboos around RSH, prevalence of early marriage in certain regions, and issues like sexual harassment, bullying, and ragging are significant factors influencing adolescent health needs in India.
