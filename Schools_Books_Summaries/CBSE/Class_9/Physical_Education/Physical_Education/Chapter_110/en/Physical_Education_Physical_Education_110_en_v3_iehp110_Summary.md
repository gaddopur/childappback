*   **Waste Management:** Addressing the generation, collection, segregation, treatment, and disposal of waste.
*   **Solid Waste:** Discarded materials losing usability, originating from homes, industries, etc.
*   **Types of Waste:**
    *   **Biodegradable:** Organic matter (plant/animal) easily broken down by nature (e.g., kitchen waste, paper).
    *   **Non-biodegradable:** Materials not easily broken down, persisting for long periods (e.g., plastic, metal, glass).
    *   **Hazardous Waste:** Includes Toxic (paints, batteries), E-Waste (electronics), and Hospital/Soiled Waste (infected materials, sharps).
*   **Waste Disposal:** Methods like landfills, burning, and composting.
*   **Consequences:** Improper disposal leads to health risks (disease transmission), environmental pollution (air, water), and aesthetic degradation.
*   **Management Strategies:** Key approaches include **Segregation at Source** (separating waste where it's generated) and the **4 R's (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, Refuse)**.

## 📘 Key Learnings

1.  **Understanding Solid Waste:**
    *   Solid waste (garbage) is anything discarded after losing its utility.
    *   Sources include households, industries, and commercial places.
    *   Waste generation has increased significantly due to population growth, lifestyle changes, industrial expansion, and technology.

2.  **Categorising Waste:**
    *   **Biodegradable:** Decomposes naturally (e.g., vegetable peels, food leftovers, paper, wood). Activity 10.1 demonstrates this decomposition process over time.
    *   **Non-biodegradable:** Does not decompose easily (e.g., plastics, metals, glass). These persist in the environment.
    *   **Hazardous Waste:** Poses significant health and environmental risks.
        *   *Toxic Waste:* Old paints, batteries, bulbs.
        *   *E-Waste:* Discarded electronics (computers, phones). Unscientific dismantling releases harmful contaminants.
        *   *Hospital Waste:* Infected tissues, body fluids, needles ('sharps'). Improper disposal can transmit diseases like HIV, Hepatitis B & C.

3.  **Consequences of Improper Disposal:**
    *   **Health Risks:** Open garbage attracts flies, cockroaches, rats, and dogs. Flies contaminate food, spreading diseases like dysentery, cholera, gastroenteritis.
    *   **Environmental Pollution:**
        *   *Air Pollution:* Burning waste releases harmful gases; decomposing garbage emits foul smells.
        *   *Water Pollution:* Waste runoff during rains pollutes rivers and water bodies, harming aquatic life and contaminating drinking water sources. Landfill leachate can contaminate groundwater.
        *   *Soil Contamination:* Harmful substances leach into the soil.
    *   **Infrastructure Issues:** Plastics clog drains, leading to waterlogging, especially during monsoons.
    *   **Aesthetic Degradation:** Littered surroundings are visually unpleasant and reduce quality of life.

4.  **Waste Management Principles (The 4 R's & Segregation):**
    *   **Segregation at Source:** The crucial first step. Separating waste into categories (wet, dry, hazardous) at home, school, or office using designated bins (e.g., Green for Biodegradable, Blue for Non-biodegradable).
    *   **Reduce:** Minimise waste generation by consuming less, avoiding unnecessary purchases, choosing products with minimal packaging, and not wasting food.
    *   **Reuse:** Use items multiple times instead of discarding them (e.g., reusing plastic containers, glass jars, old clothes, cloth shopping bags, rechargeable batteries).
    *   **Recycle:** Convert waste materials into new products (e.g., paper, glass, metals, some plastics). This conserves resources and reduces landfill load. The role of *Kabariwalas* in collecting recyclables is significant.
    *   **Refuse:** Decline items harmful to the environment or health, especially single-use plastics (e.g., refusing plastic bags).

5.  **Effective Disposal Methods:**
    *   **Composting:** An ideal method for biodegradable (organic/kitchen) waste. Decomposes waste into nutrient-rich manure using microbes. Reduces waste volume significantly ('zero waste' potential for kitchen waste). (See Box 10.3 for compost pit preparation).
    *   **Landfills:** Designated sites for final disposal. Segregation reduces the volume sent to landfills. Proper landfill management is needed to prevent groundwater contamination.
    *   **Proper Handling of Hazardous Waste:** Requires special care due to high risks. E-waste dismantling needs scientific methods; hospital waste needs careful containment and treatment.
