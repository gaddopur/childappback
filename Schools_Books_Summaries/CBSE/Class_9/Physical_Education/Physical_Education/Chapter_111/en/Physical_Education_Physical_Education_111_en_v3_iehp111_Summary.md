## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Diet and Nutrition:**

*   **Diet:** The total variety and amount of food consumed by a person daily. It's influenced by socio-cultural norms, lifestyle, and activity levels.
*   **Nutrition:** The process involving food intake, digestion, absorption, and utilisation of nutrients (proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, minerals, water) to maintain health, provide energy, and support growth and repair.

**2. Essential Nutrients and Their Functions:**

*   **Proteins:**
    *   Function: "Building blocks" essential for growth, repair of tissues (muscles). Constitute ~20% of body weight. Made of amino acids.
    *   Sources: Animal (milk, eggs, fish, meat - contain all Essential Amino Acids/EAA), Vegetable (pulses, cereals, nuts, beans - require variety for complete EAA profile).
    *   *Diagram Idea:* Simple block representing body tissue being built by smaller protein blocks.
*   **Carbohydrates:**
    *   Function: Main source of energy. Broken down into glucose. Excess stored as fat. Provide dietary fibre (cellulose).
    *   Types:
        *   Simple: Quick energy (fruits, milk, sugar).
        *   Complex: Slow energy release (breads, cereals like rice, wheat, *bajra*, *ragi*, whole grains).
    *   *Diagram Idea:* A graph showing quick energy spike from simple carbs vs. sustained energy from complex carbs.
*   **Fats:**
    *   Function: Concentrated energy source, stored for future use, insulation, protection of organs.
    *   Sources: Animal (ghee, butter, milk, eggs, meat), Vegetable (groundnut, mustard, coconut oils). Fast foods are often high in fat.
*   **Vitamins:**
    *   Function: Organic substances required in small amounts for various metabolic processes and protection against diseases. Body cannot synthesise most.
    *   Types:
        *   Fat-Soluble: A, D, E, K (Stored in body).
        *   Water-Soluble: B-complex, C (Not stored, need regular intake).
    *   *Refer Table 11.1:* Summarises sources, needs, and deficiency diseases (e.g., Vit A - Night blindness; Vit C - Scurvy; Vit D - Rickets).
    *   *📈 Diagram Idea:* A pie chart showing division into fat-soluble and water-soluble vitamins.
*   **Minerals:**
    *   Function: Inorganic substances essential for growth and body functions (e.g., bone formation, nerve function, haemoglobin formation). Required in small amounts.
    *   Examples: Calcium (bones, teeth), Iron (haemoglobin), Iodine (thyroid function), Sodium, Potassium (nerve function).
    *   *Refer Table 11.2:* Details sources and functions of key minerals (e.g., Iron - *palak*, meat; Calcium - milk, *ragi*).
*   **Water:**
    *   Function: Makes up ~70% of body weight. Essential for digestion, transport, biochemical reactions, waste elimination, temperature regulation.
    *   Requirement: 8-10 glasses per day. Inadequate intake leads to dehydration.

**3. The Concept of a Balanced Diet:**

*   A diet containing all essential nutrients (proteins, carbs, fats, vitamins, minerals, water) in the correct proportions required for normal growth, development, and maintenance of health.
*   Requirements vary based on age, sex, and physical activity level.
*   **Food Groups (Table 11.4):** A balanced diet includes items from all major groups:
    1.  Cereals & Millets (Energy, Protein)
    2.  Pulses (Protein, Carbs)
    3.  Milk & Milk Products (Protein, Fat, Calcium)
    4.  Meat, Fish, Fowls, Nuts (Protein, Fat)
    5.  Vegetables (Vitamins, Minerals, Fibre)
    6.  Fruits (Vitamins, Carbs, Fibre)
*   **Food Pyramid (Fig 11.5):** Visually represents the recommended proportions of different food groups, with cereals forming the base (consume most) and fats/sugars at the top (consume least).
    *   *📈 Diagram Idea:* A pyramid structure with food groups placed at different levels indicating quantity.

**4. Special Dietary Considerations:**

*   **Adolescents (Boys 12-22, Girls 12-18):** Rapid growth phase increases nutritional needs. Emphasis on Calcium (dairy, beans, greens) for bones/teeth and Iron (greens, beans, fortified foods) to prevent anaemia, especially in girls. Poor habits (skipping meals, fast food) can lead to deficiencies or obesity.
*   **Pregnant & Lactating Women:** Require additional energy, protein, calcium, and iron (Refer Table 11.5 for ICMR recommendations). Crucial for foetal development and preventing low birth weight. Need a balanced diet including diverse food groups (*bajra*, *ragi*, *til*, *amla*, greens, dairy).
*   **Sportspersons:** Need significantly more calories due to high energy expenditure (Refer Table 11.6). Carbohydrates (500-600g/day, complex preferred) are the primary fuel source, not excessive protein. Hydration is critical.
    *   *Pre-Competition:* High Carb, Low Fat/Protein/Fibre meal (e.g., *roti*, rice, fruits).
    *   *Competition Day:* Light, familiar, carb-rich breakfast (e.g., cereal, *roti*). Avoid sugary items or heavy foods.
    *   *During Competition:* Regular fluid intake (water, sports drinks) to prevent dehydration.

**5. Yogic Diet (Ayurveda):**

*   Recommends a *Sattvic* (pure), vegetarian diet of natural, easily digestible foods (raw vegetables, fruits, sprouts, whole grains like rice, wheat, millet).
*   Emphasizes moderation ("Do not fill your stomach"), regular meal times, and cooking methods like steaming.
*   Aids overall well-being when combined with *asanas* and *pranayama*.

**6. Understanding Malnutrition:**

*   An imbalance between the body's nutritional needs and intake. Includes both under-nutrition and over-nutrition.
*   **Under-nutrition:**
    *   Causes: Inadequate intake, poverty, ignorance (food myths/taboos), malabsorption, disease, disasters affecting food supply. Inadequate breastfeeding in infants.
    *   Effects: Stunted growth (physical/mental), increased susceptibility to illness, deficiency diseases (Kwashiorkor/Marasmus - protein deficiency; Anaemia - iron deficiency; Goitre - iodine deficiency; Scurvy, Beriberi, Rickets - vitamin deficiencies).
    *   *Indian Context:* Significant problem, especially in children (NFHS-3: 40% under-5s underweight). Government initiatives like Mid-Day Meal scheme aim to combat this.
*   **Over-nutrition:**
    *   Causes: Excessive intake of calories (especially fats, simple carbs), overeating, lack of physical activity, sedentary lifestyle, genetic factors, emotional issues. High consumption of fast food, sugary drinks.
    *   Effects: Accumulation of excess body fat leading to overweight and obesity. Increased risk of chronic diseases (diabetes, cardiovascular issues).
*   **Body Mass Index (BMI):** A tool to assess weight status (Weight in kg / Height in m²).
    *   <18.5: Underweight
    *   18.5 - 22.9: Ideal (Indian context often uses up to 23)
    *   23 - 24.9: Overweight (Indian context often uses 23-25)
    *   ≥25: Obese
    *   *📈 Diagram Idea:* A simple scale showing BMI ranges and categories.

**7. Eating Disorders:**

*   Severe disturbances in eating behaviour, often linked to psychological issues, common in adolescents (14-18 years).
*   **Anorexia Nervosa (AN):**
    *   Characteristics: Refusal to eat, obsessive fear of gaining weight, distorted body image, significant weight loss, excessive exercise.
    *   Causes: Attempt to cope with emotional problems, stress, peer pressure.
*   **Bulimia Nervosa (BN):**
    *   Characteristics: Recurrent episodes of binge eating followed by compensatory behaviours (purging - vomiting, laxative use, excessive exercise, fasting).
    *   Causes: Family problems, depression, body image issues, societal pressures.
*   **Health Consequences:** Tooth decay, gum disease, osteoporosis, heart problems, dehydration, fainting, low body temperature, delayed menstruation, increased suicide risk.
