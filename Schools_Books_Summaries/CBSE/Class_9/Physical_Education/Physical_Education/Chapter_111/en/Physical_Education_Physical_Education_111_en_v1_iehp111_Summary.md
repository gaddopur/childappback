    *   **Nutrients:** Simpler substances from food breakdown used for building, repair, energy, and protection.
        *   **Macronutrients:**
            *   **Proteins:** (Building Blocks)
                *   Sources: Animal (Milk, Eggs, Fish, Meat), Vegetable (Pulses, Cereals, Nuts)
                *   Function: Growth, Repair of tissues.
            *   **Carbohydrates:** (Main Energy Source)
                *   Types: Simple (Sugars - Fruits, Milk), Complex (Starch - Cereals, Breads, Rice)
                *   Function: Provide energy; Excess stored as fat. Dietary fibre (Cellulose).
            *   **Fats:** (Concentrated Energy Source)
                *   Types: Simple, Compound, Derived (Cholesterol)
                *   Sources: Animal (Ghee, Butter), Vegetable (Oils)
                *   Function: Energy storage, Future use.
        *   **Micronutrients:**
            *   **Vitamins:** (Organic substances for good health)
                *   Types: Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K), Water-soluble (B-complex, C)
                *   Function: Specific functions; Deficiency causes diseases.
                *   Sources: Fruits, Vegetables, Milk, Eggs etc.
            *   **Minerals:** (Essential for growth and functioning)
                *   Examples: Calcium, Iron, Iodine, Sodium, Potassium, Zinc etc.
                *   Function: Various roles (Bone formation, Haemoglobin, Nerve function etc.)
                *   Sources: Varied (Milk, Green leafy vegetables, Salt, Meat etc.)
        *   **Water:** (Essential Nutrient)
            *   Function: Medium for reactions, Transport, Waste elimination, Temperature regulation.
            *   Requirement: Approx. 8-10 glasses daily.
    *   **Nutrition:** Process of food intake, dietary patterns, and utilisation of nutrients for health.
3.  **Balanced Diet**
    *   Definition: Contains all essential nutrients in required proportions for normal growth and development.
    *   Factors Affecting Requirement: Age, Sex, Physical Activity.
    *   **Food Groups:** (For achieving balance)
        *   Cereals, Millets
        *   Pulses
        *   Milk and Milk Products
        *   Meat, Fish, Fowls (and Nuts)
        *   Vegetables
        *   Fruits
    *   **Food Pyramid:** Visual guide for balanced food choices.
4.  **Special Dietary Requirements**
    *   **Adolescents (12-18/22 yrs):** Increased needs due to growth spurt. Emphasis on Calcium and Iron. Risk of anaemia, obesity, eating disorders.
    *   **Pregnant & Lactating Women:** Increased need for Energy, Protein, Calcium, Iron. Importance of balanced diet for mother and child health (prevents low birth weight).
    *   **Sportspersons:** Higher calorie requirement due to strenuous activity. Emphasis on complex carbohydrates for energy. Specific meal planning (Pre-competition, Competition day, During competition).
5.  **Yogic Diet (Sattvic)**
    *   Concept: Vegetarian, natural, easily digestible foods (raw foods, specific spices).
    *   Benefits: Provides energy, promotes well-being (with asanas, pranayama).
    *   Preparation: Steaming preferred.
6.  **Malnutrition**
    *   Definition: Imbalance between nutritional needs and intake (includes under-nutrition and over-nutrition).
    *   **Under-nutrition:**
        *   Causes: Inadequate intake, malabsorption, poverty, ignorance, unhealthy practices (e.g., inadequate breastfeeding).
        *   Consequences: Impaired growth (physical/mental), Deficiency diseases (Kwashiorkor, Marasmus, Anaemia, Goitre, Scurvy, Rickets etc.).
    *   **Over-nutrition:**
        *   Causes: Overeating (esp. fats, carbs), Inadequate exercise, Sedentary lifestyle, Genetic factors.
        *   Consequences: Overweight, Obesity (Excess body fat accumulation), Increased risk of metabolic/digestive disorders.
    *   **Assessing Nutritional Status:** Body Mass Index (BMI) - Weight (kg) / Height (m)².
7.  **Eating Disorders** (Severe disturbances in eating behaviour, common in adolescents)
    *   **Anorexia Nervosa (AN):** Refusal to eat, obsessive fear of gaining weight, extreme weight loss. Linked to emotional problems, stress, body image issues.
    *   **Bulimia Nervosa (BN):** Recurrent binge eating followed by compensatory behaviours (vomiting, fasting, laxatives, over-exercising). Linked to family problems, depression, body image issues.
    *   Health Consequences: Tooth decay, Osteoporosis, Heart problems, Dehydration, Menstrual irregularities, Psychological distress.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Nutrients:**
Our body requires six essential types of nutrients obtained from food:
*   **Proteins:** The "building blocks" made of amino acids, crucial for growth and tissue repair. Found in milk, eggs, meat, fish, pulses, and nuts.
*   **Carbohydrates:** The primary energy source. Simple carbs (sugars in fruits, milk) provide quick energy, while complex carbs (starch in cereals, bread, rice) release energy slowly. Fibre (cellulose) is also a carbohydrate important for digestion.
*   **Fats:** Concentrated energy sources, stored for future use. Found in oils, ghee, butter, nuts, meat, and milk. Excessive intake, especially from fast food, leads to obesity.
*   **Vitamins:** Organic compounds needed in small amounts for various bodily functions. They are fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) or water-soluble (B group, C). Deficiencies lead to specific diseases (See Table 11.1 in the text for details). Sources include fruits, vegetables, dairy, and meats.
*   **Minerals:** Inorganic substances vital for growth and function (e.g., Calcium for bones, Iron for blood). Required in small quantities. Sources vary widely (See Table 11.2 in the text).
*   **Water:** Makes up ~70% of body weight. Essential for transport, chemical reactions, waste removal, and temperature regulation. Recommended intake is 8-10 glasses daily.

**2. The Concept of a Balanced Diet:**
A balanced diet provides all necessary nutrients in the correct proportions to maintain health and support growth. The requirement varies based on age, gender, and activity level. To achieve balance, one should consume foods from all major food groups daily:
*   Cereals & Millets (Energy, Protein)
*   Pulses (Protein, Carbs)
*   Milk & Products (Protein, Fat, Calcium)
*   Meat/Fish/Fowl/Nuts (Protein, Fat)
*   Vegetables (Vitamins, Minerals, Fibre)
*   Fruits (Vitamins, Carbs, Fibre)

📈 **The Food Pyramid (Conceptual Representation based on Fig 11.5):**
This pyramid visually guides balanced eating:
*   **Base (Eat Most):** Cereals, Bread, Rice (Complex Carbohydrates)
*   **Middle Levels (Eat Moderately):** Fruits & Vegetables; Pulses, Milk/Dairy, Meat/Fish/Eggs
*   **Top (Eat Sparingly):** Fats, Oils, Sugar, Sweets

**3. Special Dietary Needs:**
*   **Adolescents:** Require more energy, protein, calcium (for bones), and iron (especially girls, to prevent anaemia). A balanced diet including dairy, beans, and green leafy vegetables is crucial.
*   **Pregnant/Lactating Women:** Need additional calories, protein, calcium, and iron to support their own health and the baby's development/growth. Inadequate nutrition can lead to low birth weight and future health issues for the child. (Refer Table 11.5 for specific additional requirements).
*   **Sportspersons:** Need significantly more calories, primarily from complex carbohydrates, to fuel training and performance. Proper hydration is vital. Diet planning includes specific strategies for pre-competition, competition day, and during competition. Protein intake is important, but excessive amounts don't build more muscle; carbohydrates are the key fuel.

**4. Malnutrition: Under-nutrition vs. Over-nutrition:**
Malnutrition refers to deficiencies, excesses, or imbalances in nutrient intake.
*   **Under-nutrition:** Caused by insufficient food intake or absorption. Leads to stunted growth, weakness, and deficiency diseases like Kwashiorkor (protein deficiency), Marasmus (energy deficiency), Anaemia (iron deficiency), Goitre (iodine deficiency), etc. A major issue in India, particularly among children (NFHS-3 data cited).
*   **Over-nutrition:** Caused by excessive intake, especially of fats and carbohydrates, combined with low activity levels. Leads to overweight and obesity, increasing the risk of diseases like diabetes and cardiovascular problems.

📈 **Body Mass Index (BMI):** A tool to assess weight status.
*   **Calculation:** Weight (in kg) / [Height (in m)]²
*   **Indian Classification (as per text):**
    *   < 18: Underweight
    *   18 - 23: Ideal body weight
    *   23 - 25: Overweight
    *   > 25: Obese

**5. Eating Disorders:**
Serious psychological conditions affecting eating behaviours, often linked to body image and emotional issues.
*   **Anorexia Nervosa:** Self-starvation due to an intense fear of gaining weight.
*   **Bulimia Nervosa:** Cycles of binge eating followed by purging (vomiting, laxatives) or excessive exercise.
Both have severe health consequences, including dental problems, heart issues, and nutritional deficiencies.
