## 📘 Key Learnings

This section details the essential knowledge regarding rules, techniques, and specifications for each sport covered.

**1. Badminton:**
*   **Origin & Governance:** Originated in India (Pune, 1901 rules). Governed internationally by WBF and nationally by Badminton Association of India (BAI, est. 1934).
*   **Court & Net:** Doubles court: 13.40m x 6.10m. Net height: 1.55m (sides), 1.52m (centre).
    ```mermaid
    graph TD
        A[Badminton Court (Doubles)] -- Length --> B(13.40 m);
        A -- Width --> C(6.10 m);
        D[Net] -- Height at Sides --> E(1.55 m);
        D -- Height at Centre --> F(1.52 m);
    ```
*   **Gameplay:** Starts with a toss. Service must be below waist, diagonal. Rally point system; game is 21 points (2-point lead). 'Let' situations pause play without points. Faults lead to loss of rally.
*   **Skills:**
    *   Grips: Forehand (Shake hand), Backhand.
    *   Strokes: Forehand, Backhand, Smash (forceful downward), Drop (gentle, near net), Drive (flat, side zones), Toss/Clear (high, deep).
    *   Service: High/Long, Low/Short.

**2. Gymnastics (Artistic):**
*   **Governance:** Federation International de Gymnastique (FIG, est. 1881).
*   **Apparatus:**
    *   Men: Floor Exercise, Pommel Horse, Roman Rings, Vaulting Table, Parallel Bars, Horizontal Bar.
    *   Women: Vaulting Table, Uneven Bars, Balancing Beam, Floor Exercise.
*   **Basic Skills Examples:**
    *   Floor: Handstand, Cartwheel, Front Handspring, Round-off.
    *   Vault: Squat Vault, Split Vault.
    *   Beam: 'T' Balance, Walking variations.
    *   (Refer text for skills on other apparatus)
*   **Evaluation:** Based on performing specific elements on each apparatus, with points assigned per element.

**3. Judo:**
*   **Origin & Governance:** Derived from Japanese Ju-jitsu by Jigoro Kano (1882).
*   **Area:** 14m x 14m competition area within a larger platform. Includes contest area, danger zone, safety area.
*   **Grading:** Kyu (pupil) grades (White to Brown belt) and Dan (master) grades (Black, Red/White, Red belt).
*   **Scoring:**
    *   Ippon (Full point - decisive throw/hold for 20s).
    *   Waza-ari (Half point - near Ippon throw/hold for 15-19s). Two Waza-ari = Ippon.
    *   Yuko (Lower score - throw lacking two components/hold for 10-14s).
*   **Fundamental Skills:** Rei (Salutation), Kumikata (Gripping), Shintai (Movement), Shizentai (Posture), Ukemi (Breakfalls), Tai-sabaki (Body Management), Kuzushi (Off-balancing), Tsukuri (Fitting in), Kake (Execution).

**4. Swimming:**
*   **Governance:** Federation Internationale de Natations Aquatics (FINA, est. 1908).
*   **Pool:** Standard 50m length, 25m width, 1.80m depth, 8-10 lanes (2.5m width each). Water temp ~24°C.
*   **Strokes:**
    *   **Freestyle (Crawl):** Alternate overarm strokes, flutter kick, side breathing. Any part touches wall on turns.
    *   **Backstroke:** Alternate arm strokes on back, flutter kick, face out of water. Must finish on back.
    *   **Breaststroke:** Simultaneous 'frog-like' kick and 'heart-shaped' arm pull. Both hands touch wall simultaneously on turns/finish.
    *   **Butterfly:** Simultaneous overarm recovery, dolphin kick. Both hands touch wall simultaneously on turns/finish.
*   **Safety:** Warm-up, stretch, swim with lifeguard, know limits, shower before/after.

**5. Table Tennis:**
*   **Governance:** International Table Tennis Federation (ITTF, est. 1926).
*   **Table & Net:** Table: 274cm x 152.5cm surface, 76cm high. Net: 15.25cm high.
    ```mermaid
    graph TD
        A[Table Tennis Table] -- Length --> B(274 cm);
        A -- Width --> C(152.5 cm);
        A -- Height --> D(76 cm);
        E[Net] -- Height --> F(15.25 cm);
    ```
*   **Equipment:** Ball (40mm diameter, 2.7g, orange/white). Bat/Racket (Wooden blade, specific rubber colours - red/black).
*   **Gameplay:** Toss determines serve/receive/side. Service: Ball tossed from open palm, hit to bounce once on server's side, then opponent's. Rally scoring; game is 11 points (2-point lead). Doubles involves alternate hits between partners. 'Let' if serve hits net but lands correctly, or receiver not ready.
*   **Skills:**
    *   Grips: Shakehand, Penhold.
    *   Spin: Topspin (bat angled down), Backspin (bat angled up), Sidespin (often in serves).
    *   Strokes: Offensive/Defensive pushes (Forehand/Backhand).

**6. Wrestling:**
*   **Styles:** Freestyle (arms & legs used), Greco-Roman (no leg attacks/use), Indian Style (Kushti/Pehlwani - traditional, often on clay).
*   **Mat:** 12m x 12m overall, 7m diameter yellow bout area, 1m central circle. Thickness 5-7cm.
*   **Gameplay:** Bouts based on weight categories. Red/Blue uniforms. 3 rounds of 2 mins. Win by fall, points, or opponent disqualification.
*   **Scoring:** Takedown (1 pt), Takedown with back exposure (2+ pts), Push-out (1 pt), Par terre (mat wrestling - turning opponent earns points, hold >5s earns extra point).
*   **Tie-breaking:** Least cautions -> Highest single score move -> Last point scored. Clinch rule forces action in scoreless periods.
*   **Skills:** Stance (Square/Staggered), Motion (Circling, Attacks, Defence), Level Change (Lowering hips), Penetration (Deep step for takedown), Lifting (Using legs/hips), Back Step/Arch (Defensive/Offensive maneuvers).

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Badminton Origins:** India holds historical significance as the place where the first codified rules of Badminton were formulated (Pune, 1901). The **Badminton Association of India (BAI)**, established in 1934, governs the sport nationally, with state associations affiliated to it.
*   **Judo in India:** The text notes specific weight categories used in Judo competitions within India for sub-junior, junior, and senior levels (men and women), highlighting the organized structure of the sport nationally. (Refer www.onlinejbi.org for current categories).
*   **Traditional Wrestling (Kushti/Pehlwani):** India has a rich heritage of traditional wrestling, known as Kushti or Pehlwani, practiced in 'Akhadas' (traditional wrestling schools) often on clay or mud pits. This contrasts with the Olympic styles (Freestyle, Greco-Roman) played on mats but remains a significant part of India's sporting culture. Researching famous Indian wrestlers (Activity 6.9, 6.10) like Sushil Kumar, Sakshi Malik, or Bajrang Punia connects modern achievements to this strong tradition.
