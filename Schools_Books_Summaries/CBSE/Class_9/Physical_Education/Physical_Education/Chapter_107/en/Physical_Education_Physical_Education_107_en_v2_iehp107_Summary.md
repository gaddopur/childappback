## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Team Dynamics:**
    *   Team games emphasize cooperation, communication, and shared responsibility for success. Players learn to work together, respecting each other's roles and contributions (Introduction 7.1).

**2. Game Structures and Rules:**
    *   Each team game has a unique set of rules governing play, scoring, player conduct, and time limits. Understanding these rules is crucial for participation and fair play.
    *   **Example (Basketball):** Played on a 28x15m court (Fig 7.1), 10-minute quarters, 5 players per team on court, scoring via baskets, fouls lead to free throws or loss of possession (7.2.2).
    *   **Example (Cricket):** Played on a field with a central 20.12m pitch (Fig 7.11), involves innings, batting/bowling turns, scoring runs, various dismissal methods (bowled, caught, etc.) (7.3.2, 7.3.3).
    *   **Example (Football):** 90-minute match (two 45-min halves), 11 players per side, scoring goals, rules for free kicks, penalties, throw-ins, corner kicks, and disciplinary cards (7.4.2, Fig 7.12).
    *   **Example (Hockey):** 70-minute match (two 35-min halves), 11 players, scoring goals from within the striking circle, rules for penalty corners/strokes (7.5.2, Fig 7.19).
    *   **Example (Kabaddi):** 40-minute match (two 20-min halves), 7 players, raiding ('cant') and defending phases, scoring points by touching opponents or tackling raiders (7.6.2).
    *   **Example (Kho-Kho):** Two innings (each with two 9-min turns), 9 players chasing/running, scoring by tagging runners, specific rules for giving 'Kho' and changing direction (7.7.2, Fig 7.26).
    *   **Example (Volleyball):** Best of 5 sets (first 4 to 25 points, 5th to 15, win by 2), 6 players, scoring via grounding ball or opponent faults, rotational player movement (7.8.3, Fig 7.30).

**3. Fundamental Skills Development:**
    *   Proficiency in team games requires mastering specific physical skills involving coordination, agility, strength, and technique.
    *   **Dribbling:** Essential in Basketball (bouncing), Football (foot control), Hockey (stick control) (7.2.3, 7.4.3, 7.5.3).
    *   **Passing:** Crucial for ball movement between teammates (Basketball: Chest/Overhead/Bounce pass; Football: Instep pass; Hockey: Push/Hit; Volleyball: Underhand/Overhead pass) (7.2.3, 7.4.3, 7.5.3, 7.8.4).
    *   **Shooting/Scoring:** The objective skill (Basketball: Set/Jump/Lay-up shot; Cricket: Hitting runs; Football: Kicking goals; Hockey: Hitting/Pushing into goal; Volleyball: Spike/Smash) (7.2.3, 7.3.2, 7.4.2, 7.5.2, 7.8.4).
    *   **Defensive Techniques:** Preventing opponents from scoring (Basketball: Guarding; Cricket: Fielding/Bowling; Football: Tackling; Hockey: Stopping/Tackling; Kabaddi: Catching/Blocking; Kho-Kho: Chasing/Tagging) (7.2.3, 7.3.2, 7.4.3, 7.5.3, 7.6.3, 7.7.3).
    *   **Specific Game Skills:** Unique actions like Rebounding (Basketball), Heading (Football), Cant/Raiding (Kabaddi), Giving Kho (Kho-Kho), Service (Volleyball).

**4. Equipment and Field Specifications:**
    *   Standardized equipment and playing areas ensure fair and consistent competition. Dimensions and materials are regulated by governing bodies.
    *   **Courts/Fields:** Specific dimensions and markings for each game (See Figs 7.1, 7.10, 7.11, 7.12, 7.19, 7.26, 7.30).
    *   **Balls:** Vary in size, weight, and material (Basketball, Cricket, Football, Hockey, Volleyball).
    *   **Other Equipment:** Bats, Wickets (Cricket); Sticks (Hockey); Posts/Nets (Basketball, Volleyball, Hockey, Football); Safety Gear (Hockey Goalkeeper, Cricket Batsman/Wicketkeeper).

**5. Officiating and Fair Play:**
    *   Referees, umpires, and table officials monitor the game, enforce rules, and ensure fairness. Fouls and rule infringements result in penalties.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Indigenous Games:** This chapter highlights **Kabaddi** (7.6) and **Kho-Kho** (7.7) as popular indigenous games of India, detailing their history, rules, and skills. Their inclusion reflects India's unique sporting heritage. The formation of the **Amateur Kabaddi Federation of India (AKFI)** in 1972 and the inclusion of Kabaddi in the Asian Games (winning Gold in 1990) showcases national and international recognition. Kho-Kho's origins are traced back to Maharashtra ('Rathera'), with rule standardization efforts starting in Pune and Baroda in the early 20th century.
*   **Introduction & Development of International Sports:**
    *   **Basketball:** Introduced in India by the YMCA (Kolkata ~80 years ago, Chennai 1920). The **Basketball Federation of India (BFI)** was established in 1950 to promote the sport nationally (7.2.1). The first National event was held in Delhi (1934).
    *   **Cricket:** Brought by the British. The **Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI)** (referred to as Indian Cricket Control Board in text) formed in 1928. India played its first official Test in 1932. **Prince Ranjit Singh** ("Father of Indian Cricket") is honored through the **Ranji Trophy**, a premier domestic championship (7.3.1). The **Indian Premier League (IPL)** represents a major modern development (7.3.1).
    *   **Football:** Introduced during British rule. The first recorded game was in Calcutta (1854). The **Indian Football Association (IFA)** was established in Kolkata (1893), later succeeded by the **All India Football Federation (AIFF)** which joined FIFA in 1948. The period 1951-1962 is considered the "golden era" of Indian football, with **Asian Games gold medals (1951, 1962)** and a 4th place finish at the **1956 Melbourne Olympics** (7.4).
    *   **Hockey:** Introduced by British servicemen, with the first club in Kolkata (1885). India achieved remarkable international success, winning **Olympic gold medals** consecutively from 1928-1956, and again in 1964 and 1980 (7.5).
*   **National Governing Bodies:** The text mentions key organizations responsible for governing and promoting these sports within India: BFI (Basketball), BCCI (Cricket - implied), AIFF (Football), AKFI (Kabaddi). These bodies organize national championships and manage India's participation in international events.
