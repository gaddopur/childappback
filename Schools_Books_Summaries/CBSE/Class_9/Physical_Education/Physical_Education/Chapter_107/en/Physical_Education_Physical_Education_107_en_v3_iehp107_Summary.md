## 📘 Key Learnings

This section provides detailed explanations of the rules, gameplay, and skills for each team sport, aligning with NCERT content.

**1. Basketball**
*   **History:** Originated by Dr. James Naismith (USA, 1891). Introduced in India (Kolkata YMCA ~80 years ago), developed by Chennai YMCA (1920). Governed by BFI (1950).
*   **Gameplay:** 5 players per team on court (12 total). Played in 4 quarters (10 min each). Objective: Score by shooting the ball through the opponent's basket. Game starts with a jumpball. Ball moved by dribbling/passing. Fouls result in free throws or loss of possession.
*   **Court & Equipment:** Rectangular court (28x15m, Fig. 7.1), basket (Fig. 7.2), ball, uniforms, high-top sneakers.
*   **Skills:** Player Stance (Fig. 7.3), Ball Handling, Dribbling (Stationary, Cross-over, Reverse, Behind-the-back), Passing (Chest, Overhead, Bounce etc. - Fig. 7.4), Shooting (Set shot, Lay-up, Jump shot etc. - Fig. 7.5), Rebounding (Fig. 7.6), Individual Defence (Fig. 7.7). Requires neuromuscular coordination.

**2. Cricket**
*   **History:** Evolved gradually (Hambeldon Club ~1750), MCC, ICC (1909). ODI (1971), World Cup (1975), T20/IPL (21st Century). Introduced in India by British. BCCI (1928). India's first Test (1932). Ranjit Singh ('Father of Indian Cricket'), Ranji Trophy.
*   **Gameplay:** 11 players per team. Teams alternate batting and fielding over innings. Batting team scores runs by hitting the ball and running between wickets. Fielding team aims to dismiss batsmen (bowled, caught, etc.). Overs consist of 6 balls bowled by one bowler.
*   **Field & Equipment:** Circular/oval field with a central rectangular pitch (20.12m x 3.05m, Fig. 7.11). Equipment: Leather Ball (155.9-163g), Wooden Bat (max 10.8cm wide, 96.5cm long), Wickets (3 stumps, 2 bails). Fielding positions shown in Fig. 7.10.
*   **Officials:** Two on-field umpires, Third umpire.

**3. Football**
*   **History:** Ancient forms existed (China). Modern game governed by FIFA (1904). Introduced in India by British. IFA (Kolkata, 1893), AIFF. Golden Era (1951-62) with Asian Games wins and Olympic semi-final appearance (1956).
*   **Gameplay:** 11 players per team (including goalkeeper). Match duration: 90 mins (two 45-min halves). Objective: Score by kicking/heading the ball into opponent's goal. Starts with kick-off. Rules cover fouls (direct/indirect free kicks), penalty kicks, throw-ins, corner kicks. Yellow/Red cards for cautions/send-offs.
*   **Field & Equipment:** Rectangular field (Fig. 7.12), Goalposts, Ball (Leather, 68-70cm circumference, 410-450g).
*   **Skills:** Dribbling (Fig. 7.13), Heading (Fig. 7.14), Kicking (Fig. 7.15), Tackling (Fig. 7.16), Passing (Fig. 7.17).

**4. Hockey**
*   **History:** Introduced in India by British servicemen. First club (Kolkata, 1885). Governed by FIH (1924). India dominated Olympics (1928-1956, 1964, 1980). Artificial turf introduced (1976 Olympics).
*   **Gameplay:** 11 players per team. Duration: Two 35-min halves. Objective: Score by hitting the ball into the goal from within the striking circle using the flat side of the stick. Rules cover fouls (obstruction, dangerous play), penalty corners, penalty strokes, free hits, push-ins. Golden goal rule in extra time.
*   **Field & Equipment:** Rectangular field (Fig. 7.19) with striking circles, 23m lines. Equipment: Stick (Fig. 7.18), Ball (156-163g), Goalkeeper protective gear (helmet, pads etc.).
*   **Skills:** Hitting, Stopping, Dribbling (Fig. 7.21), Scoop (Aerial pass), Overhead Flick, Push (Fig. 7.22).

**5. Kabaddi**
*   **History:** Indigenous Indian game. Rules framed (Maharashtra, 1921). AKFI (1972). Included in Asian Games (1990).
*   **Gameplay:** 7 players per team on court (12 total). Duration: Two 20-min halves. Objective: Raider enters opponent court chanting "Kabaddi" (Cant) to tag defenders and return safely. Defenders aim to stop the raider. Points scored for tagging opponents out or successfully stopping a raider. Rules cover Cant, Out conditions, Holding, Time-outs, Bauck Line.
*   **Court:** Rectangular, soft surface (13m x 10m for men).
*   **Skills:** Raiding (Feint, Leg Raids, Hand Touch - Fig. 7.24, Foot Touch - Fig. 7.25, Kicking), Defensive (Catch/Hold - Fig. 7.23, Blocking). Requires agility, power, breath-holding.

**6. Kho-Kho**
*   **History:** Indigenous Indian game, modified 'Run and Chase' (Rathera). Rules framed (Pune, 1914), published (Baroda, 1924).
*   **Gameplay:** 9 players per team on field (12 total). Teams alternate chasing and running. 8 chasers sit in squares in the central lane, 1 active chaser. Runners enter in batches of 3. Chaser gives "Kho" (Fig. 7.28) to a teammate to activate them. Rules cover direction of movement, crossing central lane, pole turn (Fig. 7.27), fouls. Match has two innings, each with two 9-min turns.
*   **Field & Equipment:** Rectangular field (29m x 16m, Fig. 7.26) with central lane, squares, free zones, posts.
*   **Skills:** Chasing (Giving Kho, Pole Dive - Fig. 7.29, Trapping, Diving), Getting Up (Monkey Crawl, Standing), Sitting (Toe/Parallel Toe Method), Running (Chain, Double Chain).

**7. Volleyball**
*   **History:** Invented by William G. Morgan (USA, 1895). Introduced in Olympics (1964).
*   **Gameplay:** 6 players per team on court (12 total). Objective: Score points by grounding the ball on the opponent's court, or if opponent commits a fault (e.g., >3 touches, touching net). Played in sets (usually best of 5). First 4 sets to 25 points (lead of 2), 5th set to 15 (lead of 2). Players rotate clockwise (Fig. 7.31 concept) after winning the right to serve.
*   **Court & Equipment:** Rectangular court (18x9m, Fig. 7.30) divided by a net (Height: 2.43m Men, 2.24m Women). Equipment: Ball (65-67cm circumference, 260-280g), Net, Posts, Antennae.
*   **Skills:** Service (Underhand - Fig. 7.31, Overhead), Passing (Underhand/Bump, Overhead/Set), Spike/Smash (Fig. 7.32), Blocking.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

Understanding the Indian roots and journey of these team games.

*   **Indigenous Games:** Kabaddi and Kho-Kho are highlighted as popular indigenous games of India, with historical roots in Maharashtra ('Rathera' for Kho-Kho) and early rule codification within India.
*   **British Influence:** Cricket, Football, and Hockey were largely introduced and popularized in India during the British era. The first Hockey club (1885) and Football Association (IFA, 1893) were established in Kolkata.
*   **Governing Bodies:** India established national federations to govern these sports:
    *   Basketball Federation of India (BFI) - 1950
    *   Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) - 1928
    *   All India Football Federation (AIFF) - Replaced IFA
    *   Amateur Kabaddi Federation of India (AKFI) - 1972
    *   (Hockey and Kho-Kho national bodies implied through context)
*   **National Tournaments & Leagues:** The Ranji Trophy (Cricket) is a prestigious national championship. The Indian Premier League (IPL) represents a modern, high-impact T20 cricket league.
*   **International Achievements:**
    *   **Cricket:** India played its first official Test match in 1932.
    *   **Football:** India experienced a 'Golden Era' (1951-62), winning Asian Games gold medals (1951, 1962) and reaching the Olympic semi-finals (1956).
    *   **Hockey:** India achieved remarkable Olympic success, winning gold medals consecutively from 1928-1956, and again in 1964 and 1980.
    *   **Kabaddi:** India won the first Kabaddi gold medal when it was included as a discipline in the Asian Games (Beijing 1990).
*   **Pioneers:** Prince Ranjit Singh is acknowledged as the 'Father of Indian Cricket', despite playing only in England.
*   **Early Introduction:** Basketball was introduced via YMCA institutions in Kolkata and Chennai.
