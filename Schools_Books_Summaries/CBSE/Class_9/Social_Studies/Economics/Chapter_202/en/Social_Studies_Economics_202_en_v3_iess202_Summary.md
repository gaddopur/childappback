# [Class 9] Economics - Chapter 202: People as Resource

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter introduces the concept of **population as an asset** for the economy, termed **Human Resource**. Investment in this resource leads to **Human Capital Formation**, enhancing national productivity.

📊 **Concept Hierarchy:**

1.  **Human Capital:**
    *   **Definition:** Population viewed as a resource ('People as Resource'); the stock of skill and productive knowledge embodied in people. It's an asset, not a liability.
    *   **Formation:** Achieved through investments in:
        *   Education
        *   Training
        *   Medical Care (Health)
    *   **Significance:**
        *   Contributes to Gross National Product (GNP).
        *   Yields returns (higher income, productivity) like physical capital.
        *   Superior to other resources (land, physical capital) as it activates them.
        *   Drives development even with scarce natural resources (e.g., Japan).
        *   Key factor in India's Green Revolution (knowledge) and IT Revolution (skills).
    *   **Cycles:**
        *   *Virtuous Cycle:* Educated/healthy parents invest more in their children's human capital.
        *   *Vicious Cycle:* Disadvantaged parents (uneducated, poor health/hygiene) may perpetuate disadvantages in their children.

2.  **Economic Activities:**
    *   **Classification (Sectors):**
        *   *Primary Sector:* Agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry, fishing, mining, quarrying.
        *   *Secondary Sector:* Manufacturing.
        *   *Tertiary Sector:* Trade, transport, communication, banking, education, health, tourism, services, insurance.
    *   **Types:**
        *   *Market Activities:* Performed for pay or profit (production of goods/services, government service).
        *   *Non-Market Activities:* Production for self-consumption (e.g., processing primary products, own-account production).
    *   **Gender Dimension:**
        *   Historical/cultural division of labour (women often in domestic, unpaid work).
        *   Unpaid household work by women is not recognized in National Income.
        *   Women in the labour market often face lower pay (due to lower education/skill, discrimination) and less job security, especially in the unorganised sector. Educated/skilled women may achieve parity.

3.  **Quality of Population:**
    *   **Determinants:**
        *   Literacy Rate
        *   Health (indicated by Life Expectancy)
        *   Skill Formation
    *   **Significance:** Ultimately determines the country's growth rate. Literate and healthy population is an asset.
    *   **Education:**
        *   Enhances individual earnings and opportunities.
        *   Contributes to societal growth, national income, cultural richness, governance efficiency.
        *   Government Initiatives (India): Sarva Siksha Abhiyan (Universal Elementary Education), Navodaya Vidyalayas, Vocational streams, Mid-day Meal Scheme.
    *   **Health:**
        *   Essential for realizing potential and productivity.
        *   National policy focus on accessible healthcare, family welfare, nutrition (especially for underprivileged).
        *   Indicators: Life Expectancy, Infant Mortality Rate (IMR), Birth Rates, Death Rates.

4.  **Unemployment:**
    *   **Definition:** People willing to work at the going wage rates cannot find jobs. (Applies to workforce population, typically 15-59 years).
    *   **Types in India:**
        *   *Seasonal Unemployment:* Joblessness during certain months, common in agriculture.
        *   *Disguised Unemployment:* More people employed than needed; marginal productivity is zero. Common in agriculture (family farms).
        *   *Educated Unemployment:* Unemployment among matriculates, graduates, post-graduates. A paradoxical situation with surplus in some categories and shortage in others.
    *   **Consequences:**
        *   Wastage of manpower resource (asset becomes liability).
        *   Feeling of hopelessness among youth.
        *   Economic overload (increased dependency).
        *   Adverse impact on quality of life and health status.
        *   Indicator of a depressed economy.
    *   **Indian Context:** Low official unemployment rate masks issues of low income/productivity (underemployment). Self-employment in the primary sector often involves disguised unemployment. Migration from rural areas occurs in search of jobs.

## 📘 Key Learnings

*   **People as a Productive Asset:** Investing in education, health, and training transforms the population from a potential liability into valuable **human capital**. This capital is crucial for economic growth as it enhances productivity and enables the effective use of other resources like land and physical capital.
    *   *Example:* Sakal's education led to a skilled job and higher productivity, benefiting himself and the firm. Vilas, lacking education and health care, remained in low-paid, unskilled work like his mother.
    *   *Flow:* `Investment (Education, Health) ➡️ Enhanced Skills & Knowledge ➡️ Higher Productivity ➡️ Higher Income & Contribution to GNP` 📈

*   **Economic Activities & Sectors:** Human activities that add value to the national income are classified into three sectors:
    *   **Primary:** Extraction-based (e.g., farming, mining).
    *   **Secondary:** Manufacturing/Processing (e.g., factory production).
    *   **Tertiary:** Services (e.g., banking, teaching, IT services).
    *   Understanding **Market** (paid) vs. **Non-Market** (self-consumption) activities helps analyze economic contributions, including the often-unaccounted domestic work predominantly done by women (like Sakal's mother, Sheela).

*   **Quality of Population Matters:** The growth prospects of a nation depend significantly on the **quality of its population**, primarily determined by literacy levels, health status, and acquired skills.
    *   **Education:** It broadens opportunities, enhances earnings, and fosters societal progress. India aims for universal elementary education through schemes like **Sarva Siksha Abhiyan (SSA)** and improves quality through schools like **Navodaya Vidyalayas**. The **Mid-day Meal Scheme** aims to improve enrolment, retention, and nutrition. 📚
    *   **Health:** Good health allows individuals to reach their potential and contributes to productivity. India focuses on improving healthcare accessibility and nutrition, leading to increased **life expectancy** and reduced **Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)**. 🏥
    *   *Link:* `Improved Literacy & Health ➡️ Higher Quality Population ➡️ Accelerated National Growth`

*   **Understanding Unemployment:** Unemployment represents a significant waste of human resources.
    *   **Seasonal** and **Disguised** unemployment are prevalent in India's rural/agricultural sector.
    *   **Educated unemployment** is a growing concern in urban areas, indicating a mismatch between skills acquired and job market demands, leading to social waste.
    *   Unemployment negatively impacts individuals, families (poverty, health), and the overall economy (dependency, slower growth).

*   **Virtuous vs. Vicious Cycles:** Parental investment in human capital creates cycles. Educated and healthy parents are more likely to invest in their children's education and health, creating a **virtuous cycle** of development. Conversely, lack of education and poor health can lead to a **vicious cycle**, perpetuating disadvantage across generations.

*   **National Examples of Human Capital Impact:**
    *   **India's Green Revolution:** Demonstrated how greater knowledge (improved technology) dramatically increased agricultural productivity. 🇮🇳🌾
    *   **India's IT Revolution:** Highlights the rising importance of human capital (skills, knowledge) over physical capital (material, machinery). 🇮🇳💻

    *   **Literacy:** Increased from 18% (1951) to ~85% (2018). Significant disparities exist: Kerala (~94%) vs Bihar (~62%) (2011 Census); Male literacy ~16.1% higher than female; Urban ~14.2% higher than rural. 📊

    *   **Health Status:** Life expectancy increased to over 67.2 years (2021). Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) decreased significantly from 147 (1951) to 28 (2020). Crude Birth Rate dropped to 20.0 (2020), Death Rate to 6.0 (2020). 🏥
    *   **Health Infrastructure:** Significant expansion in Sub-centres, PHCs, CHCs, hospital beds, doctors, and nursing personnel over the decades (Ref: Table 2.2). However, challenges like regional disparities remain (e.g., concentration of medical colleges in a few states).
    *   **Unemployment:** High prevalence of seasonal and disguised unemployment in agriculture. Growing educated unemployment in urban areas. The employment structure is dominated by self-employment in the primary sector.

*   **Government Policies & Initiatives:**
    *   **Education:** Sarva Siksha Abhiyan (SSA), Mid-day Meal Scheme, Navodaya Vidyalayas, development of vocational streams.
    *   **Health:** National Health Policy aims to improve accessibility, focusing on underprivileged segments; development of health infrastructure across primary, secondary, tertiary sectors.
