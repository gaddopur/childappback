# [Class 9] Geography - Chapter 103 (Drainage)

## 🌟 Core Concepts

**1. Drainage Basics:**
    *   **Drainage:** The river system of an area.
    *   **Drainage Basin:** The area drained by a single river system.
    *   **Water Divide:** An elevated area (mountain/upland) separating two drainage basins (e.g., Ambala separates Indus and Ganga systems).

**2. Drainage Systems in India:**
    *   **Basis of Classification:** Broad relief features.
    *   **Major Groups:**
        *   The Himalayan Rivers
        *   The Peninsular Rivers

**3. Himalayan Rivers:**
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Origin: Northern mountains (including north of ranges for Indus & Brahmaputra).
        *   Nature: Mostly Perennial (water from rain and snowmelt).
        *   Course: Long, from source to sea.
        *   Features: Intensive erosion in upper course (gorges - e.g., Indus, Brahmaputra), carry silt/sand, form meanders, oxbow lakes, floodplains, well-developed deltas in middle/lower courses.
    *   **Major River Systems:**
        *   Indus River System
        *   Ganga River System
        *   Brahmaputra River System

**4. Peninsular Rivers:**
    *   **Characteristics:**
        *   Origin: Peninsular plateau (Western Ghats, Central Highlands).
        *   Nature: Mostly Seasonal (dependent on rainfall), reduced flow in dry season.
        *   Course: Shorter and shallower than Himalayan rivers.
        *   Features: Form deltas (east-flowing) or estuaries (west-flowing).
    *   **Major Rivers:**
        *   West Flowing (into Arabian Sea): Narmada, Tapi (long rivers in rift valleys), Sabarmati, Mahi, Bharathpuzha, Periyar (shorter coastal).
        *   East Flowing (into Bay of Bengal): Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri (major rivers), Damodar, Brahmani, Baitarni, Subarnarekha (smaller rivers).

**5. Lakes:**
    *   **Definition:** Water bodies, varying in size and characteristics.
    *   **Types based on Permanence:** Permanent, Seasonal (e.g., inland drainage basins in semi-arid regions).
    *   **Types based on Formation:**
        *   Glacial origin (e.g., most Himalayan freshwater lakes).
        *   Tectonic activity (e.g., Wular Lake).
        *   River action (e.g., Ox-bow lakes from meanders).
        *   Wind action.
        *   Coastal processes (Lagoons formed by spits/bars - e.g., Chilika, Pulicat, Kolleru).
        *   Human activities (Damming rivers for hydel power - e.g., Guru Gobind Sagar).
    *   **Types based on Water:** Freshwater (e.g., Wular, Dal, Bhimtal), Saltwater (e.g., Sambhar).
    *   **Importance:** Tourism, regulate river flow (prevent floods, maintain dry season flow), hydel power generation, moderate climate, support aquatic ecosystems, recreation.

**6. Role of Rivers in the Economy:**
    *   Fundamental importance throughout history (settlements, cities).
    *   Basic natural resource for human activities.
    *   Uses: Irrigation, navigation, hydro-power generation.
    *   Crucial for agriculture-based economies like India.

**7. River Pollution:**
    *   **Causes:**
        *   Growing demand (domestic, municipal, industrial, agricultural) reduces river volume.
        *   Discharge of untreated sewage and industrial effluents.
    *   **Effects:**
        *   Deterioration of water quality.
        *   Impacts river's self-cleansing capacity.
        *   Threatens human health and aquatic life.
    *   **Conservation Efforts:**
        *   River Action Plans (e.g., Ganga Action Plan - GAP, 1985).
        *   National River Conservation Plan (NRCP, 1995).
        *   Namami Gange Programme (2014).
        *   State-level initiatives (e.g., Namami Devi Narmade - Madhya Pradesh).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Drainage Patterns:**
A river system and the area it drains (drainage basin) are fundamental geographical concepts. Elevated lands called 'water divides' separate these basins. India's diverse relief features dictate its drainage patterns.

**2. Himalayan vs. Peninsular Rivers: A Comparison**

| Feature         | Himalayan Rivers                     | Peninsular Rivers                       |
| :-------------- | :----------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------- |
| **Origin**      | Himalayas (Snowfields, Glaciers)     | Peninsular Plateau, Central Highlands |
| **Nature**      | Perennial (Snowmelt + Rain)          | Seasonal (Mostly Rain-fed)            |
| **Course**      | Long, Extensive                      | Shorter, Shallower                      |
| **Drainage Basin**| Large                                | Comparatively Smaller                 |
| **Erosion**     | High in upper course                 | Less intense erosion                  |
| **Features**    | Gorges, V-shaped valleys, Rapids (Upper); Meanders, Oxbows, Floodplains, Deltas (Lower) | Rift Valleys (Narmada, Tapi), Estuaries (West-flowing), Deltas (East-flowing) |
| **Examples**    | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra            | Narmada, Tapi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri |

**3. Major River Systems of India:**

*   **The Indus System:** Rises near Lake Mansarovar (Tibet), flows west into Ladakh (India), forms gorges. Tributaries: Zaskar, Nubra, Shyok, Hunza (Kashmir); Satluj, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, Jhelum (join Indus near Mithankot, Pakistan). Total length ~2900 km. Drains into the Arabian Sea. *Indus Water Treaty (1960)* allows India to use 20% of its water.
*   **The Ganga System:** Headwaters 'Bhagirathi' (Gangotri Glacier) + 'Alaknanda' meet at Devaprayag. Enters plains at Haridwar. Length > 2500 km.
    *   *Himalayan Tributaries:* Yamuna (Yamunotri Glacier, joins at Allahabad), Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi (from Nepal, cause floods).
    *   *Peninsular Tributaries:* Chambal, Betwa, Son (from semi-arid areas, less water).
    *   Flows eastwards, bifurcates at Farakka (West Bengal). Bhagirathi-Hooghly (distributary) flows south to Bay of Bengal. Mainstream enters Bangladesh (joined by Brahmaputra), known as Meghna, forms Sundarban Delta (world's largest) with Brahmaputra. *Ambala* is on the water divide between Indus and Ganga. *Namami Gange* is a key conservation program.
*   **The Brahmaputra System:** Rises near Mansarovar Lake (Tibet, called Tsang Po - less water/silt here). Slightly longer than Indus. Enters India in Arunachal Pradesh (called Dihang) via a gorge near Namcha Barwa ('U' turn). Joined by Dibang, Lohit etc. to form Brahmaputra in Assam. Carries large volume of water and silt in India (high rainfall region). Forms braided channels, riverine islands (e.g., Majuli - world's largest). Prone to flooding and channel shifting in Assam/Bangladesh. Known as Jamuna in Bangladesh.

**4. Key Peninsular Rivers:**

*   **Narmada:** Rises in Amarkantak hills (MP), flows west through a rift valley. Forms Marble Rocks gorge (Jabalpur) and Dhuadhar falls. Short tributaries join at right angles. Basin covers MP, Gujarat. Drains into Arabian Sea (forms estuary). *Namami Devi Narmade* conservation scheme exists.
*   **Tapi:** Rises in Satpura ranges (Betul, MP), flows west parallel to Narmada in a rift valley. Shorter than Narmada. Basin covers MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra. Drains into Arabian Sea (forms estuary).
*   **Godavari:** Largest Peninsular river (~1500 km). Rises in Western Ghats (Nasik, Maharashtra). Largest drainage basin among Peninsular rivers (covers Maharashtra, MP, Odisha, AP). Known as 'Dakshin Ganga'. Tributaries: Purna, Wardha, Pranhita, Manjra, Wainganga, Penganga. Drains into Bay of Bengal (forms delta).
*   **Mahanadi:** Rises in highlands of Chhattisgarh (~860 km). Flows through Odisha. Basin shared by Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha. Drains into Bay of Bengal (forms delta).
*   **Krishna:** Rises near Mahabaleshwar (Western Ghats, ~1400 km). Tributaries: Tungabhadra, Koyana, Ghatprabha, Musi, Bhima. Basin shared by Maharashtra, Karnataka, AP. Drains into Bay of Bengal (forms delta).
*   **Kaveri:** Rises in Brahmagiri range (Western Ghats, ~760 km). Tributaries: Amravati, Bhavani, Hemavati, Kabini. Basin drains parts of Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu. Forms Shivasamudram Falls (2nd biggest in India, hydro-power source). Drains into Bay of Bengal (forms delta).

**5. Lakes and Their Significance:**
India has numerous lakes, differing in origin (glacial, tectonic, riverine, coastal lagoons, man-made reservoirs) and type (freshwater, saltwater).
*   **Examples:** Wular (largest freshwater, J&K, tectonic), Dal (J&K), Bhimtal, Nainital, Loktak (Manipur), Barapani (Meghalaya) - Freshwater; Sambhar (Rajasthan, saltwater, salt production), Chilika (Odisha, lagoon), Pulicat (AP/TN, lagoon), Kolleru (AP, lagoon); Guru Gobind Sagar (artificial, Bhakra Nangal).
*   **Value:** Regulate river flow, prevent floods, provide water during dry seasons, generate hydel power, moderate climate, support aquatic life, enhance natural beauty, promote tourism and recreation.

**6. Economic and Environmental Importance:**
Rivers are lifelines, crucial for India's agriculture (irrigation), transport (navigation), energy (hydro-power), and historical settlements. However, increasing demand and pollution from various sources (domestic sewage, industrial effluents) degrade water quality and river ecosystems, necessitating conservation efforts like NRCP and Namami Gange.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

India's drainage system is intrinsically linked to its unique physiography (Himalayas, Peninsular Plateau).
*   **Major Rivers:** The Ganga, Brahmaputra, Indus, Godavari ('Dakshin Ganga'), Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada, and Tapi are central to India's geography, culture, and economy.
*   **Specific Features:** The Sundarban Delta (world's largest, formed by Ganga-Brahmaputra), Majuli (world's largest riverine island, Brahmaputra), rift valley rivers (Narmada, Tapi), significant waterfalls (Shivasamudram on Kaveri).
*   **Water Management & Treaties:** The Indus Water Treaty (1960) governs water sharing with Pakistan. Numerous dams like Bhakra Nangal (Guru Gobind Sagar lake) are crucial for power and irrigation.
*   **Conservation Initiatives:** National programs like the Ganga Action Plan (GAP), National River Conservation Plan (NRCP), and the flagship 'Namami Gange' program highlight efforts to address river pollution. State initiatives like 'Namami Devi Narmade' (MP) also exist.
*   **Economic Dependence:** Rivers are vital for agriculture (major livelihood source), supporting irrigation across vast plains (e.g., Punjab, Haryana using Indus system water). Hydro-power generation is also significant.
*   **Cultural Significance:** Rivers like the Ganga hold immense cultural and religious importance. Major cities have historically developed along riverbanks.
*   **Data Points:** Ganga length (>2500 km), Indus length (~2900 km), Godavari length (~1500 km). India utilizes 20% of Indus system water under the treaty. 71% world surface is water, but only a fraction is usable freshwater.
