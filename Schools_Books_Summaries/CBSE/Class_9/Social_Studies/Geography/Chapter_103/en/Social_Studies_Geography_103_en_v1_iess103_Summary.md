# [Class 9] Geography - Chapter 3: Drainage

## 🌟 Core Concepts

**1. Drainage Basics:**
    *   **Drainage:** The river system of an area.
    *   **Drainage Basin:** The area drained by a single river system.
    *   **Water Divide:** An elevated area (mountain/upland) separating two drainage basins (See Figure 3.1).

**2. Drainage Systems in India:**
    *   **Basis of Classification:** Broad relief features.
    *   **Major Groups:**
        *   The Himalayan Rivers
        *   The Peninsular Rivers

**3. River Characteristics & Features:**
    *   **Perennial Rivers:** Have water throughout the year (fed by rain and snowmelt, typical of Himalayan rivers).
    *   **Seasonal Rivers:** Flow depends on rainfall (typical of Peninsular rivers).
    *   **River Course Stages:**
        *   *Upper Course:* Intensive erosion, gorges (See Figure 3.2), transport of silt/sand.
        *   *Middle Course:* Formation of meanders, oxbow lakes.
        *   *Lower Course:* Depositional features, floodplains, deltas (See Figure 3.3).
    *   **Estuary:** Formed by west-flowing Peninsular rivers like Narmada and Tapi where they meet the sea.
    *   **Delta:** Formed by east-flowing rivers (Ganga, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) at their mouth.
    *   **Tributary:** A stream or river flowing into a larger river.
    *   **Distributary:** A stream that branches off and flows away from a main river channel (e.g., Bhagirathi-Hooghly).
    *   **River System:** A river along with its tributaries.

**4. Major River Systems:**
    *   **Himalayan Rivers:**
        *   Indus River System
        *   Ganga River System
        *   Brahmaputra River System
    *   **Peninsular Rivers:**
        *   Narmada Basin
        *   Tapi Basin
        *   Godavari Basin
        *   Mahanadi Basin
        *   Krishna Basin
        *   Kaveri Basin
        *   Other West-flowing (Sabarmati, Mahi, etc.) & East-flowing (Damodar, Subarnarekha, etc.) rivers.

**5. Lakes:**
    *   **Types:**
        *   *Natural:* Formed by glaciers, tectonic activity, river action (oxbow lakes), wind action, coastal processes (lagoons).
        *   *Artificial/Human-made:* Formed by damming rivers for hydropower.
        *   *Freshwater:* Mostly in the Himalayas (e.g., Wular, Dal, Bhimtal).
        *   *Saltwater:* (e.g., Sambhar, Chilika, Pulicat).
    *   **Importance:** Regulate river flow, prevent flooding, hydropower, moderate climate, support aquatic ecosystems, tourism, recreation.

**6. Rivers and Economy:**
    *   Fundamental importance: Basic natural resource.
    *   Uses: Irrigation, navigation, hydro-power generation, water supply for settlements.
    *   Significance for India: Agriculture depends heavily on rivers.

**7. River Pollution:**
    *   **Causes:** Growing domestic, municipal, industrial, agricultural demand; discharge of untreated sewage and industrial effluents.
    *   **Effects:** Reduced water volume, deterioration of water quality, loss of self-cleansing capacity, impact on human health and aquatic life.
    *   **Conservation Efforts:** Ganga Action Plan (GAP), National River Conservation Plan (NRCP), Namami Gange Programme.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Understanding Drainage:**
The term 'drainage' refers to the network of rivers in a specific region. Small streams converge to form a main river, which eventually flows into a large water body like a lake or sea. The entire land area drained by this single river and its tributaries is known as a **drainage basin**. Elevated landforms like mountains or uplands act as a **water divide**, separating adjacent drainage basins, as illustrated in Figure 3.1 where an upland separates Stream A and Stream B.

**2. India's Drainage Systems: Himalayan vs. Peninsular:**
Indian rivers are broadly categorized based on their origin:

*   **Himalayan Rivers:**
    *   *Origin:* Himalayan mountains (e.g., Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra originate north of the ranges).
    *   *Nature:* Mostly **perennial**, receiving water from both rainfall and snowmelt.
    *   *Course:* Long courses from source to sea.
    *   *Features:* Perform intense erosion in upper courses (forming **gorges** like the one shown in Figure 3.2), carry large silt loads, form **meanders**, **oxbow lakes**, and well-developed **deltas** in middle and lower courses (See Figure 3.3).
*   **Peninsular Rivers:**
    *   *Origin:* Peninsular plateau and central highlands (most originate in Western Ghats).
    *   *Nature:* Mostly **seasonal**, dependent on monsoon rainfall; reduced flow in dry seasons.
    *   *Course:* Shorter and shallower courses compared to Himalayan rivers.
    *   *Features:* Most flow eastwards towards the Bay of Bengal forming **deltas**. Exceptions like Narmada and Tapi flow west through **rift valleys** and form **estuaries**. Their drainage basins are comparatively smaller.

**3. Major Himalayan River Systems:**

*   **The Indus River System:**
    *   *Origin:* Tibet, near Lake Mansarowar.
    *   *Course:* Flows west into India (Ladakh), forms gorges, joined by Zaskar, Nubra, Shyok, Hunza. Flows through Baltistan, Gilgit, emerges at Attock. Joined by Satluj, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, Jhelum near Mithankot (Pakistan). Drains into the Arabian Sea.
    *   *Length:* ~2900 km.
    *   *Basin in India:* Ladakh, J&K, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab.
    *   *Note:* As per the **Indus Water Treaty (1960)**, India uses 20% of its water.
*   **The Ganga River System:**
    *   *Origin:* Headwaters 'Bhagirathi' from Gangotri Glacier, joined by Alaknanda at Devaprayag (Figure 3.5) to form the Ganga. Emerges onto plains at Haridwar.
    *   *Tributaries:* Himalayan (Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi) and Peninsular (Chambal, Betwa, Son). Yamuna joins at Allahabad.
    *   *Course:* Flows eastwards till Farakka (West Bengal), bifurcates into Bhagirathi-Hooghly (distributary) and the main stream which enters Bangladesh (known as Meghna downstream after joining Brahmaputra).
    *   *Length:* Over 2500 km.
    *   *Delta:* Forms the **Sundarban Delta** (world's largest) with Brahmaputra.
    *   *Basin:* Largest river basin in India.
    *   *Note:* Ambala is on the water divide between Indus and Ganga systems. The **Namami Gange Programme** aims at its conservation.
*   **The Brahmaputra River System:**
    *   *Origin:* Tibet, east of Mansarowar lake (near Indus/Satluj sources). Known as Tsang Po in Tibet.
    *   *Course:* Flows east parallel to Himalayas. Takes a 'U' turn at Namcha Barwa, enters India (Arunachal Pradesh) as Dihang. Joined by Dibang, Lohit etc. to form Brahmaputra in Assam. Enters Bangladesh (known as Jamuna).
    *   *Length:* Slightly longer than Indus, most course outside India.
    *   *Features:* Carries large water volume and silt in India (high rainfall region). Forms **braided channels** and many **riverine islands** (e.g., Majuli - world's largest). Prone to flooding and channel shifting in Assam/Bangladesh due to silt deposition.

**4. Major Peninsular River Systems:**

*   **Water Divide:** Western Ghats form the main water divide.
*   **East Flowing (forming Deltas):**
    *   **Mahanadi:** Rises in Chhattisgarh highlands, flows through Odisha. Length ~860 km. Basin: Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha.
    *   **Godavari:** *Largest Peninsular river* (~1500 km). Rises in Western Ghats (Nasik, Maharashtra). *Largest basin* among Peninsular rivers (covers parts of Maharashtra, MP, Odisha, AP). Known as **Dakshin Ganga**. Tributaries: Purna, Wardha, Pranhita, Manjra, Wainganga, Penganga.
    *   **Krishna:** Rises near Mahabaleshwar (Western Ghats). Length ~1400 km. Tributaries: Tungabhadra, Koyana, Ghatprabha, Musi, Bhima. Basin: Maharashtra, Karnataka, AP.
    *   **Kaveri:** Rises in Brahmagiri range (Western Ghats). Length ~760 km. Flows through Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu. Tributaries: Amravati, Bhavani, Hemavati, Kabini. Forms **Shivasamudram Falls** (second biggest in India).
*   **West Flowing (forming Estuaries):**
    *   **Narmada:** Rises in Amarkantak hills (MP). Flows west through a **rift valley**. Creates Marble Rocks (gorge near Jabalpur) and Dhuadhar falls. Short tributaries join at right angles. Basin: MP, Gujarat. Conservation: **Namami Devi Narmade** scheme (MP Govt).
    *   **Tapi:** Rises in Satpura ranges (Betul, MP). Flows west parallel to Narmada in a **rift valley**. Shorter than Narmada. Basin: MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra.
    *   *Other West Flowing:* Sabarmati, Mahi, Bharathpuzha, Periyar (short coastal rivers due to narrow coastal plains).

**5. Lakes in India:**
India hosts diverse lakes:
*   **Formation:** Glacial action (most Himalayan freshwater lakes like Dal, Bhimtal, Nainital), Tectonic activity (Wular Lake - largest freshwater lake in India), River action (Ox-bow lakes), Wind action (in arid regions), Coastal processes (Lagoons like Chilika, Pulicat, Kolleru), Human activities (damming rivers like Guru Gobind Sagar).
*   **Types:** Permanent vs. Seasonal (like Sambhar salt lake in Rajasthan, used for salt production). Freshwater vs. Saltwater.
*   **Significance:** Lakes like Dal attract tourism. They regulate river flow, aid hydropower, moderate climate, support ecosystems, and offer recreation. Loktak Lake (Figure 3.6) is another important freshwater lake.

**6. Economic Role of Rivers:**
Rivers are vital economic resources, crucial since ancient times for settlements (now big cities). They provide water for:
*   **Irrigation:** Essential for agriculture, India's primary livelihood source.
*   **Navigation:** Facilitating transport.
*   **Hydropower Generation:** Clean energy source.
*   **Domestic/Industrial Use:** Water supply.

**7. River Pollution and Conservation:**
*   **Problem:** Increasing demand (domestic, industrial, agricultural) reduces river volume. Discharge of untreated sewage and industrial effluents pollutes rivers, exceeding their natural self-cleansing capacity (e.g., Ganga struggles to assimilate pollution near large cities).
*   **Consequences:** Affects water quality, human health, and aquatic life.
*   **Solutions:** River cleaning action plans. India initiated the **Ganga Action Plan (GAP)** in 1985, expanded into the **National River Conservation Plan (NRCP)** in 1995 to improve water quality in major rivers. The **Namami Gange Programme** (2014) is a flagship mission for Ganga's rejuvenation.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Major River Basins:** The Ganga basin is the largest in India. The Godavari has the largest basin among Peninsular rivers.
*   **Water Sharing:** The **Indus Water Treaty (1960)** governs water sharing between India and Pakistan, allowing India to use 20% of the Indus system's water, primarily for irrigation in Punjab, Haryana, and Rajasthan.
*   **Geographical Features:** Rivers originate from major Indian physiographic divisions like the Himalayas and the Peninsular Plateau (Western Ghats, Central Highlands, Amarkantak Hills, Satpura Range). Features like the Sundarban Delta (world's largest), Majuli (world's largest riverine island), Shivasamudram Falls, Marble Rocks, and Dhuadhar Falls are significant Indian landmarks.
*   **Lakes:** India has significant natural lakes like Wular (largest freshwater, tectonic origin), Chilika (largest lagoon), Sambhar (saltwater), and numerous Himalayan glacial lakes. Artificial lakes like Guru Gobind Sagar are vital for water management.
*   **Economic Dependence:** Agriculture, the mainstay of India's economy, heavily relies on river water for irrigation. Rivers are also crucial for hydropower and navigation.
*   **Conservation Initiatives:** Government programs like the **Namami Gange Programme** (Ganga rejuvenation), **Namami Devi Narmade** (Narmada conservation by MP Govt.), and the overarching **National River Conservation Plan (NRCP)** highlight national efforts to address river pollution and conservation.
*   **Cultural Significance:** Rivers like the Ganga hold immense cultural and religious significance in India. Settlements along riverbanks have grown into major cities throughout history.
