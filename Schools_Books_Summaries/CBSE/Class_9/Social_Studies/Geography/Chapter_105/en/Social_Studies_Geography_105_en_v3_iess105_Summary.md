## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter explores India's rich biodiversity, focusing on its natural vegetation and wildlife.

1.  **Introduction to India's Biodiversity**
    *   India as one of the 12 mega bio-diversity countries.
    *   Rank in plant diversity (10th globally, 4th in Asia).
    *   Statistics: ~47,000 plant species (~15,000 flowering), ~90,000 animal species.
    *   Definitions:
        *   **Natural Vegetation:** Plant community grown naturally without human aid, left undisturbed (Virgin Vegetation).
        *   **Flora:** Plants of a particular region/period.
        *   **Fauna:** Animal species of a particular region/period.
        *   **Endemic/Indigenous Species:** Purely Indian vegetation.
        *   **Exotic Species:** Plants from outside India.

2.  **Types of Natural Vegetation in India**
    *   Factors influencing vegetation (Rainfall, Temperature, Altitude).
    *   Major Types:
        *   Tropical Evergreen Forests
        *   Tropical Deciduous Forests (Moist & Dry)
        *   Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs
        *   Montane Forests (Altitudinal Zonation)
        *   Mangrove Forests

3.  **Wildlife of India**
    *   Richness and Variety: Mammals, Birds (~2000 species, 13% world total), Fish (~2546 species, 12% world stock), Amphibians, Reptiles.
    *   Distribution based on Habitat (Forests, Arid regions, Mountains, Wetlands).
    *   Unique Features: Presence of both Tigers and Lions.

4.  **Conservation of Flora and Fauna**
    *   Importance of Biodiversity: Ecosystem balance, resource provision (food, medicine, draught power).
    *   Threats: Hunting, Pollution, Deforestation, Alien Species Introduction.
    *   Consequences: Endangerment (~1300 plant species), Extinction (20 plant species, some animals).
    *   Conservation Measures by Government:
        *   Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.
        *   Biosphere Reserves (18 established, 12 in World Network).
        *   National Parks (106), Wildlife Sanctuaries (573), Zoological Gardens.
        *   Specific Projects (Project Tiger, Project Rhino, etc.).
        *   Support to Botanical Gardens.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Types of Vegetation:**

*   **Tropical Evergreen Forests:**
    *   *Rainfall:* > 200 cm (short dry season).
    *   *Regions:* Western Ghats, Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, upper Assam, Tamil Nadu coast.
    *   *Characteristics:* Tall trees (up to 60m+), luxuriant, multi-layered structure (trees, shrubs, creepers), appear green year-round (no definite leaf-shedding time).
    *   *Flora:* Ebony, mahogany, rosewood, rubber, cinchona.
    *   *Fauna:* Elephant, monkey, lemur, deer, one-horned rhinoceros (Assam, West Bengal), birds, bats, sloth, scorpions, snails.
    *   *Diagrammatic Idea:* Dense, tall canopy with multiple layers beneath.

*   **Tropical Deciduous Forests (Monsoon Forests):**
    *   *Rainfall:* 70 cm - 200 cm. Most widespread in India.
    *   *Characteristics:* Trees shed leaves for 6-8 weeks in dry summer.
    *   *(a) Moist Deciduous:*
        *   *Rainfall:* 100 cm - 200 cm.
        *   *Regions:* Eastern India (NE states, foothills of Himalayas, Jharkhand, West Odisha, Chhattisgarh), eastern slopes of Western Ghats.
        *   *Flora:* Teak (dominant), bamboos, sal, shisham, sandalwood, khair, kusum, arjun, mulberry.
    *   *(b) Dry Deciduous:*
        *   *Rainfall:* 70 cm - 100 cm.
        *   *Regions:* Rainier parts of Peninsular plateau, plains of Bihar & Uttar Pradesh.
        *   *Characteristics:* Open stretches, large areas cleared for cultivation/grazing.
        *   *Flora:* Teak, sal, peepal, neem.
    *   *Fauna (Deciduous overall):* Lion, tiger, pig, deer, elephant, variety of birds, lizards, snakes, tortoises.
    *   *Diagrammatic Idea:* Less dense than evergreen, trees visibly shed leaves seasonally.

*   **Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs:**
    *   *Rainfall:* < 70 cm.
    *   *Regions:* North-western India (semi-arid areas of Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana).
    *   *Characteristics:* Thorny trees and bushes, scattered trees, long roots, succulent stems, small/thick leaves (to minimize evaporation).
    *   *Flora:* Acacias, palms, euphorbias, cacti.
    *   *Fauna:* Rats, mice, rabbits, fox, wolf, tiger, lion, wild ass, horses, camels.
    *   *Diagrammatic Idea:* Sparse vegetation, thorny plants adapted to arid conditions.

*   **Montane Forests:**
    *   *Characteristics:* Vegetation changes with increasing altitude due to decreasing temperature. Succession mirrors tropical to tundra belts.
    *   *Altitudinal Zones:*
        *   *1000-2000m (Wet Temperate):* Evergreen broad-leaf trees (oaks, chestnuts).
        *   *1500-3000m (Temperate):* Coniferous trees (pine, deodar, silver fir, spruce, cedar). Found on southern Himalayan slopes, high altitude southern & NE India. Temperate grasslands also common at higher elevations.
        *   *>3600m (Alpine):* Stunted trees (silver fir, junipers, pines, birches) near snow-line, merging into Alpine grasslands (used by Gujjars, Bakarwals for grazing).
        *   *Highest Altitudes (Tundra):* Mosses and lichens.
    *   *Fauna:* Kashmir stag, spotted deer, wild sheep, jack rabbit, Tibetan antelope, yak, snow leopard, squirrels, Shaggy horn wild ibex, bear, rare red panda, sheep, goats (with thick hair).
    *   *Diagrammatic Idea:* Layered vegetation zones changing vertically up a mountain slope.

*   **Mangrove Forests (Tidal Forests):**
    *   *Regions:* Coastal areas influenced by tides (deltas of Ganga, Mahanadi, Krishna, Godavari, Kaveri).
    *   *Characteristics:* Mud and silt accumulation, dense mangroves with plant roots submerged under water (adapted to saline conditions).
    *   *Flora:* Sundari trees (Ganga-Brahmaputra delta - provide durable hard timber), palm, coconut, keora, agar.
    *   *Fauna:* Royal Bengal Tiger (famous), turtles, crocodiles, gharials, snakes.
    *   *Diagrammatic Idea:* Trees with specialized root systems (like pneumatophores) growing in coastal mudflats/water.

**2. Wildlife Distribution and Importance:**

*   India hosts diverse fauna adapted to varied habitats.
*   **Examples:** Elephants (hot wet forests - Assam, Karnataka, Kerala), One-horned Rhinos (swampy lands - Assam, West Bengal), Wild Ass & Camels (arid Rann of Kachchh & Thar), Indian Bison, Nilgai, Chousingha, Deer (various forests), Lion (Gir Forest, Gujarat - only natural habitat), Tiger (MP, Sundarbans, Himalayas), Leopard, Monkeys.
*   **Himalayan Fauna:** Hardy animals adapted to cold (Yak, Tibetan antelope, Bharal, Wild sheep, Kiang, Ibex, Snow Leopard, Red Panda).
*   **Aquatic Fauna:** Turtles, Crocodiles, Gharials (unique variety) in rivers, lakes, coasts. Birds are abundant and colourful (Peacock, Pheasant, Ducks, Cranes etc.).
*   **Importance:** Every species plays a role in the ecosystem. Animals provide draught power, transport, food (meat, eggs, milk, fish), insects aid pollination. Biodiversity is crucial for ecological balance and human survival (source of food crops, medicines).

**3. Conservation Needs and Efforts:**

*   Human activities (hunting, pollution, deforestation) have disturbed the ecosystem, leading to endangerment and extinction.
*   Conservation is essential for maintaining ecological balance and ensuring resource availability for the future.
*   Government initiatives like the Wildlife Protection Act, establishment of protected areas (Biosphere Reserves, National Parks, Sanctuaries), and specific species conservation projects (Project Tiger) are crucial steps.
