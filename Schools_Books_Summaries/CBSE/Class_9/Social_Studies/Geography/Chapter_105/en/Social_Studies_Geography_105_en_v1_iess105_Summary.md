# [Class 9] Geography - Chapter 5: Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

## 🌟 Core Concepts

1.  **Introduction to India's Biodiversity**
    *   India as one of the 12 mega bio-diversity countries.
    *   Flora Diversity: ~47,000 plant species (10th globally, 4th in Asia); ~15,000 flowering plants (6% of world's total); Non-flowering plants (ferns, algae, fungi).
    *   Fauna Diversity: ~90,000 animal species; ~2,000 bird species (13% of world's total); ~2,546 fish species (12% of world's stock); 5-8% of world's amphibians, reptiles, and mammals.
    *   Key Terms:
        *   **Natural Vegetation:** Plant community grown naturally without human aid and left undisturbed.
        *   **Virgin Vegetation:** Natural vegetation undisturbed by humans.
            *   **Endemic/Indigenous Species:** Purely Indian vegetation.
            *   **Exotic Species:** Plants from outside India.
        *   **Flora:** Plants of a particular region or period.
        *   **Fauna:** Animals of a particular region or period.

2.  **Factors Influencing Vegetation**
    *   Relief (Land, Soil) - *Implicit, but influences where types grow.*
    *   Climate:
        *   Temperature
        *   Photoperiod (Sunlight)
        *   **Precipitation (Rainfall):** Key differentiator for major vegetation types in India.

3.  **Types of Vegetation in India**
    *   **Tropical Evergreen Forests:**
        *   Characteristics: >200 cm rainfall, short dry season, warm & wet climate, tall trees (up to 60m+), multi-layered structure, no definite leaf-shedding time (appear green year-round).
        *   Location: Western Ghats, Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, upper Assam, Tamil Nadu coast.
        *   Flora: Ebony, mahogany, rosewood, rubber, cinchona.
        *   Fauna: Elephant, monkey, lemur, deer, one-horned rhinoceros (Assam, West Bengal), birds, bats, sloth, scorpions, snails.
    *   **Tropical Deciduous Forests (Monsoon Forests):**
        *   Characteristics: 70-200 cm rainfall, shed leaves for 6-8 weeks in dry summer.
        *   Sub-types:
            *   **Moist Deciduous:** 100-200 cm rainfall. Locations: Eastern India (NE states, foothills of Himalayas, Jharkhand, West Odisha, Chhattisgarh, eastern slopes of Western Ghats). Flora: Teak (dominant), bamboos, sal, shisham, sandalwood, khair, kusum, arjun, mulberry.
            *   **Dry Deciduous:** 70-100 cm rainfall. Locations: Rainier parts of Peninsular plateau, plains of Bihar & Uttar Pradesh. Flora: Teak, sal, peepal, neem; open stretches, often cleared for cultivation/grazing.
        *   Fauna (Common): Lion, tiger, pig, deer, elephant, birds, lizards, snakes, tortoises.
    *   **Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs:**
        *   Characteristics: <70 cm rainfall, thorny trees & bushes, scattered trees, long roots, succulent stems, thick/small leaves (minimise evaporation).
        *   Location: North-western India (semi-arid areas of Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana).
        *   Flora: Acacias, palms, euphorbias, cacti.
        *   Fauna: Rats, mice, rabbits, fox, wolf, tiger, lion, wild ass, horses, camels.
    *   **Montane Forests:**
        *   Characteristics: Vegetation changes with altitude due to decreasing temperature.
        *   Altitudinal Zones:
            *   1000-2000m: Wet temperate forests (Oaks, chestnuts).
            *   1500-3000m: Temperate forests (Coniferous: pine, deodar, silver fir, spruce, cedar). Locations: Southern slopes of Himalayas, high altitude southern & NE India.
            *   >3000m: Temperate grasslands.
            *   >3600m: Alpine vegetation (Silver fir, junipers, pines, birches), progressively stunted, merging into Alpine grasslands (used by Gujjars, Bakarwals), eventually Tundra vegetation (mosses, lichens) near snow-line.
        *   Fauna: Kashmir stag, spotted deer, wild sheep, jack rabbit, Tibetan antelope, yak, snow leopard, squirrels, shaggy horn wild ibex, bear, rare red panda, sheep, goats (with thick hair).
    *   **Mangrove Forests (Tidal Forests):**
        *   Characteristics: Found in coastal areas influenced by tides, dense mangroves with submerged roots, adapted to mud & silt accumulation.
        *   Location: Deltas of Ganga, Mahanadi, Krishna, Godavari, Kaveri.
        *   Flora: Sundari trees (Ganga-Brahmaputra delta - durable timber), palm, coconut, keora, agar.
        *   Fauna: Royal Bengal Tiger (famous), turtles, crocodiles, gharials, snakes.

4.  **Wildlife of India**
    *   Rich diversity (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, insects).
    *   Specific Habitats:
        *   Elephants: Hot wet forests (Assam, Karnataka, Kerala).
        *   One-horned Rhinoceros: Swampy/marshy lands (Assam, West Bengal).
        *   Wild Ass & Camels: Arid areas (Rann of Kachchh, Thar Desert).
        *   Lions: Gir Forest, Gujarat (only natural habitat in India).
        *   Tigers: Forests of Madhya Pradesh, Sundarbans (West Bengal), Himalayan region.
        *   Himalayan Fauna: Yak, Tibetan antelope, bharal, wild sheep, kiang, ibex, snow leopard, red panda (adapted to cold).
        *   Aquatic Fauna: Turtles, crocodiles, gharials (rivers, lakes, coasts).
    *   Importance: Ecological balance, pollination, biological control, source of food (fish), milch animals, draught power, transport, meat, eggs.

5.  **Conservation of Flora and Fauna**
    *   Threats: Hunting/poaching, pollution (chemical, industrial waste, acid deposits), alien species introduction, deforestation (for cultivation/habitation), habitat loss.
    *   Consequences: Ecosystem imbalance, ~1,300 plant species endangered, 20 extinct; many animal species endangered/extinct.
    *   Government Initiatives:
        *   **Wildlife Protection Act (1972).**
        *   **Biosphere Reserves:** 18 set up (e.g., Sundarbans, Nanda Devi, Nilgiri, Gulf of Mannar, Panna); 12 included in World Network.
        *   **National Parks (106), Wildlife Sanctuaries (573), Zoological Gardens.**
        *   **Project Tiger, Project Rhino, Project Great Indian Bustard,** etc.
        *   Financial/technical assistance to Botanical Gardens (since 1992).
    *   Need for Conservation: Essential for ecosystem stability and human survival.

6.  **Medicinal Plants**
    *   Traditional use in India (Ayurveda).
    *   Examples & Uses: Sarpagandha (blood pressure), Jamun (diabetes, digestion), Arjun (earache, blood pressure), Babool (eye sores, tonic), Neem (antibiotic), Tulsi (cough/cold), Kachnar (asthma, ulcers, digestion).
    *   Conservation Status: Many medicinal plants are threatened or endangered.

## 📘 Key Learnings

*   **India's Rich Biodiversity:** India holds a significant position globally in terms of plant and animal diversity due to varied climatic and relief features. Understand the scale (species numbers) and terminology (flora, fauna, endemic, exotic).
*   **Vegetation Types are Linked to Climate:** Rainfall is the primary determinant of the major forest types in India.
    *   **Evergreen:** High rainfall (>200 cm), no dry season, dense, multi-layered, always green. Found in Western Ghats, Islands, NE India. *Think:* Dense rainforests.
    *   **Deciduous:** Moderate rainfall (70-200 cm), distinct dry season, shed leaves. Most widespread type. Divided into Moist (100-200 cm) and Dry (70-100 cm). *Think:* Monsoon forests.
    *   **Thorn:** Low rainfall (<70 cm), arid/semi-arid conditions, drought-adapted plants (thorns, deep roots, small leaves). Found in NW India, rain-shadow areas. *Think:* Desert vegetation.
    *   **Montane:** Vegetation changes with altitude (temperature decrease). From broad-leaf temperate to coniferous, then alpine grasslands, finally tundra mosses/lichens. Found in Himalayas and high hills. *Think:* Mountain layers.
    *   **Mangrove:** Coastal, tidal areas, salt-tolerant trees with specialized roots. Found in river deltas. *Think:* Swamp forests by the sea.
*   **Wildlife Distribution:** Different animal species are adapted to specific vegetation zones and climatic conditions (e.g., Rhinos in swamps, Lions in Gir's dry deciduous/scrub, Snow Leopards in high Himalayas).
*   **Interdependence in Ecosystems:** Every species (plant or animal) plays a role. Biodiversity is crucial for ecological balance, pollination, food sources, and resources (medicinal plants, timber, animal products).
*   **Threats and Conservation Imperative:** Human activities (hunting, pollution, deforestation) severely threaten biodiversity, leading to endangered and extinct species. Conservation efforts (Protected Areas like National Parks, Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves; specific projects like Project Tiger; legislation like Wildlife Protection Act) are vital for preserving this natural heritage and ensuring ecological stability.
*   **Medicinal Plant Heritage:** India has a long tradition of using plants for medicine, but many of these valuable resources are now under threat.
