## 🌟 Core Concepts

**1. Significance of Population:**
    *   Humans as pivotal elements: Utilise resources, create society/culture.
    *   Resources & disasters gain meaning in relation to humans.
    *   Population characteristics (numbers, distribution, growth, qualities) form the basis for understanding the environment.
    *   Humans as producers and consumers of resources.

**2. Population Size and Distribution (India):**
    *   **Size:** Magnitude of population (e.g., India's 1.21 billion in 2011).
    *   **Distribution:** Spatial spread of population (uneven in India).
    *   **Census:** Official enumeration of population; primary source of demographic data in India.

**3. Population Density (India):**
    *   **Definition:** Number of persons per unit area (e.g., per sq km).
    *   Provides a clearer picture of distribution patterns.
    *   Factors influencing density: Terrain, climate, soil fertility, rainfall.

**4. Population Growth and Change:**
    *   **Growth:** Change in population size over time.
        *   Absolute Increase: Net number added.
        *   Growth Rate: Percentage change per annum.
    *   **Processes of Change:**
        *   Birth Rate: Number of live births per 1000 people/year.
        *   Death Rate: Number of deaths per 1000 people/year.
        *   Migration: Movement of people (Internal & International).

**5. Population Composition:**
    *   **Age Structure:** Distribution of population across different age groups (though not detailed in this text excerpt, adolescent population is discussed).
    *   **Adolescent Population:** Significant group (10-19 years) with specific needs.

**6. National Population Policy (NPP):**
    *   Government framework for population-related goals (e.g., NPP 2000).
    *   Focus on family planning, health, education, and welfare.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Why Study Population?**
   - People are central to social studies. They develop the economy and society, make and use resources, and are themselves resources.
   - Concepts like 'resource', 'calamity', or 'disaster' are meaningful only in relation to human beings. Coal is just rock until humans develop technology to use it; a flood becomes a disaster when it affects populated areas.
   - Understanding population numbers, distribution, growth, and characteristics is essential for appreciating all aspects of the environment.

**2. India's Population: Size, Distribution, and Density**
   - **Size:** As of March 2011, India's population was 1,210.6 million (1.21 billion), constituting over 17.5% of the world's population.
   - **Area:** India covers 3.28 million sq km, which is only 2.4% of the world's land area.
   - **Distribution:** Highly uneven.
      *   Uttar Pradesh (199 million in 2011) is the most populous state, holding about 16% of India's population.
      *   States like Sikkim (approx. 0.6 million) and UTs like Lakshadweep (approx. 64,429) have very small populations.
      *   Almost half of India's population resides in just five states: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Bihar, West Bengal, and Andhra Pradesh (as per 2011 data).
      *   Rajasthan, the largest state by area, has only 5.5% of the total population.
   - **Density:** Calculated as persons per sq km. India's average density was 382 persons/sq km in 2011, making it one of the most densely populated countries.
      *   Density varies significantly: from 1,102 persons/sq km in Bihar to only 17 persons/sq km in Arunachal Pradesh.
      *   **Factors Influencing Density:**
         *   *Low Density Areas (e.g., Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim):* Rugged terrain, unfavourable climatic conditions. (States below 250 persons/sq km often share these characteristics).
         *   *Moderate Density Areas (e.g., Assam, Peninsular States):* Hilly, dissected, rocky terrain, moderate/low rainfall, shallow/less fertile soils.
         *   *High Density Areas (e.g., Northern Plains like UP, Bihar, West Bengal; Kerala):* Flat plains, fertile soils, abundant rainfall.

      *   *Figure 6.2:* Pie chart showing the population share of major states, highlighting the concentration in states like Uttar Pradesh.

**3. Population Growth in India**
   - **Dynamic Nature:** Population numbers, distribution, and composition constantly change due to births, deaths, and migration.
   - **Measuring Growth:**
      *   *Absolute Increase:* The total number added over a period (e.g., population in 2011 minus population in 2001). India added 181.46 million people between 2001 and 2011.
      *   *Annual Growth Rate:* Percentage increase per year. A rate of 2% means an increase of 2 persons for every 100 people in the base population.
   - **India's Growth Trend:**
      *   *1951-1981:* Steadily increasing annual growth rate (peaking around 2.22% in 1981), leading to rapid population increase (361 million in 1951 to 683 million in 1981).
      *   *Since 1981:* Gradual decline in the *rate* of growth (1.64% between 2001-2011). This is a positive sign, often linked to declining birth rates.
      *   *Paradox:* Despite the declining growth *rate*, the *absolute* number of people added each decade remained very high (around 182 million in the 1990s and 2000s). This is because even a lower rate applied to a very large base population yields a large absolute increase.
      *   India overtook China in 2023 to become the world's most populous country.
   - **Diagrams:**
      *   *Table 6.1:* Shows total population, absolute increase per decade, and annual growth rate from 1951 to 2011.
      *   *Figure 6.4(a):* Bar graph illustrating the trend in annual population growth rates from 1951-2011, showing the peak around 1971-81 and subsequent decline.
      *   *Figure 6.4(b):* Line graph showing the steady increase in total population size from 1901 to 2011.

**4. Processes of Population Change**
   - **Birth Rate (BR):** Number of live births per 1000 persons in a year. Historically high BR in India is a major component of growth.
   - **Death Rate (DR):** Number of deaths per 1000 persons in a year. The rapid decline in DR has been the main driver of India's population growth rate.
   - **Natural Increase:** Difference between Birth Rate and Death Rate. Until 1980, high BR and declining DR led to high natural increase. Since 1981, declining BR has slowed the rate of natural increase.
   - **Migration:** Movement of people across regions/territories.
      *   *Internal Migration:* Movement within India (e.g., rural to urban). Does not change total population size but significantly impacts distribution and composition (age, sex) of population in different areas.
      *   *International Migration:* Movement between countries.
      *   *Rural-Urban Migration in India:* A major trend driven by:
         *   "Push" factors (rural): Poverty, lack of employment opportunities.
         *   "Pull" factors (urban): Increased employment prospects, better living conditions.
      *   *Impact:* Steady increase in the percentage of urban population (from 17.29% in 1951 to 31.80% in 2011). Growth in the number of 'million plus cities' (from 35 in 2001 to 53 in 2011, and 59 in 2023).

**5. Adolescent Population in India**
   - Constitutes about one-fifth (20%) of India's total population, typically aged 10-19 years.
   - Represents the nation's future human resource potential.
   - Have higher nutritional requirements than children or adults.
   - Challenges: Widespread inadequate nutrition, deficiency, stunted growth. Anaemia is a significant problem, especially among adolescent girls.
   - Need for focused attention through development processes, improved awareness via literacy and education.

**6. National Population Policy (NPP) 2000**
   - A comprehensive policy framework building upon India's Family Planning Programme (initiated in 1952).
   - Aims to promote responsible and planned parenthood on a voluntary basis, improving individual health and welfare.
   - **Key Objectives of NPP 2000:**
      *   Free and compulsory school education up to 14 years of age.
      *   Reducing Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) to below 30 per 1000 live births.
      *   Achieving universal immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
      *   Promoting delayed marriage for girls.
      *   Making family welfare a people-centred programme.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **Key Demographic Indicators (2011 Census):**
    *   Total Population: 1,210.6 million (1.21 billion).
    *   Global Share: Over 17.5% of world population on 2.4% of world area.
    *   Population Density: 382 persons per sq km (average).
    *   Most Populous State: Uttar Pradesh (199 million).
    *   Least Populous State: Sikkim (approx. 0.6 million).
    *   Least Populous UT: Lakshadweep (64,429).
    *   Recent Milestone: India surpassed China to become the world's most populous nation in 2023 (Source: UN).
*   **Migration Trends:** Significant internal migration, particularly from rural to urban areas, driven by economic factors ('push' from rural poverty/unemployment, 'pull' of urban opportunities).
    *   Urbanisation: Urban population share increased from 17.29% (1951) to 31.80% (2011).
    *   Growth of Cities: Number of 'million plus cities' grew from 35 (2001) to 53 (2011), reaching 59 by 2023.
*   **Adolescent Population:** A large cohort (approx. 1/5th of total population, aged 10-19) representing India's future workforce. Specific health challenges like undernutrition and anaemia among adolescent girls are national concerns.
*   **National Population Policy (NPP) 2000:** India's specific policy framework addressing population issues with goals like achieving universal elementary education, reducing IMR below 30/1000, universal immunization, promoting delayed marriage for girls, and making family welfare people-centric. This reflects national efforts towards population stabilization and human development.
