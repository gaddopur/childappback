# [Class 9] Civics - Chapter 403: Electoral Politics

## 🌟 Core Concepts

**1. Democracy and Elections:**
    1.1. Need for Representative Democracy: Why direct rule is impractical in large societies.
    1.2. Elections as a Mechanism: For choosing representatives, forming governments, and ensuring accountability.
    1.3. Purpose of Elections: Allowing people to choose lawmakers, government leaders, and guiding policies.

**2. Democratic Elections - Minimum Conditions:**
    2.1. Universal Adult Franchise & Equal Value: One person, one vote, one value.
    2.2. Real Choice: Freedom for parties/candidates to contest; meaningful options for voters.
    2.3. Regularity: Elections held at fixed intervals.
    2.4. People's Preference Prevails: The candidate preferred by the majority wins.
    2.5. Free and Fair Conduct: Absence of fear, coercion, or malpractice.

**3. Political Competition:**
    3.1. Nature: Competition among parties and candidates.
    3.2. Demerits: Potential for disunity, factionalism, 'dirty tricks', short-term focus.
    3.3. Merits: Creates incentives for leaders/parties to serve the people; ensures accountability.

**4. India's Electoral System:**
    4.1. Election Types: General Elections, By-elections.
    4.2. Electoral Constituencies:
        4.2.1. Area-based representation (Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, Local Bodies - Wards).
        4.2.2. Principle of roughly equal population per constituency.
        4.2.3. Reserved Constituencies: For SC, ST (Lok Sabha/Assemblies), OBC, and Women (Local bodies, and now Parliament/Assemblies via Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023).
    4.3. Voters' List (Electoral Roll):
        4.3.1. Principle: Universal Adult Franchise (18+ years).
        4.3.2. Preparation and Revision: Ensuring accuracy and inclusion.
        4.3.3. EPIC (Election Photo Identity Card).
    4.4. Nomination of Candidates:
        4.4.1. Eligibility: Voter status, minimum age (25 years for Lok Sabha/Assembly).
        4.4.2. Party Nomination ('Ticket').
        4.4.3. Formalities: Nomination form, security deposit, legal declaration (criminal cases, assets/liabilities, education).
    4.5. Election Campaign:
        4.5.1. Purpose: Public discussion, informing voters.
        4.5.2. Duration: Typically two weeks between final candidate list and polling.
        4.5.3. Methods: Rallies, meetings, media outreach, slogans.
        4.5.4. Regulation: Election Law (spending limits, prohibitions) & Model Code of Conduct.
    4.6. Polling and Counting:
        4.6.1. Polling Day: Voting at polling booths.
        4.6.2. Voting Method: EVMs (Electronic Voting Machines).
        4.6.3. Counting Process: Secure storage of EVMs, counting under observation, declaration of results.

**5. Evaluating Indian Elections:**
    5.1. Independence of Election Commission (EC): Appointment, powers, autonomy.
    5.2. Popular Participation: Voter turnout trends, participation across social groups, belief in vote's efficacy.
    5.3. Acceptance of Outcome: Ruling parties often lose; acceptance of verdict by defeated parties.

**6. Challenges to Free and Fair Elections in India:**
    6.1. Money Power: Unfair advantage for rich candidates/parties.
    6.2. Criminalization: Candidates with criminal backgrounds contesting and winning.
    6.3. Dynastic Succession: Dominance of certain families in party politics.
    6.4. Lack of Meaningful Choice: Similarity between major parties.
    6.5. Disadvantage for Smaller Parties/Independents.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. Why Elections are Necessary:**
In large, modern democracies, direct rule by all citizens is impossible due to scale and complexity. Therefore, people rule through elected representatives. Elections provide the essential mechanism for citizens to:
*   **Choose** their representatives who will make laws.
*   **Select** the party/leaders who will form the government and take major decisions.
*   **Influence** the policies that guide the government.
*   **Change** representatives or the ruling party if they are dissatisfied with their performance (Accountability).
The Haryana Assembly Election of 1987, where Devi Lal's Lok Dal defeated the incumbent Congress party based on campaign promises (like loan waivers), illustrates how elections can lead to changes in government and policy based on popular dissatisfaction and choice.

**2. What Makes an Election Democratic?**
For an election to be truly democratic, it must satisfy these minimum conditions:
*   **Universal Suffrage with Equal Value:** Every adult citizen (18+ in India) must have the right to vote, and each vote must carry equal weight.
*   **Meaningful Choice:** There should be multiple political parties and candidates free to contest, offering voters genuine alternatives in policies and leadership.
*   **Regular Intervals:** Elections must be held periodically (e.g., every 5 years for Lok Sabha and State Assemblies in India) to allow people to reassess their choices.
*   **Majority Rule:** The candidate or party preferred by the people should actually get elected.
*   **Free and Fair Conduct:** The election process must be free from intimidation, fraud (rigging), or undue influence, allowing voters to express their preference freely.

**3. Political Competition: A Necessary Evil?**
Electoral competition, primarily among political parties and candidates, is inherent to elections.
*   **Demerits:** It can foster disunity, factionalism, negative campaigning ('mudslinging'), use of 'dirty tricks', and focus on short-term populist measures over long-term policies. It might deter some capable individuals from entering politics.
*   **Merits:** Despite its drawbacks, competition compels parties and leaders to be responsive to public demands. Knowing they can be voted out forces them to address issues people care about and perform better to retain power. It acts as an incentive mechanism for accountability, similar to how market competition forces businesses to serve customers well.

**4. The Indian Electoral System Explained:**
India follows a systematic process for conducting elections:
*   **Constituencies:** The country is divided into geographical areas for election purposes.
    *   **Lok Sabha:** 543 constituencies, each electing one Member of Parliament (MP). Each constituency should have a roughly equal population.
    *   **Vidhan Sabha (State Assembly):** Each state is divided into Assembly constituencies, electing Members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs).
    *   **Local Bodies:** Villages/towns are divided into wards for Panchayat/Municipal elections.
*   **Reserved Constituencies:** To ensure representation for weaker sections, some constituencies are reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC - 84 in Lok Sabha) and Scheduled Tribes (ST - 47 in Lok Sabha). Only persons from these communities can contest from reserved seats. Reservation also exists for Other Backward Classes (OBC) and women (one-third) in local bodies, and now constitutionally mandated for women in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies (*Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023*).
*   **Voters' List (Electoral Roll):** Prepared before elections, listing all eligible voters (citizens aged 18+). Based on the principle of Universal Adult Franchise. It is regularly revised. The Election Photo Identity Card (EPIC) is issued, though other identity proofs are also accepted for voting.
*   **Nomination:** Eligible citizens (voters aged 25+) can contest. Parties nominate candidates ('party ticket'). Candidates file a nomination form, pay a security deposit, and submit a legal declaration detailing assets, liabilities, criminal cases (if any), and educational qualifications.
*   **Election Campaign:** A period (usually two weeks) for candidates/parties to interact with voters, hold rallies, and present their manifestos. Regulated by:
    *   **Election Law:** Prohibits bribery, threats, appeals based on caste/religion, misuse of government resources, and sets spending limits (e.g., ₹25 lakh for Lok Sabha, ₹10 lakh for Assembly per constituency - *Note: These limits are periodically revised and may be higher now*).
    *   **Model Code of Conduct:** A consensus-based code preventing misuse of government machinery, places of worship for propaganda, and announcement of new schemes after elections are declared.
    *   **Slogans:** Parties use catchy slogans to capture public attention, e.g., *Garibi Hatao* (Congress, 1971), *Save Democracy* (Janata Party, 1977), *Land to the Tiller* (Left Front, 1977), *Protect the Self-Respect of the Telugus* (TDP, 1983).
*   **Polling & Counting:**
    *   **Polling:** Voters cast votes secretly at designated polling booths using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs). Voter's finger is marked with indelible ink. Party agents observe the process.
    *   **Counting:** EVMs are sealed and stored securely. On a fixed date, votes are counted constituency-wise under supervision. The candidate with the highest votes is declared elected.

**5. How Democratic are Indian Elections?**
Several factors suggest Indian elections are largely democratic:
*   **Independent Election Commission (EC):** Appointed by the President but operates independently, similar to the judiciary. The CEC is hard to remove. The EC has vast powers to conduct, supervise, and control elections, implement the Code of Conduct, punish violators, and even order repolls. It can issue guidelines to the government during election periods.
*   **Popular Participation:**
    *   Voter turnout in India has remained stable or increased over decades, unlike declines in many Western democracies (See Chart 1: India vs UK Turnout).
    *   Participation is higher among the poor, illiterate, and underprivileged sections compared to the affluent (See Chart 2: Turnout by Social Groups).
    *   A significant majority of Indians feel their vote matters and can influence governance (See Chart 3: Belief in Vote's Impact).
    *   Interest and participation in election-related activities have increased (See Chart 4: Participation in Activities).
*   **Acceptance of Electoral Outcomes:** The results are generally accepted as the 'people's verdict', even by the losing party. Ruling parties (both national and state) frequently lose elections, indicating that outcomes reflect popular will rather than manipulation by those in power. About half of sitting MPs/MLAs lose elections, showing anti-incumbency is common.

**6. Challenges Remaining:**
Despite strengths, Indian elections face significant challenges:
*   **Money Power:** Candidates/parties with substantial funds have an unfair advantage, potentially influencing voters or campaign reach.
*   **Criminalization:** Individuals with criminal backgrounds sometimes secure party tickets and win elections, often using muscle power or intimidation.
*   **Dynastic Politics:** Political parties are often dominated by a few families, with tickets distributed preferentially to relatives, limiting opportunities for ordinary citizens.
*   **Limited Choice:** Voters sometimes find little difference between the policies and practices of major competing parties.
*   **Disadvantage for Smaller Players:** Smaller parties and independent candidates struggle to compete effectively against larger, established parties due to resource constraints.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

*   **National Economic/Social Data 📊:**
    *   **Representation:** Lok Sabha has 543 constituencies. As of 2019, 84 seats are reserved for SCs and 47 for STs. The *Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023* reserves one-third of seats in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies for women. Reservations for OBCs and women exist in Panchayats and Municipalities.
    *   **Voter Participation:** India has the largest electorate globally. Turnout has been around 60-67% in recent Lok Sabha elections, showing high participation, especially among disadvantaged groups. A majority (67% in 2004 study) believe their vote makes a difference.
    *   **Election Expenditure:** Conducting elections is expensive (e.g., Govt spent ~₹3,500 crore in 2014 Lok Sabha polls, total expenditure including parties/candidates estimated much higher). This raises questions about affordability but needs context (e.g., compared to defence or infrastructure spending).
    *   **Accountability:** Ruling parties frequently lose elections at both central and state levels (roughly 2 out of 3 elections in the last 25 years saw ruling party lose). High turnover of elected representatives (incumbents losing).
    *   **Institutions:** The Election Commission of India (ECI) is a powerful, independent constitutional body overseeing the entire process. Introduction of EVMs and EPIC are key technological/administrative steps.
    *   **Specific Examples:** The 1987 Haryana election (Devi Lal), famous slogans like *Garibi Hatao* (1971), *Save Democracy* (1977), *Land to the Tiller* (1977), *Protect the Self-Respect of the Telugus* (1983) illustrate campaign dynamics. The EC's actions (e.g., reprimanding govt., ordering repolls) demonstrate its authority.
