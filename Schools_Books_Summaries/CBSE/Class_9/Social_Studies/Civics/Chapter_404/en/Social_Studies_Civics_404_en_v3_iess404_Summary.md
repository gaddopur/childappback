# [Class 9] Civics - Chapter 4: Working of Institutions

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter explores how democratic governments function through various institutions responsible for making, implementing, and interpreting laws and policies.

**📊 Concept Hierarchy:**

1.  **Government & Decision Making:**
    *   Need for Rules and Procedures in Democracy.
    *   Major Policy Decisions: How they are taken (using the Office Memorandum example).
2.  **Political Institutions:**
    *   **Definition:** Arrangements in modern democracies to attend to governmental tasks (making laws, implementing decisions, resolving disputes).
    *   **Necessity:** Ensure tasks are performed, provide continuity, allow consultation, prevent hasty decisions, uphold the spirit of democracy.
    *   **Constitutional Role:** Constitution lays down basic rules on powers and functions.
3.  **Key Institutions in India (National Level - Union Government):**
    *   **Legislature (Parliament):**
        *   Role: Supreme law-making body, controls government finances, forum for discussion/debate, holds government accountable.
        *   Houses: Lok Sabha (House of the People) & Rajya Sabha (Council of States).
        *   President: Part of Parliament (gives assent to laws).
    *   **Executive:**
        *   Role: Implements laws and policies, takes day-to-day decisions.
        *   Types:
            *   **Political Executive:** Elected leaders (PM, Council of Ministers) - take major decisions, answerable to people.
            *   **Permanent Executive (Civil Services):** Appointed officials (Civil Servants/Bureaucrats) - assist political executive, provide expertise, ensure continuity.
        *   Key Functionaries: Prime Minister (Head of Government), President (Head of State), Council of Ministers (Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State).
    *   **Judiciary:**
        *   Role: Settles disputes (citizens, citizen-government, state governments, union-state), interprets the Constitution, protects Fundamental Rights.
        *   Structure: Integrated system (Supreme Court, High Courts, District Courts).
        *   Independence: Crucial for democracy; ensures impartiality.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. How are Major Policy Decisions Taken? The Mandal Commission Example**

*   **The Decision:** The Government of India issued an Office Memorandum (O.M. No. 36012/31/90-Est (SCT)) on Aug 13, 1990, reserving 27% of vacancies in government jobs for Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBC).
*   **The Process (Chain of Events):**
    1.  **Commission:** Second Backward Classes Commission (Mandal Commission) appointed (1979), submitted report (1980) recommending 27% reservation.
    2.  **Discussion:** Report discussed in Parliament for years.
    3.  **Political Promise:** Janata Dal included implementation in its 1989 election manifesto.
    4.  **Government Formation:** Janata Dal formed government; V.P. Singh became PM.
    5.  **Formal Intention:** President announced government's intention in Parliament address.
    6.  **Cabinet Decision:** Union Cabinet took a formal decision (Aug 6, 1990).
    7.  **Parliament Informed:** PM informed both Houses of Parliament.
    8.  **Order Drafted:** Department of Personnel and Training drafted the O.M. based on Cabinet decision, got Minister's approval.
    9.  **Order Issued:** Joint Secretary signed and issued the O.M. (Aug 13, 1990).
*   **The Dispute & Resolution:**
    1.  **Controversy:** Widespread protests and counter-protests; debated hotly.
    2.  **Legal Challenge:** Cases filed in courts challenging the order's validity.
    3.  **Judicial Verdict:** Supreme Court (in 'Indira Sawhney & others Vs Union of India case', 1992) declared the order valid but asked for modification (exclude 'well-to-do'/creamy layer).
    4.  **Implementation:** Government issued a modified O.M. (Sep 8, 1993).

**2. The Need for Political Institutions**

*   Governments perform diverse functions: ensuring security, providing education/health, collecting taxes, implementing welfare schemes.
*   Institutions provide a framework:
    *   Assign responsibilities clearly.
    *   Ensure continuity even when leaders change.
    *   Involve rules, procedures, meetings which, though sometimes causing delays, allow wider consultation and prevent bad decisions from being rushed.
    *   Uphold the spirit of democracy over potentially autocratic single-person rule.

**3. Parliament: The Legislature**

*   **Why Parliament?** It's the supreme political authority representing the people.
    *   Final authority for making/changing/abolishing laws (Legislation).
    *   Exercises control over the government (Executive). In India, the government needs Parliament's (specifically Lok Sabha's) support.
    *   Controls government finances (Public money spent only with Parliament's sanction).
    *   Highest forum for discussion and debate on public issues and national policy.
*   **Two Houses (Bicameral Legislature):**
    *   **Lok Sabha (House of the People):** Directly elected by people. Exercises real power. More powerful in money matters and controlling the Council of Ministers. Has more members. Term: 5 years (can be dissolved).
    *   **Rajya Sabha (Council of States):** Mostly indirectly elected (by state MLAs). Represents interests of states. Permanent house (members have 6-year terms, 1/3rd retire every 2 years). Has special powers regarding states.
    *   **Relative Power:** Lok Sabha is more powerful due to its direct mandate, control over money bills, and ability to remove the government through a 'no-confidence' motion. In case of disagreement on ordinary laws, a joint session is held where Lok Sabha's view usually prevails due to its larger size.

**4. Political Executive: Government in Action**

*   **Executive:** Functionaries who take day-to-day decisions and implement policies (often referred to as 'the government').
*   **Political vs. Permanent Executive:**
    *   **Political:** Elected for a specific period (e.g., Prime Minister, Ministers). Take major policy decisions. Accountable to the people. Have the final say as they represent the people's will.
    *   **Permanent (Civil Services):** Appointed on a long-term basis (e.g., Secretaries, IAS officers). Assist the political executive, provide expertise and advice, implement decisions. Remain in office even if the government changes.
    *   **Power Dynamics:** Ministers (Political Executive) have more power than Civil Servants (Permanent Executive) because in a democracy, the will of the elected representatives (and thus the people) is supreme. Ministers decide the overall policy framework and objectives, taking expert advice but making the final call.
*   **Prime Minister (PM) and Council of Ministers:**
    *   **PM Appointment:** Appointed by the President; must be the leader of the majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha.
    *   **Council of Ministers Appointment:** Appointed by the President on the PM's advice. Usually MPs from the majority party/coalition.
    *   **Council Structure:**
        *   **Cabinet Ministers:** (~25) Top leaders in charge of major ministries. Form the 'inner ring'; decisions taken in Cabinet meetings are on behalf of the Council.
        *   **Ministers of State (Independent Charge):** In charge of smaller ministries; attend Cabinet meetings when invited.
        *   **Ministers of State:** Assist Cabinet Ministers.
    *   **Cabinet Form of Government:** Decisions are taken collectively in the Cabinet. Ministers must publicly support Cabinet decisions (Collective Responsibility).
    *   **PM's Powers:** Chairs Cabinet meetings, coordinates ministries, distributes work, dismisses ministers, supervises overall functioning. The PM is the most powerful figure within the Cabinet, especially if their party has a clear majority. Coalition politics can impose constraints.
*   **The President: Head of State**
    *   **Role:** Head of State (like the Queen of Britain); largely ceremonial functions. Supervises overall institutional functioning.
    *   **Election:** Indirectly elected by MPs and MLAs. Represents the entire nation but lacks a direct popular mandate like the PM.
    *   **Powers:** Exercises powers mostly on the advice of the Council of Ministers. All government activities, laws, major appointments (Chief Justice, Judges, Governors, Election Commissioners), treaties are in the President's name. Supreme Commander of Defence Forces.
    *   **Discretionary Power:** Key power is appointing the PM, especially when no party has a clear majority in Lok Sabha. Can ask the Council of Ministers to reconsider advice (but bound if advice is given again). Can delay assent to bills and send them back for reconsideration (but must sign if passed again).
    *   **Parliamentary vs. Presidential System:** India has a Parliamentary system where Parliament is supreme and the executive (PM/Cabinet) is drawn from and responsible to it. The US has a Presidential system where the President is directly elected, head of both state and government, and not dependent on legislative support.

**5. The Judiciary: Guardian of the Constitution**

*   **Role:** Resolves disputes, interprets the Constitution, acts as guardian of Fundamental Rights.
*   **Structure:** Integrated judiciary with the Supreme Court at the apex, controlling judicial administration. Decisions of higher courts are binding on lower courts.
    *   Supreme Court (for the nation)
    *   High Courts (in states)
    *   District Courts & Local Courts
*   **Independence:** Crucial for democracy. Judiciary is not under the control of the Legislature or Executive. Judges act impartially.
    *   **Appointment:** Judges of Supreme Court/High Courts appointed by President on advice of PM and in consultation with the Chief Justice (in practice, senior judges select new judges).
    *   **Removal:** Extremely difficult; requires impeachment by Parliament (never happened in India).
*   **Powers:**
    *   **Judicial Review:** Can declare laws (legislative) or actions (executive) invalid if found unconstitutional.
    *   **Guardian of Fundamental Rights:** Citizens can approach courts if rights are violated.
    *   **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** Anyone can approach the court if public interest is harmed by government actions. Courts can intervene against misuse of power and check malpractices.
    *   The Indian judiciary is considered very powerful and enjoys public confidence.
