# [Class 9] Civics - Chapter 4: Working of Institutions

## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter explores the crucial role of institutions in a democratic government, focusing on how major decisions are made, implemented, and disputes resolved within the framework of India's political system.

📊 **Concept Hierarchy:**

1.  **Need for Political Institutions:**
    *   To manage complex governmental tasks (security, welfare, economy).
    *   To establish rules, procedures, and responsibilities.
    *   To ensure continuity and stability beyond individual leaders.
    *   To facilitate consultation and prevent hasty/bad decisions.
    *   To uphold the spirit of democracy through checks and balances.

2.  **Major Policy Decision-Making Process:**
    *   Illustrated via the Mandal Commission Reservation Order (O.M. No. 36012/31/90-Est (SCT)).
    *   Involves multiple stages: Commission Report -> Political Deliberation/Promise -> Cabinet Decision -> Parliamentary Information -> Bureaucratic Drafting & Issuance (Office Memorandum) -> Public Debate/Protest -> Judicial Review -> Final Implementation (potentially modified).

3.  **Key Institutions of Indian Government (Union Level):**
    *   **Legislature (Parliament):**
        *   Role: Law-making (Legislation), controlling the executive, financial control (budget), forum for debate and discussion.
        *   Structure: Bicameral - Lok Sabha (House of the People) & Rajya Sabha (Council of States). President as integral part.
        *   Relative Powers: Lok Sabha's supremacy in money matters and government formation (confidence). Joint sessions for resolving deadlocks.
    *   **Executive:**
        *   Types: Political Executive (elected, policy decisions) vs. Permanent Executive (appointed, implementation, expertise).
        *   **Political Executive:**
            *   President: Head of State (nominal powers), ceremonial functions, appoints PM, gives assent to laws, discretionary powers in specific situations.
            *   Prime Minister: Head of Government (real powers), leader of majority party/coalition, chairs Cabinet, coordinates ministries, selects ministers.
            *   Council of Ministers: Includes Cabinet Ministers (core decision-making body), Ministers of State (Independent Charge), and Ministers of State. Principle of collective responsibility.
        *   **Permanent Executive (Civil Services/Bureaucracy):**
            *   Role: Implementing government policies, providing expert advice, day-to-day administration, ensuring continuity. Works under political executive control.
    *   **Judiciary:**
        *   Role: Interpreting the Constitution, resolving disputes (citizens, govt., states), safeguarding Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review (checking constitutionality of laws/actions).
        *   Structure: Integrated system - Supreme Court (apex), High Courts (states), District & Local Courts.
        *   Independence: Crucial for democracy; ensured through appointment process, security of tenure, separation from legislature/executive.

4.  **Inter-institutional Relations:**
    *   Checks and balances between Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary.
    *   Parliament controls the Executive through no-confidence motions and financial control.
    *   Executive (President) appoints Judiciary heads (with consultation).
    *   Judiciary can review actions of Legislature and Executive.

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. How are Major Policy Decisions Taken? The Mandal Commission Example**

*   **The Decision:** The Government of India issued an Office Memorandum (O.M.) on Aug 13, 1990, reserving 27% of vacancies in central government jobs for Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs).
*   **The Process:**
    *   **Background:** The Second Backward Classes Commission (Mandal Commission), appointed in 1979, recommended this reservation in its 1980 report.
    *   **Political Trigger:** The Janata Dal promised implementation in its 1989 election manifesto. After forming the government, PM V.P. Singh's cabinet decided to implement it (Aug 6, 1990).
    *   **Institutional Roles:**
        *   *Cabinet:* Took the formal decision.
        *   *Prime Minister:* Informed Parliament.
        *   *President:* Mentioned the government's intention earlier in a Parliamentary address.
        *   *Parliament:* Discussed the report over years, influencing the decision indirectly.
        *   *Civil Servants (Permanent Executive):* Drafted and issued the O.M. based on Cabinet instructions.
    *   **Conflict & Resolution:** Widespread protests erupted. The matter went to the Supreme Court ('Indira Sawhney & others Vs Union of India case').
    *   *Judiciary:* The Supreme Court (1992) upheld the reservation order as valid but asked the government to exclude the 'creamy layer' (well-to-do persons) among SEBCs. This led to a modified O.M. (Sept 8, 1993).
*   **Significance:** This case illustrates that major decisions involve a long chain of events, multiple institutions, political considerations, public reaction, and judicial intervention. It highlights the complexity and checks-and-balances inherent in the system. *(Diagram Idea: Flowchart showing the stages from Mandal Commission appointment to the final modified O.M.)*

**2. The Need for Political Institutions**

*   Governments perform diverse functions (security, education, health, taxation, welfare).
*   Institutions provide a framework:
    *   **Division of Labour:** Assigning specific tasks (e.g., law-making to Parliament, implementation to Executive).
    *   **Rules & Procedures:** Ensuring decisions are not arbitrary and processes are followed.
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** Providing mechanisms (like Judiciary) to settle disputes.
    *   **Stability & Continuity:** Ensuring governance continues even when leaders change.
    *   **Wider Consultation:** Allowing diverse views to be considered, preventing hasty or poor decisions, even if it causes delays.

**3. Parliament: The Supreme Legislative Body**

*   **Why Parliament?** It exercises supreme political authority on behalf of the people.
*   **Key Functions:**
    *   **Law Making (Legislation):** Final authority to make, change, or abolish laws.
    *   **Control over Executive:** The government (Council of Ministers) is responsible to the Lok Sabha. It can remain in power only if it enjoys majority support. Parliament scrutinizes government actions.
    *   **Financial Control:** Controls government finances; public money can be spent only with Parliament's sanction (budget approval).
    *   **Forum of Debate:** Highest forum for discussion on public issues and national policy.
*   **Two Houses:**
    *   **Lok Sabha (House of the People):** Directly elected by people; 543 elected members (+2 nominated Anglo-Indians, provision now removed); 5-year term (can be dissolved earlier). Exercises real power in most matters, especially financial bills and confidence in the government.
    *   **Rajya Sabha (Council of States):** Represents states; mostly indirectly elected by state MLAs; 238 elected + 12 nominated by President; Permanent house (members have 6-year terms, 1/3rd retire every 2 years). Has special powers regarding state matters.
*   **Relative Power:** Lok Sabha is more powerful due to its direct mandate, control over the Council of Ministers (no-confidence motion), and supremacy in money matters. In case of disagreement on ordinary laws, a joint session is held, where Lok Sabha's view usually prevails due to its larger size. *(Diagram Idea: Venn diagram showing unique and shared powers of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, including the President's role)*

**4. Political and Permanent Executive**

*   **Executive:** Implements laws and policies.
*   **Political Executive:** Elected leaders (PM, Ministers) who hold office for a specific term. They make major policy decisions.
*   **Permanent Executive (Civil Services/Bureaucracy):** Appointed officials (IAS, IPS, etc.) who remain in office long-term, regardless of ruling party changes. They assist the political executive, provide expertise, and handle day-to-day administration.
*   **Why Minister has Final Say:** In a democracy, the will of the people (represented by the elected minister) is supreme. The minister is answerable to the people. While civil servants provide expert advice, the minister decides the policy direction, considering the broader picture and political mandate. *(Diagram Idea: Chart contrasting Political and Permanent Executive on basis of Election/Appointment, Tenure, Role, Power)*

**5. Prime Minister and Council of Ministers**

*   **Prime Minister (PM):** The most important political institution; Head of Government.
    *   *Appointment:* Appointed by the President, who chooses the leader of the majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha.
    *   *Powers:* Chairs Cabinet meetings, coordinates ministries, distributes work, can dismiss ministers, leader of the ruling party/coalition. The government falls if the PM resigns.
    *   *Influence:* Power can vary based on personality and political context (e.g., single-party majority vs. coalition government). Coalition politics imposes constraints.
*   **Council of Ministers:** Official body of all ministers (60-80 members).
    *   *Appointment:* Appointed by President on PM's advice. Usually MPs. Non-MPs must become MPs within 6 months.
    *   *Ranks:*
        *   **Cabinet Ministers:** Top leaders, in charge of major ministries. Form the Cabinet (approx. 25 ministers), the core decision-making body.
        *   **Ministers of State (Independent Charge):** In charge of smaller ministries; attend Cabinet meetings when invited.
        *   **Ministers of State:** Assist Cabinet Ministers.
    *   *Collective Responsibility:* The Council works as a team; decisions are binding on all ministers. No minister can openly criticize a government decision. Supported by the Cabinet Secretariat (senior civil servants).

**6. The President: Head of State**

*   **Role:** Head of State, largely ceremonial functions (like the British Monarch). Supervises overall institutional functioning.
*   **Election:** Indirectly elected by MPs and MLAs. Represents the entire nation but lacks direct popular mandate like the PM.
*   **Powers (Nominal):**
    *   All governmental activities, laws, policy decisions, major appointments (CJI, Judges, Governors, Election Commissioners) are in the President's name.
    *   Supreme Commander of Defence Forces.
    *   Gives assent to bills passed by Parliament (can send back non-money bills for reconsideration once).
*   **Real vs. Nominal Power:** Exercises powers mostly on the advice of the Council of Ministers (headed by PM). Bound by reconsidered advice.
*   **Discretionary Power:** Key instance is appointing the PM when no party/coalition has a clear majority in Lok Sabha. Appoints someone likely to secure majority support and asks them to prove it.

**7. The Judiciary: Guardian of the Constitution**

*   **Role:** Settles disputes, interprets the Constitution, protects Fundamental Rights.
*   **Structure:** Integrated judiciary with Supreme Court at the apex, controlling judicial administration nationwide. Decisions binding on lower courts (High Courts, District Courts).
*   **Independence:** Crucial for its function. It's not under the control of Legislature or Executive.
    *   *Appointment:* Judges appointed by President on advice of PM and in consultation with Chief Justice of India (CJI). Practically, senior judges (collegium) play a key role in selection.
    *   *Removal:* Extremely difficult; requires impeachment motion passed by special majority in both Houses of Parliament.
    *   *Powers:*
        *   **Judicial Review:** Can declare laws (legislative) or actions (executive) invalid if found unconstitutional.
        *   **Guardian of Fundamental Rights:** Citizens can approach courts if rights are violated (Writs).
        *   **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** Allows anyone to approach court if public interest is harmed by government actions.
        *   Intervenes to prevent misuse of power and check malpractices.
*   **Significance:** A powerful and independent judiciary enjoys high public confidence and is essential for democracy. *(Diagram Idea: Pyramid structure showing the hierarchy of Indian courts)*
