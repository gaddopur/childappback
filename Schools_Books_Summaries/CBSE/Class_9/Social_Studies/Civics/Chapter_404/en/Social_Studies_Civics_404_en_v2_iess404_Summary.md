## 🌟 Core Concepts

This chapter explores the crucial role of institutions in a democracy, focusing on how major decisions are made, implemented, and disputed within the Indian governmental framework.

**📊 Concept Hierarchy:**

1.  **Democratic Governance:**
    *   Need for Institutions (Rules, Procedures, Stability)
    *   Functions: Security, Welfare (Education, Health), Taxation, Administration, Development.
2.  **Major Policy Decision-Making (Illustrated by Mandal Commission Example):**
    *   The Trigger: Need for Reservation for SEBC.
    *   The Process:
        *   Commission Appointment (Second Backward Classes Commission, 1979 - Mandal Commission).
        *   Report & Recommendations (1980 - 27% reservation).
        *   Political Deliberation (Parliamentary discussions, Party Manifestos - Janata Dal 1989).
        *   Government Formation & Intent (V.P. Singh Govt., President's Address).
        *   Formal Decision (Union Cabinet Approval).
        *   Announcement (PM's Statement in Parliament).
        *   Implementation Order (Office Memorandum - O.M. No. 36012/31/90-Est (SCT)).
    *   Public Reaction: Debate, Protests, Controversy.
    *   Dispute Resolution: Role of Judiciary (Supreme Court - 'Indira Sawhney vs Union of India' case, 1992 verdict, Modification order).
3.  **Key Institutions in Indian Democracy:**
    *   **Legislature (Parliament):**
        *   Role: Law-making (Legislation), Control over Government (Executive), Control over Finances, Forum for Discussion/Debate.
        *   Structure:
            *   President (Part of Parliament, gives assent to laws).
            *   Two Houses:
                *   **Lok Sabha (House of the People):** Directly elected, larger membership, supreme power in most matters (money bills, confidence motions, joint sessions).
                *   **Rajya Sabha (Council of States):** Indirectly elected (by MLAs), represents states, special powers over state matters, permanent house.
    *   **Executive:**
        *   Role: Implementing laws and policies, day-to-day administration.
        *   Types:
            *   **Political Executive:** Elected leaders, take major decisions (e.g., Prime Minister, Council of Ministers). Accountable to the people.
            *   **Permanent Executive (Civil Services):** Appointed officials, long-term basis, assist political executive, provide expertise (e.g., Secretaries, Civil Servants).
        *   Key Functionaries:
            *   **President:** Head of State, nominal powers, ceremonial role, appoints PM and other key officials, gives assent to laws, exercises discretion in appointing PM if no majority. Elected indirectly (MPs, MLAs).
            *   **Prime Minister (PM):** Head of Government, exercises real power, leader of majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha, chairs Cabinet, coordinates ministries, appoints/dismisses ministers. Appointed by President.
            *   **Council of Ministers:** Appointed by President on PM's advice. Includes:
                *   *Cabinet Ministers:* Top leaders, major ministries, core decision-making body.
                *   *Ministers of State (Independent Charge):* Smaller ministries.
                *   *Ministers of State:* Assist Cabinet Ministers.
            *   **Civil Servants:** Implement decisions, provide information/expertise.
    *   **Judiciary:**
        *   Role: Interpreting laws, resolving disputes (citizens, citizen-government, state governments, union-state), safeguarding Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review.
        *   Structure: Integrated system (Supreme Court, High Courts, District Courts).
        *   **Supreme Court:** Apex court, controls judicial administration, decisions binding on all courts, highest court of appeal.
        *   **Independence:** Crucial for democracy. Achieved through:
            *   Appointment Process (Judges select judges with minimal executive interference).
            *   Security of Tenure (Difficult removal process - impeachment).
            *   Powers (Judicial Review, Guardian of Fundamental Rights, Public Interest Litigation - PIL).

## 📘 Key Learnings

**1. How are Major Policy Decisions Taken? The Mandal Commission Example**

*   **The Decision:** The Government of India issued an Office Memorandum (O.M.) on Aug 13, 1990, reserving 27% of vacancies in government jobs for Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBC).
*   **The Background:** This decision was the result of a long process starting with the Second Backward Classes Commission (Mandal Commission) appointed in 1979. Its 1980 report recommended this reservation.
*   **The Political Process:** The recommendation was discussed in Parliament. The Janata Dal included its implementation in its 1989 election manifesto. After forming the government, PM V.P. Singh's cabinet formally decided to implement it (Aug 6, 1990). The PM informed Parliament, and the relevant department drafted and issued the O.M.
*   **The Conflict:** The decision led to widespread protests and court cases, challenging its validity.
*   **The Resolution:** The Supreme Court, in the 'Indira Sawhney & others Vs Union of India case' (1992), upheld the reservation but asked the government to exclude the 'well-to-do' (creamy layer) among the backward classes. A modified O.M. was issued in 1993.

**2. The Need for Political Institutions**

*   Governments perform complex tasks: ensuring security, providing education/health, collecting taxes, running welfare schemes.
*   Institutions (like Parliament, Executive, Judiciary) provide a framework with rules and procedures.
*   They ensure continuity even when individuals change.
*   They facilitate consultation with a wider set of people, preventing hasty or bad decisions, even if it causes delays. This is vital for the spirit of democracy.

**3. Parliament: The Supreme Law-Making Body**

*   **Functions:**
    *   **Legislative:** Final authority to make, change, or abolish laws.
    *   **Control over Executive:** The government (Council of Ministers) is responsible to and requires the support of Parliament (specifically Lok Sabha).
    *   **Financial Control:** Controls government finances; public money can only be spent with Parliament's sanction (budget approval).
    *   **Debate and Discussion:** Highest forum for discussing public issues and national policy.
*   **Two Houses:**
    *   **Lok Sabha:** Directly elected, represents the people. More powerful in money matters and in determining the government's survival (no-confidence motion). View likely prevails in joint sessions due to larger numbers.
    *   **Rajya Sabha:** Indirectly elected, represents states. Has special powers related to states but less power in financial matters and government formation. Can only delay money bills by 14 days.

**4. The Executive: Implementing Decisions**

*   **Political Executive (Ministers):** Elected, hold office for a specific term. Take major policy decisions. Accountable to the people. The Minister has the final say as they represent the people's will, even if civil servants have more technical expertise.
*   **Permanent Executive (Civil Servants):** Appointed, long-term career officials. Assist ministers, provide expertise, handle day-to-day administration. Implement decisions.
*   **Prime Minister (PM):** The real head of the government. Appointed by the President, must command a majority in Lok Sabha. Chairs cabinet, coordinates ministries, distributes work, can dismiss ministers. Power can be constrained in coalition governments.
*   **Council of Ministers:** Includes Cabinet Ministers (core decision-making body), Ministers of State (Independent Charge), and Ministers of State. Works as a team based on collective responsibility.
*   **President:** Head of State, largely ceremonial role (nominal executive). Appoints PM, judges, governors etc., on the advice of the Council of Ministers. Gives assent to bills. Can ask the Council to reconsider advice once. Exercises discretion when no party has a clear majority post-election.

**5. The Judiciary: Guardian of the Constitution**

*   **Role:** Settles disputes, interprets the Constitution, protects Fundamental Rights.
*   **Independence:** Crucial for its function. Ensured by:
    *   *Appointment:* Senior judges play a key role in selecting new judges.
    *   *Removal:* Very difficult process (impeachment by Parliament).
    *   *Powers:* Can review laws/executive actions (Judicial Review) and declare them unconstitutional if they violate the Constitution. Can issue directives (Public Interest Litigation - PIL).
*   **Integrated Structure:** Supreme Court at the apex, followed by High Courts and lower courts. Supreme Court decisions are binding on all.
