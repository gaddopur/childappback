# [Class 9] History - Chapter 302

## 🌟 Core Concepts

Here is a hierarchical breakdown of the core concepts discussed in this chapter:

1.  **The Age of Social Change (Post-French Revolution Context)**
    *   Spread of ideas: Freedom, Equality, Individual Rights, Control of Social Power.
    *   Emergence of New Political Groups:
        *   **Liberals:** Advocated for religious tolerance, individual rights against rulers, representative parliamentary government, property-based suffrage (not universal), opposed dynastic rule.
        *   **Radicals:** Wanted government based on majority population, supported women's suffrage, opposed privileges of landowners/factory owners, disliked wealth concentration (but not private property itself).
        *   **Conservatives:** Initially opposed change, later accepted gradual change while respecting the past.
    *   Influence of Industrial Revolution:
        *   New social/economic problems: Urbanisation, poor working conditions (long hours, low wages, unemployment), housing/sanitation issues.
        *   Rise of industrial capitalists (often Liberals/Radicals) believing in individual enterprise.
    *   Nationalism: Revolutionaries aiming to create 'nations' with equal rights (e.g., Mazzini in Italy).

2.  **The Rise of Socialism**
    *   Core Idea: Opposition to private property (seen as root of social ills), emphasis on collective social interests over personal gain.
    *   Visions of Socialist Society:
        *   **Cooperatives:** Proposed by Robert Owen (New Harmony) and Louis Blanc (government-encouraged).
        *   **Marxism (Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels):**
            *   Analysis of Industrial Society as 'Capitalist'.
            *   Theory of Exploitation: Profit generated by workers, accumulated by capitalists.
            *   Solution: Overthrow of capitalism and private property.
            *   Goal: A 'Communist' society with socially controlled property.
    *   Support and Organisation:
        *   Spread of socialist ideas by the 1870s.
        *   Formation of the **Second International** (international socialist body).
        *   Workers' associations (Trade Unions) demanding better conditions, reduced hours, voting rights.
        *   Formation of socialist parties (e.g., SPD in Germany, Labour Party in Britain, Socialist Party in France).

3.  **The Russian Empire Before 1917**
    *   Political Structure: **Tsarist Autocracy** (Tsar Nicholas II), no parliamentary subjection.
    *   Economy and Society:
        *   Predominantly **Agrarian** (85% population), major grain exporter.
        *   **Industrialisation** in pockets (St Petersburg, Moscow), growth in late 19th century (railways, foreign investment).
        *   **Social Divisions:**
            *   *Workers:* Divided by skill, village links; poor conditions (long hours, low wages - especially for women), poor housing; united for strikes.
            *   *Peasants:* Cultivated most land, but nobility/crown/Church owned large estates; deep religious beliefs but little respect for nobility; land hunger; unique **commune (mir)** system for periodic land pooling/redistribution.
    *   Socialism in Russia:
        *   Political parties illegal before 1914.
        *   **Russian Social Democratic Workers Party (RSDWP)** (founded 1898, Marxist, illegal operation).
        *   **Socialist Revolutionary Party** (formed 1900, focused on peasants' rights, land transfer).
        *   Debate within Socialists: Peasants vs. Workers as main revolutionary force.
        *   Split in RSDWP:
            *   **Bolsheviks** (led by Vladimir Lenin): Advocated for a disciplined party controlling member quality in a repressive society.
            *   **Mensheviks:** Favoured a party open to all.
        *   **Jadidists:** Muslim reformers seeking modernised Islam within the empire.

4.  **The Russian Revolution**
    *   **The 1905 Revolution:**
        *   Context: Autocracy, poor worker conditions (rising prices, falling real wages).
        *   Trigger: Dismissal of workers at Putilov Iron Works.
        *   **Bloody Sunday:** Peaceful procession attacked by police/Cossacks, sparking widespread strikes, university closures, demands for civil liberties and a constituent assembly.
        *   Outcome: Creation of an elected consultative Parliament (**Duma**), but Tsar curtailed its power, dismissed early Dumas, and restricted political activity.
    *   **Impact of World War I (1914-1917):**
        *   Initial patriotic support waned as Tsar refused to consult Duma.
        *   Heavy casualties ("Russian steam roller" suffered defeats), army demoralisation.
        *   Destruction of crops/buildings by retreating army, leading to refugees.
        *   Severe impact on industry (cut off from supplies, equipment breakdown, labour shortages).
        *   Food shortages in cities, bread riots by winter 1916.
    *   **The February Revolution (1917):**
        *   Setting: Grim conditions in Petrograd (food shortages, cold winter).
        *   Events: Lockout, strikes (led by women on International Women's Day), demonstrations, clashes, army mutiny (refusal to fire, joining workers).
        *   Formation of **Petrograd Soviet** (workers' and soldiers' council).
        *   Outcome: **Abdication of Tsar Nicholas II** (March 2nd), formation of **Provisional Government** by Soviet and Duma leaders.
    *   **Developments After February:**
        *   Provisional Government dominated by army officials, landowners, industrialists, liberals, socialists.
        *   Removal of restrictions on public meetings/associations; Soviets formed widely.
        *   Return of **Lenin** (April 1917); **April Theses** (end war, land to peasants, nationalise banks).
        *   Growing workers' movement, factory committees, trade unions, soldiers' committees.
        *   Provisional Government's power declined, Bolshevik influence grew; government repression (July Days).
        *   Peasants seized land (encouraged by Socialist Revolutionaries).
    *   **The October Revolution (1917):**
        *   Context: Conflict between Provisional Government and Bolsheviks; Lenin feared dictatorship.
        *   Planning: Lenin persuaded Petrograd Soviet/Bolshevik Party for socialist seizure of power; Military Revolutionary Committee (under Leon Trotskii) organised it.
        *   Events (Oct 24th): Uprising began, seizure of government buildings, arrest of ministers, shelling of Winter Palace (by ship Aurora), city control by Military Revolutionary Committee.
        *   Outcome: All Russian Congress of Soviets approved Bolshevik action; Bolsheviks controlled Moscow-Petrograd area by December.

5.  **Building the Socialist State (USSR)**
    *   **Immediate Post-October Changes:**
        *   Opposition to private property: **Nationalisation** of most industry and banks.
        *   Land declared social property; peasants allowed to seize nobility's land.
        *   Partition of large houses in cities.
        *   Ban on old aristocratic titles; new uniforms designed (e.g., Soviet hat - *budeonovka*).
        *   Bolshevik Party renamed **Russian Communist Party (Bolshevik)**.
        *   Dismissal of Constituent Assembly (Jan 1918) after failing to win majority.
        *   **Treaty of Brest Litovsk** (March 1918): Peace with Germany.
        *   Establishment of a **one-party state**; trade unions under party control; secret police (**Cheka**, later OGPU/NKVD) suppressed dissent.
        *   Initial artistic/cultural experimentation, later curtailed by censorship.
    *   **The Civil War (1918-1920):**
        *   Cause: Bolshevik land redistribution led to army breakup; opposition from non-Bolshevik socialists ('greens'), pro-Tsarists ('whites'), liberals.
        *   Conflict: Bolsheviks ('reds') vs. 'greens' and 'whites' (backed by French, American, British, Japanese troops).
        *   Characteristics: Looting, banditry, famine; harsh measures by 'whites' against peasants lost them support.
        *   Outcome: Bolshevik victory by Jan 1920, controlling most of former empire, aided by cooperation with non-Russian nationalities/Muslim jadidists (though tensions existed, e.g., Khiva massacre).
        *   Formation of **USSR (Soviet Union)** in Dec 1922, granting political autonomy to non-Russian nationalities (partially successful due to unpopular Bolshevik policies like discouraging nomadism).
    *   **Making a Socialist Society:**
        *   **Centralised Planning:** Introduced Five-Year Plans to set economic targets.
        *   **Industrial Growth:** Focus on heavy industry (oil, coal, steel production increased significantly); new factory cities (e.g., Magnitogorsk).
        *   **Social Consequences:** Rapid construction led to poor working conditions, hard lives for workers, housing issues.
        *   **Social Welfare Measures:** Extended schooling, university access for workers/peasants, factory crèches, cheap public healthcare, model living quarters (uneven implementation due to limited resources).
    *   **Stalinism and Collectivisation:**
        *   Context: Grain supply problems in late 1920s; Stalin believed rich peasants (**kulaks**) and traders were hoarding grain (speculation).
        *   Policy: Emergency measures (1928) - enforced grain collections, raids on kulaks. **Collectivisation** program launched (from 1929) - forced peasants onto collective farms (**kolkhoz**), state control over large farms, elimination of kulaks.
        *   Resistance: Peasants destroyed livestock; resistance met with severe punishment (deportation, exile).
        *   Consequences: Livestock numbers fell drastically; production did not increase immediately; devastating **famine (1930-1933)** killing over 4 million.
        *   Repression: Criticism within the Party suppressed; accusations of conspiracy against socialism; mass arrests, imprisonments in labour camps, executions (purges) by 1939 (affecting over 2 million).

6.  **Global Influence of the Russian Revolution and the USSR**
    *   Inspiration: Fired imagination worldwide, possibility of a workers' state.
    *   Spread of Communism: Formation of communist parties in many countries (e.g., Great Britain, India).
    *   **Comintern:** Bolshevik-founded international union of pro-Bolshevik socialist parties; encouraged colonial peoples.
    *   Education: USSR's Communist University of the Workers of the East trained non-Russians.
    *   Global Stature: USSR gave socialism a global face by WWII.
    *   Later Critiques: By 1950s, acknowledged that USSR government style deviated from revolutionary ideals; repression and denial of freedoms alongside economic development.
    *   Legacy: International reputation declined by late 20th century, but socialist ideals retained respect; ideas rethought globally.
    *   **Impact on India:** Inspired many Indians (attended Communist University); Communist Party of India formed (mid-1920s); figures like Jawaharlal Nehru and Rabindranath Tagore visited and wrote about Soviet Socialism; writings in various Indian languages.

## 📘 Key Learnings

This chapter traces the evolution of socialist thought in Europe and culminates in the Russian Revolution, a pivotal event that attempted to establish the world's first socialist state.

*   **Diverse Political Responses to Change:** Following the French Revolution, Europe saw varied responses to societal transformation.
    *   **Liberals** sought constitutional, representative government with limited suffrage (property owners), religious tolerance, and protection of individual rights, opposing unchecked monarchical power.
    *   **Radicals** pushed for majority-based government, supported universal suffrage (including women), and opposed aristocratic/capitalist privileges, though not private property itself.
    *   **Conservatives** gradually accepted the need for slow change while emphasizing tradition and respect for the past.
    *   *(Conceptual Diagram Idea: A table comparing Liberals, Radicals, and Conservatives on key issues like suffrage, property, role of government, pace of change).*

*   **Industrialisation and the Rise of Socialism:** The Industrial Revolution created vast wealth but also immense social problems (poor working conditions, inequality, urban squalor). These conditions fueled the search for alternatives.
    *   **Socialism** emerged as a critique of private property, arguing it led to exploitation and social ills. Socialists advocated for collective or social control of property for the common good.
    *   Early socialists like **Robert Owen** and **Louis Blanc** proposed **cooperatives**.
    *   **Karl Marx** provided a powerful critique of **capitalism**, arguing that workers were exploited to create profit for capitalists. He believed workers must overthrow capitalism to establish a classless **communist** society where property was socially controlled.

*   **Pre-Revolutionary Russia:** Russia in the early 20th century was unique:
    *   Politically: An **autocracy** under Tsar Nicholas II, lacking parliamentary checks.
    *   Economically: Overwhelmingly agrarian, with pockets of heavy industry facing issues like foreign dependence and poor infrastructure.
    *   Socially: Deep divisions existed. Workers faced harsh conditions but were capable of collective action (strikes). Peasants desired land owned by the nobility and had a unique communal land system (**mir**). Socialist ideas spread through illegal parties like the Marxist **RSDWP** (split into **Bolsheviks** and **Mensheviks**) and the peasant-focused **Socialist Revolutionaries**.
    *   *(Conceptual Diagram Idea: A mind map showing the key characteristics of Tsarist Russia - Political, Economic, Social, and the emerging Socialist movements).*

*   **The Revolutions of 1905 and 1917:**
    *   The **1905 Revolution**, sparked by **Bloody Sunday**, forced the Tsar to concede a parliament (**Duma**), but its powers were limited, and autocratic rule largely continued.
    *   **World War I** devastated Russia, causing massive casualties, economic breakdown, and food shortages, fatally weakening the Tsarist regime.
    *   The **February Revolution (1917)** saw strikes and army mutinies in Petrograd leading to the Tsar's abdication and the formation of a **Provisional Government** alongside the powerful **Petrograd Soviet**.
    *   The **October Revolution (1917)** was a deliberate seizure of power by the **Bolsheviks**, led by **Lenin**, overthrowing the Provisional Government based on the slogans "Peace, Land, and Bread".
    *   *(Conceptual Diagram Idea: A timeline highlighting key events from 1905 (Bloody Sunday) to October 1917 (Bolshevik takeover), including WWI impact and the February Revolution).*

*   **Building the Soviet State and its Consequences:**
    *   The Bolsheviks immediately implemented radical changes: **nationalisation** of banks and industry, land redistribution (decree allowing peasants to seize land), withdrawal from WWI (**Treaty of Brest Litovsk**), and establishment of a **one-party state** suppressing opposition.
    *   The **Civil War (1918-1920)** pitted the Bolshevik 'Reds' against anti-Bolshevik 'Whites' and 'Greens', complicated by foreign intervention. Bolshevik victory led to the formation of the **USSR** in 1922.
    *   Under **Stalin**, the state pursued rapid industrialisation through **Five-Year Plans** and forced **collectivisation** of agriculture.
        *   Industrialisation achieved significant growth but at the cost of terrible working and living conditions.
        *   Collectivisation aimed to modernise farming and control grain supplies by eliminating private peasant farms (**kulaks**) and creating state farms (**kolkhoz**). It faced massive resistance, led to destruction of livestock, a catastrophic **famine (1930-33)**, and widespread repression (deportations, executions).
    *   The Soviet state provided some social benefits (expanded education, healthcare, crèches) but became highly authoritarian, marked by censorship and political **purges**.
    *   *(Conceptual Diagram Idea: A cause-and-effect chart for Collectivisation, showing reasons -> actions -> peasant resistance -> consequences (famine, repression, industrial impact)).*

*   **Global Impact:** The Russian Revolution demonstrated that a workers' state was possible, inspiring socialist and communist movements globally, including in colonial territories like India. The **Comintern** actively promoted revolution. However, the repressive nature of the Soviet regime, especially under Stalin, tarnished its image, leading to complex and varied interpretations of socialism worldwide.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

The chapter highlights several connections between events in Europe/Russia and India:

1.  **Influence of Revolutionary Ideas:** Post-French Revolution ideas of individual rights and social power were discussed in India by figures like **Raja Rammohan Roy** and **Derozio**. Italian nationalist **Giuseppe Mazzini's** writings were read by nationalists in India.
2.  **Inspiration from the Russian Revolution:** The revolution and the attempt to build a socialist state significantly inspired many Indians.
    *   Several Indians attended the **Communist University of the Workers of the East** in the USSR.
    *   The **Communist Party of India** was formed in the mid-1920s, maintaining links with the Soviet Communist Party.
    *   Prominent figures like **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Rabindranath Tagore** visited the USSR and wrote about Soviet socialism, impressed by the efforts towards literacy and equality (Source G).
    *   **Shaukat Usmani**, an Indian revolutionary, wrote about the sense of equality and freedom he perceived in Soviet Russia in 1920 (Source F).
    *   **M.N. Roy**, an Indian revolutionary and Comintern leader, was active in Central Asia during the Civil War period (Source B).
    *   Numerous writings about the revolution and Soviet Russia appeared in various Indian languages (Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu).
3.  **Contextual Link to Indian Planning:** While not explicitly stated as economic *data* in the text, the Soviet experiment with **centralised planning (Five-Year Plans)** and its focus on rapid industrialisation and social equality served as a significant inspiration or point of reference for economic planning debates and models adopted in post-independence India. The Soviet emphasis on state-led development resonated with sections of the Indian leadership aiming to overcome colonial economic backwardness and reduce inequality.
