# [Class 9] History - Chapter 2: Socialism in Europe and the Russian Revolution

## 🌟 Core Concepts

Here is a hierarchical breakdown of the core concepts discussed in this chapter:

1.  **Post-French Revolution Ideologies in Europe**
    *   Context: Desire for societal change after 1789.
    *   **Liberalism:** Advocated for individual rights, representative government, rule of law, religious tolerance. Opposed dynastic rule but often limited suffrage (e.g., to men of property).
    *   **Radicalism:** Demanded government based on majority rule, supported universal adult franchise (including women's suffragette movements), opposed privileges of landowners/factory owners, disliked wealth concentration but not necessarily private property itself.
    *   **Conservatism:** Initially opposed change, later accepted gradual change while respecting the past. Opposed Liberals and Radicals.
2.  **Industrial Society and its Challenges**
    *   Impact of Industrial Revolution: Urbanisation, factory system, expansion of railways.
    *   Worker Conditions: Long hours, poor wages, unemployment, inadequate housing and sanitation.
    *   Responses: Liberals and Radicals sought solutions, often believing in individual effort and enterprise if freedom was ensured. Nationalists aimed for 'nations' with equal rights.
3.  **The Rise of Socialism**
    *   Core Idea: Opposition to private property as the root of social ills; advocating for social/collective control of property for collective interest.
    *   **Early Socialist Visions:**
        *   Cooperatives: Robert Owen (New Harmony), Louis Blanc (government-encouraged cooperatives).
    *   **Marxism (Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels):**
        *   Analysis of Capitalism: Industrial society as 'capitalist', owners (capitalists) profiting from workers' labour.
        *   Class Struggle: Inevitable conflict between workers and capitalists.
        *   Solution: Overthrow of capitalism, establishment of a socialist/communist society with socially controlled property.
    *   **Spread and Organisation:** Second International (1870s), formation of workers' associations and parties (e.g., SPD in Germany, Labour Party in Britain, Socialist Party in France). Limited political power before 1914 but influenced legislation.
4.  **The Russian Empire Before 1917**
    *   **Political System:** Tsarist Autocracy (absolute monarchy under Tsar Nicholas II). No parliamentary accountability.
    *   **Economy:** Predominantly agrarian (85% population), major grain exporter. Industrialisation concentrated in pockets (St Petersburg, Moscow), foreign investment, railway expansion. Industry largely privately owned, poor working conditions despite some government supervision.
    *   **Society:**
        *   Peasants: Cultivated most land but nobility, crown, Church owned large estates. Deeply religious, resented nobility, desired land redistribution. Unique feature: periodic land pooling via commune (*mir*).
        *   Workers: Divided by skill (e.g., metalworkers as 'aristocrats'), village links, gender (women paid less). United for strikes over conditions. Poor living conditions (dormitories).
        *   Social Divisions: Clear separation between elites and masses.
    *   **Emergence of Russian Socialism:**
        *   Russian Social Democratic Workers Party (RSDWP, 1898 - Marxist): Operated illegally. Divided into:
            *   **Bolsheviks** (led by Lenin): Favoured disciplined party, controlled membership.
            *   **Mensheviks:** Favoured party open to all.
        *   Socialist Revolutionary Party (1900): Focused on peasant rights, land transfer from nobles. Disagreed with Social Democrats over peasants' role (Lenin saw peasants as differentiated, not united).
5.  **The 1905 Revolution**
    *   Causes: Autocracy, poor worker conditions, Russo-Japanese War impact (implied), price rise, real wage decline. Trigger: Bloody Sunday (Jan 1905) - peaceful workers' procession fired upon.
    *   Events: Nationwide strikes, university closures, formation of unions and professional associations (Union of Unions), demand for constituent assembly.
    *   Outcome: Tsar conceded creation of an elected consultative Parliament (**Duma**). Limited impact as Tsar dismissed early Dumas and restricted voting laws. Political activity remained restricted.
6.  **Impact of World War I on Russia (1914-1917)**
    *   Initial popular support waned as Tsar refused Duma consultation.
    *   Military Defeats: Heavy losses on the Eastern Front (over 7 million casualties by 1917), army demoralisation, destruction of crops/buildings during retreat leading to refugees.
    *   Economic Disruption: Industry suffered (cut off from suppliers, equipment breakdown), labour shortages, grain diverted to army, severe food shortages in cities (bread riots).
    *   Political Impact: Discredited the Tsar and government, Tsarina's German origins and Rasputin's influence fueled unpopularity. Soldiers unwilling to fight.
7.  **The February Revolution (1917)**
    *   Context: Grim conditions in Petrograd (winter, food shortages).
    *   Events:
        *   Feb 22: Factory lockout.
        *   Feb 23 (International Women's Day): Sympathy strikes led by women, demonstrations.
        *   Feb 25: Duma suspended.
        *   Feb 26-27: Demonstrations intensify, police headquarters ransacked, cavalry refuses to fire, regiments mutiny.
        *   Formation of **Petrograd Soviet** (workers' and soldiers' council).
    *   Outcome: Tsar Nicholas II abdicated (March 2). Formation of **Provisional Government** by Soviet and Duma leaders. Aim for Constituent Assembly based on universal suffrage.
8.  **Developments Between February and October 1917**
    *   Dual Power (implied): Provisional Government (influenced by landowners, industrialists, liberals, socialists) vs. Soviets (workers, soldiers).
    *   Lenin's Return (April): **April Theses** - demanded end to war, land transfer to peasants, nationalisation of banks, power to Soviets ("All Power to the Soviets!"). Renaming Bolshevik party to Communist Party.
    *   Growing Unrest: Workers' movements spread (factory committees), trade unions grew, soldiers' committees formed. Peasant land seizures (July-Sept), encouraged by Socialist Revolutionaries.
    *   Provisional Government Response: Resisted worker control, arrested Bolshevik leaders after July demonstrations. Power weakened, Bolshevik influence grew.
9.  **The October Revolution (1917)**
    *   Context: Lenin feared Provisional Government dictatorship, pushed for uprising.
    *   Organisation: Military Revolutionary Committee (under Leon Trotskii) appointed by Petrograd Soviet.
    *   Events (Oct 24): Bolshevik supporters seize government offices, arrest ministers. Winter Palace stormed (shelled by ship *Aurora*). Petrograd under Bolshevik control.
    *   Legitimation: All Russian Congress of Soviets approved Bolshevik action. Uprisings in other cities (fighting in Moscow).
    *   Outcome: Bolsheviks seized power, controlled Moscow-Petrograd area by December.
10. **Building the Socialist State: Post-October Changes & Civil War**
    *   **Immediate Bolshevik Policies:**
        *   Opposition to private property: Nationalisation of industry and banks (Nov 1917).
        *   Land declared social property; peasants allowed to seize nobility's land.
        *   Housing partitioned in cities. Aristocratic titles banned. New uniforms (e.g., *budeonovka* hat).
        *   Party renamed Russian Communist Party (Bolshevik).
        *   Constituent Assembly elections held (Nov 1917) - Bolsheviks failed to get majority, Assembly dismissed by Lenin (Jan 1918).
        *   Peace Treaty: Brest-Litovsk treaty with Germany (March 1918), ending Russia's involvement in WWI despite opposition.
        *   One-Party State: Only Bolsheviks/Communists participate in elections to All Russian Congress of Soviets (Parliament). Trade unions under party control.
        *   Repression: Secret police (**Cheka**, later OGPU/NKVD) punished critics. Censorship introduced.
    *   **The Civil War (1918-1920):**
        *   Conflict: Bolsheviks ('Reds') vs. opponents ('Greens' - Socialist Revolutionaries, 'Whites' - pro-Tsarists, supported by foreign powers like France, Britain, USA, Japan).
        *   Causes: Opposition to Bolshevik uprising, land redistribution (army breakup), foreign fear of socialism.
        *   Characteristics: Widespread fighting, looting, banditry, famine. 'White' harshness towards peasants lost them support.
        *   Outcome: Bolshevik victory by Jan 1920. Reasons: Control of key areas, peasant support (fear of land return to nobles), cooperation with some non-Russian nationalities/Muslim Jadidists (though sometimes brutal, e.g., Khiva).
    *   **Formation of USSR (1922):** Union of Soviet Socialist Republics created from former Russian empire, granting political autonomy to non-Russian nationalities (partly successful due to unpopular Bolshevik policies like discouraging nomadism).
11. **Making a Socialist Society: Planning and Stalinism**
    *   **Economic Policy:**
        *   Centralised Planning: Introduced for economic development. State Planning Commission (Gosplan - implied) set targets.
        *   **Five Year Plans:** (First two: 1927-32, 1933-38). Government fixed prices to promote industrial growth.
        *   Results: Rapid industrial growth (oil, coal, steel production increased significantly), new factory cities (e.g., Magnitogorsk).
        *   Costs: Poor working conditions due to rapid construction, harsh life for workers, limited resources impacting social improvements.
    *   **Social Changes:** Extended schooling, university access for workers/peasants, factory crèches, cheap healthcare, model living quarters (uneven implementation).
    *   **Stalinism and Collectivisation:**
        *   Context: Grain supply crisis (1927-28). Peasants unwilling to sell at fixed prices. Stalin blamed **kulaks** (well-to-do peasants) and speculation.
        *   Policy: Forced **Collectivisation** (from 1929). Aim to create large, state-controlled farms (*kolkhoz*) using modern machinery, eliminate kulaks, take land from individual peasants.
        *   Implementation: Party members enforced grain collections, raided kulaks. Peasants forced onto collective farms; land/implements transferred to kolkhoz ownership, profits shared.
        *   Resistance: Peasants destroyed livestock (cattle fell by 1/3 between 1929-31), resisted authorities. Punishments: Severe punishment, deportation, exile.
        *   Consequences: Production did not increase immediately. Devastating famine (1930-33), over 4 million deaths. Independent cultivators treated unsympathetically.
        *   Political Purges: Criticism of collectivisation/planning led to accusations of conspiracy against socialism. By 1939, over 2 million in prisons/labour camps, many executed after forced confessions (including talented professionals).
12. **Global Influence of the Russian Revolution and the USSR**
    *   Inspiration: Fired imagination of workers and colonial peoples worldwide. Possibility of a 'workers' state'.
    *   Spread of Communism: Formation of Communist Parties in many countries (e.g., Great Britain, India).
    *   **Comintern (Communist International):** Bolshevik-founded international union of pro-Bolshevik socialist parties, encouraged colonial peoples.
    *   Education: USSR's Communist University of the Workers of the East trained non-Russians.
    *   Global Stature: USSR gave socialism a global face by WWII. Acknowledged as a great power with developed industry/agriculture, feeding the poor.
    *   Criticism & Decline: By 1950s, acknowledged internally and externally that USSR's repressive style of government (denial of freedoms, purges) contradicted revolutionary ideals. International reputation declined by late 20th century, but socialist ideals retained respect. Led to rethinking of socialism globally.

## 📘 Key Learnings

This chapter explains the rise of socialist ideas in Europe against the backdrop of the Industrial Revolution and the limitations of liberal democracy, culminating in the Russian Revolution, a pivotal event that attempted to build the world's first socialist state.

1.  **Diverse Political Responses to Change:** The French Revolution sparked debates across Europe.
    *   **Liberals:** Wanted constitutional government, individual rights, religious tolerance, but often restricted voting rights (mainly to property-owning men).
    *   **Radicals:** Advocated for majority rule, universal suffrage (including women), and opposed privileges, disliking wealth concentration.
    *   **Conservatives:** Initially resisted change, later accepted gradual transformation while valuing tradition.
    *   *Conceptual Diagram:* A table comparing these three groups on key issues like Suffrage, Private Property, Role of Monarchy/State, Pace of Change.

2.  **Socialism as a Critique of Industrial Capitalism:**
    *   Industrialisation created wealth but also immense hardship for workers (long hours, low pay, poor conditions).
    *   Socialists identified **private property** as the root cause, arguing it led to exploitation for personal gain rather than collective welfare.
    *   Early socialists proposed **cooperatives** (Owen, Blanc) as alternatives.
    *   **Marxism** provided a powerful critique, viewing history as a class struggle and predicting workers would overthrow capitalism to establish a **communist society** with social ownership of means of production.

3.  **Pre-Revolutionary Russia: An Empire Ripe for Change:**
    *   **Autocratic Rule:** Tsar Nicholas II held absolute power, unresponsive to popular demands.
    *   **Economic Backwardness:** Primarily agricultural, with pockets of heavy industry facing issues like poor working conditions and foreign dependence.
    *   **Social Tensions:** Vast gap between nobility/clergy and peasants/workers. Peasants desired land; workers faced exploitation. The *mir* system made Russian peasants distinct.
    *   **Rise of Opposition:** Illegal political parties emerged: Marxists (SDWP - Bolsheviks/Mensheviks) focused on workers; Socialist Revolutionaries focused on peasants.

4.  **The Path to Revolution (1905-1917):**
    *   **1905 Revolution:** Triggered by 'Bloody Sunday', forced the Tsar to create the Duma, but its powers were limited and autocracy largely remained. Showed potential for mass action.
    *   **WWI's Devastating Impact:** Military defeats, economic collapse, food shortages, and massive casualties delegitimized the Tsarist regime completely.
    *   *Conceptual Diagram:* A timeline highlighting key events: 1898 (SDWP) -> 1905 (Bloody Sunday, Duma) -> 1914 (WWI begins) -> Feb 1917 (Tsar Abdicates) -> Apr 1917 (Lenin's April Theses) -> Oct 1917 (Bolshevik Revolution) -> 1918-20 (Civil War) -> 1922 (USSR Formed) -> 1929 (Collectivisation).

5.  **The Twin Revolutions of 1917:**
    *   **February Revolution:** Spontaneous uprising (strikes, demonstrations, army mutiny) in Petrograd over war weariness and food shortages, leading to the Tsar's abdication and establishment of a Provisional Government.
    *   **October Revolution:** Planned seizure of power by the Bolsheviks, led by Lenin and Trotskii, overthrowing the Provisional Government based on the promise of "Peace, Land, and Bread" and power to the Soviets.
    *   *Conceptual Diagram:* Flow charts detailing the triggers, key actions, and immediate outcomes for both the February and October Revolutions.

6.  **Consolidating Power and Building Socialism:**
    *   **Bolshevik Actions:** Immediate decrees on peace (Brest-Litovsk) and land (nationalisation, peasant seizure). Nationalisation of banks/industry. Establishment of a one-party state, use of secret police (Cheka), and censorship.
    *   **Civil War:** Bolsheviks ('Reds') fought against diverse opposition ('Whites' and 'Greens'), backed by foreign powers. Bolshevik victory secured their control over most of the former empire.
    *   **USSR Formation (1922):** An attempt to manage national diversity within a socialist framework.

7.  **Stalinism: Industrialisation at a High Human Cost:**
    *   **Centralised Planning (Five Year Plans):** Achieved rapid industrialisation but involved harsh working conditions and suppression of dissent.
    *   **Forced Collectivisation:** Aimed to modernise agriculture and control grain supply by forcing peasants onto state farms (*kolkhoz*). Led to massive resistance, destruction of livestock, a devastating famine (1930-33), and elimination of 'kulaks'.
    *   **Purges:** Widespread arrests, executions, and imprisonment in labour camps targeting perceived opponents, including party members and professionals.

8.  **Global Legacy:**
    *   The Russian Revolution inspired socialist and communist movements worldwide, particularly in colonial countries fighting for independence.
    *   The USSR emerged as a major world power, demonstrating an alternative model of development.
    *   However, its repressive methods and denial of freedoms led to disillusionment and a rethinking of socialist ideals globally by the late 20th century.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context

The ideas of socialism and the events of the Russian Revolution had a noticeable impact on India, particularly within the context of its own anti-colonial struggle and search for socio-economic transformation.

1.  **Early Awareness:** Even before the Russian Revolution, ideas from post-revolutionary Europe circulated in India. Figures like **Raja Rammohan Roy** and **Henry Derozio** discussed the significance of the French Revolution, indicating an engagement with European political thought. Giuseppe Mazzini's nationalist ideas also influenced Indian thinkers.
2.  **Inspiration from the Revolution:** The overthrow of the Tsar and the establishment of what was perceived as a 'workers' state' inspired many Indians fighting against British imperialism.
    *   Several Indians attended the **Communist University of the Workers of the East** in the USSR.
    *   The **Communist Party of India (CPI)** was formed in the mid-1920s, maintaining links with the Soviet Communist Party and the **Comintern**.
3.  **Impressions of Soviet Russia:**
    *   Important political and cultural figures like **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Rabindranath Tagore** visited the Soviet Union and wrote about their experiences, often highlighting perceived progress in literacy, social equality, and the upliftment of the masses (See Source G: Tagore's account).
    *   Indian writers published numerous works in Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Malayalam, Tamil, and Telugu about the revolution, Lenin, and the Soviet state (e.g., R.S. Avasthi, S.D. Vidyalankar).
    *   Accounts like **Shaukat Usmani's** (Source F) emphasized the sense of equality and freedom observed in Soviet Russia in the early 1920s, particularly the mingling of Europeans and Asians without barriers of caste or religion.
4.  **Complex Interactions:** Figures like **M.N. Roy**, an Indian revolutionary active in international communist circles (founder of Mexican CP, Comintern leader), provide insights into the complexities on the ground, such as the varied responses to the revolution in Central Asia (Source B).
