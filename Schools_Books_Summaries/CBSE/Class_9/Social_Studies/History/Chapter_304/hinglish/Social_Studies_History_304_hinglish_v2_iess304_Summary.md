# [Class 9] History - Chapter 304: Forest Society and Colonialism (Van Samaj aur Upniveshvad)

## 🌟 Core Concepts (Mukhya Avdharnayein) 📊

Yeh chapter explore karta hai ki colonial rule (British shasan) ke time India aur Indonesia (Java) mein forests (vanon) aur wahan rehne wale logon (forest dwellers) ki life kaise badli.

1.  **Forests ka Mahatva (Importance of Forests):**
    *   Rozmarra ki cheezein (Everyday items): Furniture, paper, dyes, spices, medicines, fuel, fodder.
    *   Biodiversity ka khazana (Rich biodiversity): Ek chhote patch mein bhi 500 alag plant species ho sakti hain (e.g., Amazon, Western Ghats).
    *   Local communities ki lifeline: Fuel, fodder, food (fruits, tubers), building material, traditional practices.

2.  **Deforestation ke Kaaran (Causes of Deforestation):**
    *   **Pre-colonial:** Population badhne se cultivation ka expansion.
    *   **Colonial Era (Main Focus):**
        *   **Land Improvement ka Idea:** British sochte the ki forests 'unproductive' hain aur cultivation se revenue badhega. Commercial crops (jute, sugar, wheat, cotton) ko badhawa diya. (1880-1920 mein cultivated area 6.7 million hectares badha).
        *   **Railways ki Zaroorat (Need for Railways):**
            *   Sleepers (lakdi ke fatte) tracks ke liye (1 mile = 1760-2000 sleepers).
            *   Locomotives ke liye fuel (indhan).
            *   Colonial trade aur army movement ke liye essential.
        *   **Jahaz Banane ki Zaroorat (Need for Shipbuilding):** England mein oak forests khatam hone lage toh Royal Navy ke liye India se strong timber (lakdi) export ki gayi.
        *   **Plantations (Bagan):** Natural forests ko saaf karke Tea, Coffee, Rubber ke plantations lagaye gaye European market ke liye.

3.  **Commercial Forestry ka Uday (Rise of Commercial Forestry):**
    *   **British Chinta:** Local use aur traders dwara 'reckless felling' se forests barbaad ho jayenge.
    *   **Dietrich Brandis:** German expert, First Inspector General of Forests in India.
    *   **System ki Shuruat:**
        *   Indian Forest Service (1864).
        *   Indian Forest Act (1865, amended 1878, 1927).
        *   Imperial Forest Research Institute (Dehradun, 1906).
    *   **'Scientific Forestry':**
        *   Natural mixed forests ko kaat kar ek hi type ke valuable trees (Teak, Sal) ko straight rows mein lagana (Plantation).
        *   Forests ka survey, mapping, working plans banana.
        *   Cutting aur replanting ka cycle.
        *   *Criticism:* Ecologists ise scientific nahi maante kyunki yeh biodiversity khatam karta hai aur local needs poori nahi karta.
    *   **Forest Act (1878) ke Categories:**
        *   **Reserved Forests (Aarakshit Van):** Best forests. Villagers kuch nahi le sakte the.
        *   **Protected Forests (Surakshit Van):** Kuch rights diye gaye, lekin strict rules the.
        *   **Village Forests (Gram Van):** Local use ke liye, lekin government control mein.

4.  **Logon ki Zindagi par Asar (Impact on People's Lives):**
    *   **Loss of Traditional Rights:** Fuelwood collection, grazing, hunting, fishing, collecting fruits/roots sab illegal ho gaya.
    *   **Hardship & Conflict:** Logon ko lakdi churani padti thi, forest guards se pareshani, bribes. Mahilayein (women) fuelwood ke liye pareshan.
    *   **Shifting Cultivation (Jhum Kheti) par Rok:**
        *   Local names: *dhya, penda, bewar, nevad, jhum, podu* (India); *lading* (SE Asia), *milpa* (Central America), *chena* (Sri Lanka).
        *   Colonial view: Harmful for timber, difficult to tax.
        *   Impact: Ban laga diya gaya, communities ko displace kiya gaya, kuch ne vidroh (rebellion) kiya.
    *   **Hunting (Shikar) par Asar:**
        *   Traditional hunting (survival ke liye) ko poaching (avaidh shikar) bana diya gaya.
        *   British officials aur Rajas ke liye Big game hunting ek sport ban gaya. Bade paimane par tigers, leopards, wolves maare gaye (1875-1925 mein 80,000+ tigers).
    *   **Naye Vyapar aur Rozgar (New Trades & Employments):**
        *   Kuch communities ne traditional kaam chhod kar forest products ka trade shuru kiya (e.g., Mundurucu in Brazil - rubber).
        *   Trade par government ka control, European firms ko monopoly mili.
        *   Nomadic communities (Korava, Karacha, Yerukula) ki livelihood chini gayi, 'Criminal Tribes' declare kiya gaya.
        *   Plantations (Assam) mein forest communities (Santhals, Gonds, Oraons) se low wage par kaam karaya gaya, conditions kharab thi.

5.  **Van Vidroh (Rebellion in the Forest):**
    *   Colonial policies ke khilaf protests. Leaders: Siddhu & Kanu (Santhal Parganas), Birsa Munda (Chhotanagpur), Alluri Sitarama Raju (Andhra Pradesh).
    *   **Case Study: Bastar Rebellion (1910):**
        *   Location: Chhattisgarh (borders AP, Orissa, MH). Communities: Maria, Muria Gonds, Dhurwas, Bhatras, Halbas.
        *   Causes: Forest reservation (1905 - two-thirds), stopping shifting cultivation/hunting/collection, increased rent, free labour demands (begar), famines (1899-1900, 1907-08).
        *   Initiative: Dhurwas of Kanger forest. Leader figure: Gunda Dhur (Nethanar village).
        *   Methods: Messages (mango boughs, earth, chillies, arrows), bazaars lootna, officials ke ghar/schools/police stations jalana.
        *   Suppression: British ne sena bheji, firing ki, gaon khali ho gaye. Gunda Dhur pakde nahi gaye.
        *   Outcome: Reservation temporarily ruka, area kam kiya gaya. Lekin exploitation jaari raha.

6.  **Java mein Van Parivartan (Forest Transformations in Java):**
    *   Colonial Power: Dutch.
    *   Similarities with India: Timber for ships, Forest laws, Scientific forestry, impact on locals.
    *   **Kalangs:** Skilled forest cutters. Dutch ne unpar control karne ki koshish ki, Kalangs ne 1770 mein vidroh kiya (suppressed).
    *   **Dutch Scientific Forestry:** Villagers ka access restrict kiya gaya. Specific purposes ke liye hi wood cutting allowed thi.
    *   **Blandongdiensten System:** Free labour aur buffaloes provide karne ke badle kuch villages ko rent se exemption. Baad mein kam wages diye gaye.
    *   **Samin's Challenge (c. 1890):** Surontiko Samin ne state ownership par sawal uthaya ("Hawa, paani, zameen, lakdi state ne nahi banayi"). Movement faila, logon ne tax/fine dene se mana kiya, labour nahi kiya.
    *   **War aur Deforestation:** World Wars mein forests ka heavy exploitation hua (British war needs in India, Japanese exploitation in Java). Dutch ne 'scorched earth' policy apnai.

7.  **Forestry mein Naye Vikas (New Developments in Forestry):**
    *   **Post-1980s Shift:** Governments ko realise hua ki communities ko door rakhne se conflicts badhe hain.
    *   **Conservation par Focus:** Timber collection se zyada conservation important hua.
    *   **Community Involvement:** Realisation ki forest ke paas rehne wale logon ko involve karna zaroori hai.
    *   **Sacred Groves (Pavitra Upvan):** India mein kai jagah (Mizoram to Kerala) dense forests bache hain kyunki villages ne unhe *sarnas, devarakudu, kan, rai* jaise sacred groves maan kar protect kiya.
    *   **Community Patrolling:** Kuch villages khud apne forests ki patrolling kar rahe hain.

## 📘 Key Learnings (Mukhya Seekh) 📈

1.  **Colonialism ne Forests ko Resource ki tarah dekha:** British aur Dutch ne forests ko sirf timber, revenue aur commercial products ka source maana. Unhone local communities ki needs aur traditional rights ko ignore kiya. *Diagram Idea: Flowchart showing colonial needs (Ships, Railways, Revenue) -> Deforestation & New Forest Laws.*

2.  **'Scientific Forestry' Local Needs ke Khilaf thi:** Yeh system sirf commercial timber (Teak, Sal) par focused tha. Isne mixed forests ko khatam kiya jo fuel, fodder, food, aur dusri zarooraton ko poora karte the. Villagers ko apne hi forests se bahar kar diya gaya. *Diagram Idea: Comparison table - Villagers' Forest Needs vs. Colonial Forest Needs.*

3.  **Forest Laws ne Logon ki Life Mushkil Bana di:** Roz ki activities jaise lakdi ikattha karna, pashu charana, shikar karna illegal ho gaya. Isse logon ko chori karne par majboor hona pada aur Forest Guards dwara exploitation badha. Shifting cultivators aur nomadic communities ki livelihood khatam ho gayi.

4.  **Resistance ek Natural Response tha:** Jab traditional rights छीने gaye aur exploitation badha, toh Bastar aur Java jaise jagahon par logon ne vidroh kiya. Yeh dikhata hai ki forest communities passive victims nahi the, unhone changes ko shape karne ki koshish ki. *Case Study Focus: Bastar - Causes, Events, Outcome.*

5.  **Colonial Forest Policies ke Long-Term Impacts hain:** Aaj bhi forest conservation aur tribal rights ke beech conflict dikhta hai. Biodiversity loss aur communities ka displacement colonial era ki policies ka natija hai. *Modern Connection: Forest Rights Act 2006 in India.*

6.  **India aur Java mein Similarities:** Dono jagah European powers (British/Dutch) ne similar reasons se forests ko control kiya, similar laws (scientific forestry, restrictions) lagaye, aur local logon par similar negative impacts hue. Isse colonial patterns samajhne mein madad milti hai.

## 🌏 Bharatiya Context (Indian Context)

*   **National Economic/Social Data & Examples 📊:**
    *   **Deforestation Data:** 1880-1920 ke beech India mein forest cover 9.7 million hectares kam hua (108.6 se 98.9 million hectares).
    *   **Railway Sleepers:** Madras Presidency mein 1850s mein har saal 35,000 ped sleepers ke liye kaate ja rahe the. Ek mile track ke liye 1760-2000 sleepers ki zaroorat thi.
    *   **Hunting Impact:** 1875-1925 mein rewards ke liye 80,000+ tigers, 150,000 leopards, 200,000 wolves maare gaye. Maharaja of Sarguja ne akele 1,157 tigers maare.
    *   **Specific Communities:** Baigas (Central India - petition against stopping shifting cultivation), Gonds, Santhals, Oraons (recruited for Assam tea plantations), Korava, Karacha, Yerukula (Madras Presidency - lost livelihood, called 'criminal tribes').
    *   **Regions:** Bastar (Chhattisgarh), Chhotanagpur (Jharkhand), Santhal Parganas, Singhbhum forests.
    *   **Forest Products & Trees:** Sal, Teak (commercial timber), Mahua (flowers for food/alcohol, seeds for oil), Tendu leaves (for bidis), Bamboo (fences, baskets), Semur (silk-cotton tree).
    *   **Traditional Practices:** Shifting cultivation (*Jhum, Penda, Bewar* etc.), *Devsari/Dand/Man* (fee paid to use another village's forest).
    *   **Rebellions:** Santhal Rebellion (Siddhu-Kanu), Munda Rebellion (Birsa Munda), Rampa Rebellion (Alluri Sitarama Raju), Bastar Rebellion (Gunda Dhur).
    *   **Modern Conservation:** Sacred Groves (*Sarnas, Devarakudu, Kan, Rai*), Community forest management initiatives. Forest Rights Act, 2006 ka relevance aaj bhi hai jo historical injustice ko address karne ki koshish karta hai.
